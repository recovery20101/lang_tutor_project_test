# Chapter 2: Nouns

To use nouns correctly in Spanish, you need to know their grammatical **gender** (usually arbitrary, unrelated to biological gender) and their **plural form**.

**Three main noun groups:**
- Nouns ending in **-o** (*libro*, *vino*) — almost always **masculine**
- Nouns ending in **-a** (*casa*, *patata*) — mostly **feminine**
- Nouns ending in **-e** or a consonant (*nube*, *tacón*) — either gender

## **2.1 Plural forms of nouns**

### **2.1.1 Plural of nouns ending in an unstressed vowel**

**Rule:** Add **-s** to the singular form.

**Examples:**

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| la casa | las casas | house/s |
| la tribu | las tribus | tribe/s |

### **2.1.2 Plural of nouns ending in a consonant**

**Rule:** Usually add **-es**. This includes words of one syllable and words with a final *stressed* syllable ending in **-s**.

**Examples:**

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| el olor | los olores | smell/s |
| el farol | los faroles | streetlight/s |
| el mes | los meses | month/s |
| el autobús | los autobuses | bus/buses |

### **2.1.3 Plural of nouns ending in a stressed vowel**

**Rule:** Usually add **-s**, except when the singular ends in **-í**: most words in **-í** add **-es**, though some add only **-s**. Some words in **-ú** have plural in **-úes**.

**Examples:**

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| el sofá | los sofás | sofa/s |
| el pie | los pies | foot/feet |
| el canapé | los canapés | sofa/s |
| el dominó | los dominós | domino/es |
| el menú | los menús | menu/s |
| el israelí | los israelíes | Israeli/s |
| el ají [LA] | los ajíes | chili pepper/s |
| el esquí | los esquís | ski/s |
| el tabú | los tabúes | taboo/s |
| el bambú | los bambúes | bamboo/s |

### **2.1.4 Nouns which do not adopt a distinct plural form**

#### 2.1.4.1 Nouns ending in unstressed -s or -x

**Rule:** Nouns with a final *unstressed* syllable ending in **-s**, and those ending in **-x**, do not change in the plural.

**Spelling changes when forming plurals:**
1. **-z** → **-ces** before **-es**: *voz* → *voces*
2. **-n/-s** with accent on final syllable → accent drops in plural: *avión* → *aviones*, *inglés* → *ingleses*
3. Combined vowels with **í** or **ú** retain the accent: *raíz* → *raíces*, *baúl* → *baúles*
4. **-en** endings require an accent in the plural to preserve stress: *imagen* → *imágenes*, *resumen* → *resúmenes*
5. Irregular stress shift: *carácter* → *caracteres*, *régimen* → *regímenes*, *espécimen* → *especímenes*

**Examples:**

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| la crisis | las crisis | crisis/crises |
| el virus | los virus | virus/viruses |
| el fax | los fax | fax/faxes |
| la voz | las voces | voice/s |
| el avión | los aviones | plane/s |
| el inglés | los ingleses | Englishman/people |
| la imagen | las imágenes | image/s |
| el carácter | los caracteres | character/s |
| el régimen | los regímenes | regime/s |

### **2.1.5 Plural of compound nouns**

**Rule:** Three patterns depending on compound type:
1. Verb/preposition + plural noun → no distinct plural form (*el/los salvapantallas*)
2. Single-word compound not ending in plural → normal plural added (*los mirasoles*)
3. Two separate nouns → usually only the **first** takes a plural; but if the second qualifies the first, **both** are pluralised

**Examples:**

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| el salvapantallas | los salvapantallas | screensaver/s |
| el paracaídas | los paracaídas | parachute/s |
| el mirasol | los mirasoles | sunflower/s |
| el barco vivienda | los barcos vivienda | houseboat/s |
| el país miembro | los países miembros | member country/ies |
| el documento maestro | los documentos maestros | master document/s |

*Note: There is disagreement over **clave**: **puntos clave** or **puntos claves** 'key points'.*

### **2.1.6 Plural of foreign words**

**Rule:** The general trend is to add **-s** only, even for consonant endings. Some words of French origin drop final **-t**. Some foreign words have become fully integrated and follow the **-es** rule; in other cases two forms compete.

Latin words ending in **-t** or **-um** may appear with or without **-s**; for **-um** words, replacing with **-o** and treating as Spanish is also recommended.

**Examples:**

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| el córner | los córners | corner (football) |
| el barman | los barmans | barman/barmen |
| el cabaret/cabaré | los cabarets/cabarés | cabaret/s |
| el hotel | los hoteles | hotel/s |
| el gol | los goles | goal/s |
| el escáner | los escáneres | scanner/s |
| el club | los clubs / clubes | club/s |
| el déficit | los déficit/s | deficit/s |
| el currículum | los currículum/s | curriculum/a |
| el currículo | los currículos | curriculum/a |

### **2.1.7 Plural of proper nouns**

**Rule:** When used with the plural definite article to designate a group collectively, proper nouns generally **do not** take a plural form (unless the name itself begins with *los/las*). In other non-collective uses, plural forms are used — except for names ending in **-z** or a stressed final **-s**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Los Uribe de Colombia se encuentran en todo el mundo. | The Uribes of Colombia are found throughout the world. |
| Viven Velascos en todo el Perú. | Velascos live all over Peru. |
| Hay pocos Solís y Suárez en Suecia. | There are few Solíses and Suárezes in Sweden. |

### **2.1.8 Nouns which are always plural**

**Rule:** Some common nouns exist only in the plural form.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| las afueras | outskirts |
| los alrededores | surroundings |
| los auriculares / cascos | headphones |
| los celos | jealousy |
| los comestibles | foodstuffs |
| los espaguetis | spaghetti |
| las gafas / los anteojos [LA] | spectacles |
| las tijeras | scissors |
| las tinieblas | darkness |
| las vacaciones | holidays |
| los víveres | provisions |

*Nouns used in both singular and plural: **el/los bigote/s**, **el/los pantalón/pantalones**, **la/las nariz/narices**, **la/las escalera/s**.*

*Note: **El celo** means 'zeal'; **la escalera** is also 'a ladder'.*

### **2.1.9 Mass (uncountable) nouns**

**Rule:** Spanish tends to use certain nouns both as mass/uncountable nouns in the singular and as plural countable nouns — where English would not use the plural form. Conversely, some English mass nouns correspond to countable nouns in Spanish.

**Examples:**

| Singular (mass) | Plural (countable) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| el jabón | los jabones | soap / bars of soap |
| la tostada | las tostadas | toast / slices of toast |
| la amistad | unas amistades | friendship / some friends |
| los muebles | — | the furniture (countable in Spanish) |

### **2.2.1 Nouns referring to persons or animals**

#### 2.2.1.1 Masculine -o → feminine -a

**Rule:** If the masculine noun ends in **-o**, the feminine is usually formed by changing **-o** to **-a**.

**Examples:**

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| el abuelo | la abuela | grandfather / grandmother |
| el zorro | la zorra | fox / vixen |

#### 2.2.1.5 Nouns that can be masculine or feminine

**Rule:** Some nouns use the same form for both genders — the article alone makes the distinction. Common with nouns ending in **-a** (especially **-ista**), **-e**, and **-nte**. Also applies to some nouns ending in a consonant and a few in **-o**.

**Examples:**

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| el futbolista | la futbolista | footballer |
| el intérprete | la intérprete | interpreter |
| el espía | la espía | spy |
| el cantante | la cantante | singer |
| el joven | la joven | young man / woman |
| el piloto | la piloto | pilot |

*Exception: **el monje / la monja** 'monk/nun'.*

#### 2.2.1.6 Fixed gender nouns

**Rule:** Some nouns have a fixed gender regardless of the biological gender of the person or animal referred to. For wild animals, the distinction is made by adding **macho** 'male' or **hembra** 'female'.

**Examples:**

| Noun | Gender | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| la persona | feminine | person (any gender) |
| la víctima | feminine | victim |
| la estrella | feminine | (film) star |
| el personaje | masculine | character |
| el genio | masculine | genius |
| el panda | masculine | panda |
| la víbora | feminine | adder |
| las garzas macho | — | male herons |
| el macho del tejón | — | male badger |

### **2.2.2 Determining gender from noun endings**

#### 2.2.2.1 Nouns ending in -o and -a

**Rule:** **-o** endings are usually **masculine**, **-a** endings are usually **feminine**. Exceptions:
- (a) Words ending in **-ista** — see 2.2.1.5
- (b) **La mano**, and abbreviated feminines: **la foto**, **la moto**, **la radio** [SP]
- (c) **El día**, **el mapa**, **el planeta**, **el tranvía** — masculine despite **-a**
- (d) Nouns ending in **-a** referring to men: **el cura**, **el poeta**
- (e) A large number of **-ma** words are masculine — see Table 2.1 below

| el aroma | aroma | el idioma | language |

| el diagrama | diagram | el pijama [SP] | pyjamas |
| el dilema | dilemma | el poema | poem |
| el diploma | diploma | el problema | problem |
| el dogma | dogma | el programa | programme |
| el drama | drama | el síntoma | symptom |

#### 2.2.2.2 Feminine endings other than -a

**Rule:** Nouns with the following endings are usually **feminine**: **-ad**, **-tud**, **-ción**, **-sión**, **-umbre**, **-ie**. Many are abstract nouns.

Exceptions:
- **El pie** 'foot'
- Words ending in **-triz**: **la matriz**, **la cicatriz**
- Words in **-is**: mostly feminine (*la crisis*, *la tesis*), but some are masculine: **el énfasis**, **el análisis**

**Examples:**

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| la caridad | charity |
| la solicitud | application |
| la acusación | accusation |
| la decisión | decision |
| la cumbre | summit |
| la especie | species |

| Spanish | Meaning |

| el tatuaje | tattooing |
| el fiambre | cold meat |
| el gabán | overcoat |
| el almacén | warehouse |
| el interruptor | switch |
| el pirulí | lollipop |

### **2.2.5 Names of countries, regions and towns**

**Rule:** Place names ending in *unstressed* **-a** are **feminine**; the rest are **masculine**. Some place names include the article as an integral part of the name.

| Place | Gender | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bolivia | feminine | ends in unstressed -a |
| Andalucía | feminine | ends in unstressed -a |
| (el) Ecuador | masculine | ends in -or |
| Panamá | masculine | ends in stressed -á |
| el Sanlúcar moderno | masculine | modern usage |
| la Huelva histórica | feminine | historic usage |

### **2.2.6 Nouns of dual gender with different meanings**

**Rule:** Some nouns change their **meaning** according to the gender used.

**Examples:**

| Masculine | Meaning | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| el capital | capital (money) | la capital | capital (city) |
| el cólera | cholera | la cólera | anger |
| el cometa | comet | la cometa | kite |
| el corte | cut | la corte | (royal) court |
| el frente | front | la frente | forehead |
| el margen | margin | la margen | (river) bank |
| el orden | order (arrangement) | la orden | command / religious order |
| el Papa | the Pope | la papa [LA] | potato |
| el parte | dispatch, report | la parte | part, portion |
| el pendiente | earring | la pendiente | slope |
| el pez | fish (alive in water) | la pez | pitch, tar |

**Nouns where gender shifts refer to individual vs group:**

| Feminine (group/place/book) | Masculine (individual) |
|---|---|
| la guardia (the guard, female guard) | el guardia (male guard) |
| la policía (the police force, policewoman) | el policía (policeman) |
| la guía (guidebook, female guide) | el guía (male guide) |
| la vigía (lookout post, female lookout) | el vigía (watchman) |

**Related noun pairs with distinct forms:**

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning shift |
|---|---|---|
| el banco | la banca | bank / banking system |
| el fruto | la fruta | product/result / edible fruit |
| el bolso | la bolsa | handbag / plastic bag |

### **2.2.7 Nouns of doubtful gender**

**Rule:** A small group of nouns has variable or contested gender.

- **Mar** 'sea': mostly masculine in general use. Those whose lives depend on the sea use **la mar**. Always feminine in: **la pleamar/bajamar**, **en alta mar**, **hacerse a la mar**.
- **Azúcar** 'sugar': widely used as masculine but commonly appears with feminine adjectives: *azúcar blanca*, *extrafina*, *granulada*.
- **Arte** 'art': usually masculine in the singular; always feminine in the plural: **las bellas artes**.

*For regional variation of gender, see 30.6.2.*

## **2.3 Collective nouns and agreement**

**Rule:** Collective nouns are singular but refer to a group (*multitud*, *mayoría*, *gente*, *docena*, *mitad*). When used alone, a verb in close proximity is usually **singular** (though it may be plural in English). When joined to a plural noun by **de**, or when the verb is distant from the noun, a **plural** verb is safest for learners.

*Note: Native speaker usage is not always clear-cut — collective (singular) or individual-parts (plural) thinking governs the choice.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| La mayoría no protestó. | The majority did not protest. | singular |
| El gobierno no ha decidido. | The government has/have not decided. | singular |
| La mayoría de las casas son viejas. | The majority of the houses are old. | plural (with de + plural noun) |
| La gente se calló un momento…, luego siguieron charlando. | People stopped for a moment…, then they continued chattering. | singular then plural |
| más de la mitad son refugiados | more than half are refugees | plural |
