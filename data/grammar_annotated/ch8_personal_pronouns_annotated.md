# Chapter 8: Personal pronouns

Personal pronouns refer to participants in a conversation ('I/me', 'you', 'we/us') or stand in place of nouns. Despite the name, 3rd person pronouns can refer to animals, things or neuter concepts.

Spanish pronouns exist in four roles: **subject** ('she', 'we'), **direct object** ('her', 'us'), **indirect object** ('to her', 'to us'), and **after prepositions** ('behind her'). Reflexive pronouns cover 'herself', 'ourselves' etc.

**Key notes on forms:**
- Written accents distinguish **mí** (me), **tú** (you), **él** (he) from **mi** (my), **tu** (your), **el** (the). No accent on **ti** — no identical word exists.
- **Weak pronouns** (direct/indirect object) can only be used with a verb. **Strong pronouns** (subject and prepositional) can be used independently: *La he visto* (weak) vs. *Con ella* (strong, never ~~Con la~~).

**Table 8.1 — Personal pronoun forms:**

| | Subject | Direct object | Indirect object | Prepositional object |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st sg.** | yo | me | me | mí |
| **2nd sg. familiar** | tú | te | te | ti |
| **2nd sg. polite** | usted | lo (m.) / la (f.) | le | usted |
| **2nd sg. familiar [LA]** | vos | te | te | vos |
| **3rd sg. masc.** | él | lo | le | él |
| **3rd sg. fem.** | ella | la | le | ella |
| **3rd sg. neuter** | ello | lo | le | ello |
| **1st pl. masc.** | nosotros | nos | nos | nosotros |
| **1st pl. fem.** | nosotras | nos | nos | nosotras |
| **2nd pl. masc. familiar [SP]** | vosotros | os | os | vosotros |
| **2nd pl. fem. familiar [SP]** | vosotras | os | os | vosotras |
| **2nd pl. polite [SP and LA] / familiar [LA]** | ustedes | los (m.) / las (f.) | les | ustedes |
| **3rd pl. masc.** | ellos | los | les | ellos |
| **3rd pl. fem.** | ellas | las | les | ellas |

*Note: In central and northern Spain, **le** rather than **lo** is preferred as the 3rd person singular direct object pronoun for males. See 8.6.*

## **8.1 Subject pronouns**

### **8.1.1 Subject pronouns of the 2nd person**

**Rule:** Spanish has multiple 2nd person forms depending on register and region.

| Form | Use | Region |
|---|---|---|
| **tú** | familiar singular | Spain + Latin America |
| **usted** | polite singular | Spain + Latin America |
| **vos** | familiar singular (replaces tú) | River Plate, Central America, parts of Andes |
| **vosotros/as** | familiar plural | Spain only |
| **ustedes** | polite plural (Spain) / polite and familiar plural (Latin America, Canary Islands, west Andalusia) | Spain + Latin America |

**Key grammar rule:** **Usted** and **ustedes** always take **3rd person verb forms**. For **vos** verb forms, see Chapter 10 and 30.2.1.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ¿Vosotros estáis de acuerdo? | Are you in agreement? | [SP] familiar plural |
| ¿No podés hablar vos con Carlos? | Can't you talk to Carlos? | [LA] vos familiar |
| Pasen ustedes primero. | Please go in first. | polite or familiar [LA]; polite [SP] |

*Foreign learners are advised to use polite forms until familiar with regional practice.*

### **8.1.2 Uses of subject pronouns**

#### 8.1.2.1 General uses — emphasis, contrast, clarity

**Rule:** Subject pronouns in Spanish are used for **emphasis**, to **indicate a change in reference**, or for **clarity** (e.g. where verb forms are not person-specific: *yo/él quería*). In all other cases they are **omitted** — Spanish verb endings are usually person-specific and indicate the subject alone. Native English speakers should take care **not to overuse** subject pronouns.

An appropriate form of **mismo** can be added for extra emphasis.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tú y yo somos muy parecidos. | You and I are very much alike. |
| Tú mismo lo firmaste. | You yourself signed it. |
| Yo puedo viajar hoy, pero él no podrá ir hasta mañana. | I can travel today, but he won't be able to go until tomorrow. |
| corremos | we run (pronoun omitted — ending is sufficient) |
| — ¿Quién abrió la puerta? — Yo. | 'Who opened the door?' 'Me.' (pronoun used alone without verb) |

#### 8.1.2.2 Subject pronouns with certain prepositions

**Rule:** After **entre**, **excepto/salvo/menos**, **hasta/incluso**, **según**, and **como**, the **subject pronouns** *tú* and *yo* are used — not the prepositional forms *mí* and *ti*.

However, **después de** (after) takes the **prepositional** object pronoun, while **antes que** (before) takes the **subject** pronoun.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Entre tú y yo, creo que está chiflado. | Between you and me, I think he is barmy. |
| Intentaré después de ti. | I'll have a go after you. |
| si terminas antes que yo | if you finish before me |

#### 8.1.2.3 Ello — neuter subject pronoun

**Rule:** **Ello** is a neuter pronoun ('it', 'this', 'that') referring to a statement or concept — something to which no gender can be ascribed. It is rarely used in speech; **esto** or **eso** normally replace it.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ello/Esto dio paso a un largo periodo de inestabilidad. | This gave rise to a long period of instability. |

#### 8.1.2.4 Subject pronouns to translate 'it's me' etc.

**Rule:** The verb **ser** agrees in person and number with the subject pronoun used after it.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Soy yo, Juan. | It's me, Juan. |
| Somos nosotros quienes invitamos a María. | It's we who invited María. |

## **8.2 Direct and indirect object pronouns**

**Rule:** Direct and indirect object pronouns are **weak** pronouns — they can only be used with a verb and normally **precede** finite verbs. Direct and indirect forms are identical **except** in the 3rd person (*lo/la/los/las* vs. *le/les*). *Usted/ustedes* also use 3rd person forms.

**Summary of 3rd person object pronouns:**

| | Direct object | Indirect object |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | lo (m.) / la (f.) | le |
| Plural | los (m.) / las (f.) | les |

### **8.2.1 Direct object pronouns**

**Rule:** Direct object pronouns replace whoever or whatever receives the direct action of the verb. In the 3rd person they agree in **gender and number** with the item referred to. Mixed-gender groups use the masculine plural.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No lo conozco muy bien. | I don't know him very well. |
| Me criticaron. | They criticized me. |
| Nos han visto. | They have seen us. |
| Deja las cartas aquí y las subiré más tarde. | Leave the letters here and I'll take them upstairs later. |
| — ¿Dónde están Ana y Eduardo? — No los he visto. | 'Where are Ana and Eduardo?' 'I haven't seen them.' |

*Notes:*
- *Haber* is sometimes used with a direct object pronoun for an unexpressed noun: *Los hay en rojo* 'We have them in red'.
- *Los* can refer to *años*: *No los aparenta* 'He doesn't look it (his age)'.

### **8.2.2 Uses of indirect object pronouns**

#### 8.2.2.1 To whom something is given, directed or said

**Rule:** Indirect object pronouns most commonly indicate **to whom** something is given, directed or said.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Les trajo un regalo. | She brought them a present. |
| ¿Qué te ha dicho? | What has he said to you? |
| Le dirigió la mirada. | She looked at him. |

#### 8.2.2.3 With impersonal expressions

**Rule:** Indirect object pronouns are used with impersonal expressions using **resultar** (less commonly *ser*) + adjective (*fácil*, *difícil*, *imposible*, *necesario* etc.). The pronoun identifies the person the expression applies to.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Les resultó difícil adaptarse. | It was difficult for them to adapt. |
| No nos será fácil resolver el conflicto. | It will not be easy for us to resolve the dispute. |

### **8.2.3 Redundant use of indirect object pronouns**

**Rule:** Indirect object pronouns are commonly used **alongside** a noun functioning as the indirect object — especially when the noun refers to a person. This is sometimes called the redundant or doubling use.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Dile al mesero que traiga la comida. | Tell the waiter to bring the meal. |
| A Jorge no le gustó la película. | Jorge didn't like the film. |

## **8.3 Prepositional object pronouns**

**Rule:** Prepositional object pronouns have the same forms as subject pronouns **except** for 1st and 2nd person singular: *yo/tú* → *mí/ti*. Special combined forms: **conmigo** (con + mí), **contigo** (con + ti).

These are **strong pronouns** — they are used when the pronoun is governed by a preposition and stands apart from the verb.

### **8.3.1 General uses of prepositional object pronouns**

**Rule:** Prepositional object pronouns must be used when the pronoun is governed by a preposition. Note: some prepositions take subject pronouns instead — see 8.1.2.2.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Esto es para ustedes. | This is for you. |
| Estaban corriendo hacia vos. | They were running towards you. |
| Quería discutirlo contigo. | He wanted to discuss it with you. |
| Para ello necesito tu ayuda. | To do it I need your help. |

### **8.3.2 Uses of prepositional object pronouns after *a***

#### 8.3.2.1 For emphasis or contrast — *a* + pronoun alongside a weak pronoun

**Rule:** The sequence **a** + prepositional object pronoun is often used **alongside** a weak pronoun (direct or indirect) for clarity, emphasis or contrast.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| A mí no me han dicho nada. | No one has said anything to me. |
| ¿A ti te apetece cenar fuera? | Do you fancy going out for a meal? |
| Lo han detenido a él, pero no a ella. | He has been arrested but not her. |

**Rule:** When **a** indicates movement, direction or destination, only the prepositional object pronoun is used — **not** alongside a weak pronoun.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Vino a mí corriendo. | She ran up to me. |
| Se dirigió a mí. | He addressed me. |
| Nos acercamos a ellas. | We approached them. |

## **8.4 Reflexive pronouns**

**Rule:** Reflexive pronouns are used when the pronoun refers back to the subject. Weak reflexive pronouns generally **precede** finite verb forms (but see 8.5). Prepositional forms are used after a preposition, often alongside a weak reflexive. Add **mismo** for extra emphasis. Special form: **consigo** (con + sí).

**Table 8.2 — Reflexive pronouns:**

| Person | With verbs (weak) | Prepositional |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | me | mí |
| 2nd sg. familiar (tú/vos) | te | ti (or vos) |
| 2nd sg. polite (usted) | se | sí |
| 3rd sg. | se | sí |
| 1st pl. | nos | nosotros/as |
| 2nd pl. familiar [SP] (vosotros) | os | vosotros/as |
| 2nd pl. polite [SP] (ustedes) | se | sí |
| 2nd pl. familiar [LA] (ustedes) | se | sí |
| 3rd pl. | se | sí |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me corté. | I cut myself. |
| Estaba enfadado consigo mismo. | He was angry with himself. |
| No cabía en sí de alegría. | She was beside herself with joy. |
| No nos fiamos de nosotras mismas. | We do not trust ourselves. |
| Te estás engañando a ti mismo. | You are deceiving yourself. |

*Notes:*
- English '-self' used for mere emphasis (not reflexive): **Me lo dijo ella misma** (not ~~sí misma~~).
- After **entre**, **sí** is often replaced by non-reflexive pronouns: *Lo comentaron entre ellos* 'They discussed it among themselves'.

## **8.5 The position and order of personal pronouns**

**Rule:** Weak pronouns (direct/indirect object + non-prepositional reflexives) **directly precede** the finite verb **except** in: positive commands, infinitives and gerunds.

### **8.5.1 Position of personal pronouns in commands**

**Rule:** In **positive** commands, pronouns always **follow** and are joined to the verb. In **negative** commands, pronouns always **precede** the verb. Adding a pronoun to an imperative may require a written accent to preserve correct stress.

**Examples:**

| Positive command | English | Negative command | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ábrelo. | Open it. | No lo abras. | Don't open it. |
| Termínenlos. | Finish them. | No los terminen. | Do not finish them. |
| Acércate a mí. | Come towards me. | No te acerques a mí. | Do not approach me. |

### **8.5.2 Position of personal pronouns with infinitives and gerunds**

**Rule:** Weak pronouns frequently **follow and attach** to infinitives and gerunds. However, they may alternatively **precede** some governing verbs (especially auxiliaries). This is not possible when the infinitive is governed by a reflexive verb.

When attaching a pronoun to an infinitive or gerund, a written accent may be needed to preserve stress.

**Safest rule:** When in doubt, attach pronouns to the end of the infinitive or gerund: *Se negó a comprármelo*.

**Examples:**

| Attached | Preceding | English |
|---|---|---|
| Acaba de llamarme. | — | She has just called me. |
| Ha estado consultándonos. | — | He has been consulting us. |
| — | Le quería dar una sorpresa. | She wanted to give him a surprise. |
| — | Le sigue escribiendo a pesar de todo. | She continues to write to him despite everything. |
| — | Me lo hizo traer. | He made me bring it. |

**Verbs that allow pronoun before the infinitive:** acabar de, conseguir/lograr, deber, empezar a, hacer, ir a, intentar, necesitar, poder, preferir, querer, soler, tener que, terminar de, tratar de, volver a.

**Verbs that allow pronoun before the gerund:** andar, continuar/seguir, estar, venir.

### **8.5.3 The order of personal pronouns**

**Rule:** When two weak pronouns occur together, the **indirect object precedes the direct object**. The reflexive **se** is **always** in initial position regardless.

**Critical rule — le/les → se:** Whenever two 3rd person pronouns would begin with *l-*, the indirect object (*le/les*) is **always replaced by se**. Combinations like *les lo* or *le las* are **always incorrect**.

When *se* is ambiguous (he/she/you/them), a prepositional phrase with *a* + pronoun can be added for clarity.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No pienso dártelo. | I do not intend to give it to you. |
| Os lo han descrito detalladamente. | They have described it to you in detail. |
| Se nos presentó como un especialista. | He introduced himself to us as a specialist. |
| Ya se lo he mandado. | I've already sent it to him. |
| ¿Por qué no se lo propones a ella? | Why don't you ask her? |
| Queremos ofrecérselo a usted. | We want to offer it to you. |

## **8.6 Use of *le*(s) in place of *lo*(s) and *la*(s)**

### **8.6.1 Le instead of lo — leísmo**

**Rule:** The majority of Spanish speakers and the Spanish Academy prefer **lo** as the direct object pronoun for 'him'. However, especially in Madrid and central/northern Spain, **le** is preferred instead — this usage is called **leísmo** and is accepted by the Academy. The term also applies (less commonly) to the use of **les** for **los**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Variant |
|---|---|---|
| Lo veremos mañana. | We shall see him tomorrow. | Standard / Academy |
| Le veremos mañana. | We shall see him tomorrow. | [SP central/northern — leísmo] |

### **8.6.2 Other uses of *le* and *les***

#### 8.6.2.1 Le/les for *usted/ustedes* as direct object

**Rule:** **Le/les** is used as the polite 2nd person direct object pronoun for *usted/ustedes* — common in Spain and parts of Latin America.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No sé cómo esto le va a afectar a usted. | I do not know how this is going to affect you. |
| Encantado de conocerles. | Pleased to meet you. |

#### 8.6.2.2 Le/les with psychological verbs

**Rule:** **Le/les** is preferred with psychological verbs — especially when the subject is an inanimate noun or clause (e.g. *molestar*, *preocupar/inquietar*, *creer*, *entender*). This is common throughout the Spanish-speaking world.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Le molesta la luz del sol. | The sunlight is bothering him. |
| Le preocupa que el niño pese tan poco. | She is worried because the boy weighs so little. |

#### 8.6.2.3 Le/les required after impersonal se

**Rule:** **Le/les** is required (not *lo/la/los/las*) after impersonal **se** when the object is human. Obligatory in Spain, usual in Latin America.

#### 8.6.2.4 Les for los — central and northern Spain

**Rule:** **Les** for **los** ('them', referring to male persons) is common in central and northern Spain.

*Note: Also in central and northern Spain, **la** may replace **le** for female human beings (**laísmo**: *Dila que venga* 'Tell her to come'). This is **not** recognised by the Spanish Academy.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Variant |
|---|---|---|
| Les llevaron a su casa. | They took them to their home. | [SP central/northern] |
| Los llevaron a su casa. | They took them to their home. | Standard |

### CMP: lo vs le as 3rd person direct object pronoun

**Rule:** The choice between **lo** and **le** for the direct object 'him' is the central question of **leísmo**. The standard is **lo**; **le** is accepted in central/northern Spain.

| | lo | le |
|---|---|---|
| Reference | him (standard), it (masc.) | him (leísmo — Spain), you (usted) |
| Academy | preferred | accepted for male persons |
| Region | all Spanish-speaking world | central/northern Spain; usted throughout |
| Plural | los (them, masc.) | les (usted/ustedes; les for los in central Spain) |

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Lo veremos mañana. | We'll see him tomorrow. | standard — lo |
| Le veremos mañana. | We'll see him tomorrow. | leísmo — le [central SP] |
| No sé cómo esto le va a afectar a usted. | I don't know how this will affect you. | usted → le |
| Se le considera un autor importante. | He is considered an important author. | after se impersonal → le |
| Le molesta la luz del sol. | The sunlight bothers him. | psychological verb → le |
