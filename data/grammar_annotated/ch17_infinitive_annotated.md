# Chapter 17: Infinitive constructions

Infinitive forms end in **-ar**, **-er** or **-ir** and are invariable. They correspond to English 'to + verb' but also frequently translate English '-ing' forms.

**Key uses of the Spanish infinitive:**
- After a preposition: *después de cenar* 'after having supper'
- After another verb: *Quiero hablar* 'I want to speak'
- As the subject of a finite verb (see 17.4)
- As a verbal noun (see 17.6)
- As an imperative (see 19.3.2)

**Critical rule:** In Spanish, the infinitive is the **only** verb form that can follow a preposition — never the gerund. Compare English 'after speaking' → Spanish *después de hablar* (never ~~después de hablando~~).

*For placement of object pronouns with infinitives, see 8.5.2.*

## **17.1 Verb + infinitive**

### **17.1.1 Same subject — infinitive**

**Rule:** When one verb follows another and **both share the same subject**, the second verb takes the **infinitive**. Typical with modal verbs (Ch. 16), psychological verbs, and verbs expressing necessity or accomplishment.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Prefiero hablar con él. | I'd prefer to talk to him. |
| Recuerdo haberlo conocido en Jerez. | I remember having met him in Jerez. |
| Necesitamos comprar gasolina. | We need to buy petrol. |
| ¿Lograste hacerlo? | Did you manage to do it? |

### **17.1.2 Different subjects — direct infinitive**

**Rule:** A small number of verbs allow a direct infinitive even when the **subjects differ** (English uses 'to + verb'; Spanish can skip *que* + subjunctive). Main verbs in this group: **hacer** (make), **mandar/ordenar** (order), **dejar** (allow), **permitir** (permit), **impedir** (prevent), **persuadir** (persuade), **prohibir** (forbid), and perception verbs: **oír**, **escuchar**, **mirar**, **sentir**.

When no subject is specified for the infinitive, it has a **passive or impersonal sense**.

*For the alternative subjunctive construction, see 12.1.1.*

**Examples — different subjects:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Nos hicieron traducir un texto medieval. | They made us translate a medieval text. |
| No permiten fumar a los pasajeros. | They don't allow the passengers to smoke. |
| Le prohibieron poner la tele. | They forbade him to put on the TV. |
| Lola mandó callar a su hermano. | Lola told her brother to be quiet. |
| Te oí entrar anoche. | I heard you come in last night. |

**Examples — no subject specified (passive/impersonal):**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hice llamar a la policía. | I had the police called. |
| Oí cantar una rumba. | I heard someone singing a rumba. |
| Mandó llenar los bidones. | He ordered the drums to be filled. |

## **17.2 Prepositions + infinitive**

**Rule:** In Spanish, **only the infinitive** (never the gerund) follows a preposition. Which preposition follows which verb must be learned case by case, but some general patterns exist.

### **17.2.1 Preposition required by preceding verb**

**Rule:** The preposition is determined by the preceding verb. Five main preposition patterns:

**(a) A + infinitive** — used after verbs expressing: beginning, preparation or readiness, physical movement towards, obligation or persuasion.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Empezó/Se puso a llorar. | He began to cry. |
| Se comprometen a devolver lo pagado. | They undertake to return what has been paid. |
| Han venido a pintar la casa. | They've come to paint the house. |
| Me obligaron a pagar la multa. | They obliged me to pay the fine. |

*Note: **ir a** + infinitive (going to do) and **volver a** + infinitive (to do again) both require **a**: **No volveré a hacerlo** 'I won't do it again'.*

**(b) De + infinitive** — after verbs expressing movement away from, refraining, dissuading, or cessation; also cause or source; **acabar de** = 'to have just'.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me abstuve de reír. | I refrained from laughing. |
| ¿Han terminado de hablar? | Have they finished speaking? |
| Acaban de llegar. | They have just arrived. |
| Me alegro de haber ido. | I'm pleased I went. |
| Nos acusaron de ser espías. | They accused us of being spies. |
| Acuérdate de apagar las luces. | Remember to turn off the lights. |
| Alicia trató de convencerlos. | Alicia tried to convince them. |

*Similar verbs: **arrepentirse de** (to regret), **cansarse de** (to tire of), **cesar/dejar/parar de** (to stop), **disuadir de** (to dissuade from).*

**(c) Por + infinitive** — powerful longing or struggle 'for'; also begin/end 'by', opt 'for'.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Guillermo está luchando por triunfar. | Guillermo is fighting to succeed. |
| Se muere por poder hablar contigo. | She's dying to be able to talk to you. |
| Siempre acaba por aceptarlo. | He always ends up by accepting it. |
| Al final optó por estudiar informática. | In the end she opted for studying computing. |

*Similar verbs: **esforzarse por** (to make an effort to), **rabiar por** (to be dying to).*

**(d) En + infinitive** — matches English verb + 'in/on' + gerund.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Luis insistió en venir con nosotros. | Luis insisted on coming with us. |
| Quedamos en reunirnos a las dos. | We agreed on meeting at two o'clock. |
| Persisten en llamarnos. | They persist in calling us. |

*Note also: **pensar en** 'to think of/about'.*

**(e) Con + infinitive** — a few common verbs.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Le han amenazado con quitarle el permiso. | They have threatened him with taking away his licence. |
| Me conformo con dormir en el sofá. | I'm happy to sleep on the sofa. |
| María sueña con vivir al lado del mar. | María dreams of living by the sea. |

### **17.2.2 Preposition required by preceding noun**

**Rule:** Sometimes a specific preposition is demanded by a **noun**, not a verb. If the noun is derived from a verb, it usually takes the **same preposition** as the corresponding verb. Otherwise **de** is very common, though other prepositions are possible.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| su empeño en llamarme de usted | his insistence on addressing me as *usted* |
| Tiene la suerte de vivir en Madrid. | He's lucky enough to live in Madrid. |
| la idea de veranear con ellos | the idea of going on holiday with them |
| el derecho a votar | the right to vote |
| cierta tendencia a criticar | a certain tendency to be critical |

### **17.2.3 Translating 'the first/last to'**

**Rule:** After **último** 'last', **único** 'only', **primero** 'first' and similar ordinals, the infinitive is preceded by **en**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Fue el tercero y último en terminar. | He was the third and last to finish. |
| Ella fue la primera en llegar. | She was the first to arrive. |

### **17.2.4 Prepositions independent of a preceding item**

**Rule:** Some prepositions are used independently — not tied to a specific preceding verb or noun. Most common: **para** (in order to), **sin** (without), **por** (because of), and temporal prepositions **después de** (after), **antes de** (before).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Jorge se acostó sin cenar. | Jorge went to bed without having supper. |
| Huélelo antes de beberlo. | Smell it before drinking it. |

*For **por** and **para** + infinitive, see 22.1(b) and 22.2(c).*

### **17.2.5 Special cases: preposition + infinitive with unexpected meanings**

#### 17.2.5.1 Infinitives with passive sense — *a*, *sin*, *a medio*

**Rule:** Three constructions give the infinitive a **passive or incomplete sense**:
- **sin** + infinitive → 'un-' + past participle ('unironed', 'unsorted')
- **a medio** + infinitive → 'half-' + past participle ('half-built')
- **a** + infinitive → 'to be' + past participle ('to be settled')

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| una camisa sin planchar | an unironed shirt |
| una casa a medio construir | a half-built house |
| una deuda a liquidar | a debt to be settled |

*See also **por** in 22.1(k) for similar constructions.*

#### 17.2.5.2 Special meanings of *de* + infinitive

**Rule:** **De** + infinitive has three distinct meanings beyond the standard verb-governed use:
1. **Cause**: 'from' + gerund
2. **Ser + de + infinitive** = 'to be' + past participle (expectation/value judgement)
3. **Conditional sense** = 'if' clause (equivalent to *si* + past subjunctive)

**Examples:**

| Construction | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| cause | Estoy ronco de tanto hablar. | I'm hoarse from speaking so much. |
| *ser* + *de* + inf. | Es de esperar que dimita. | It is to be hoped that he'll resign. |
| *ser* + *de* + inf. | Su honestidad era de admirar. | His honesty was to be admired. |
| conditional | De saberlo no habría pagado. | If I had known I wouldn't have paid. |

#### 17.2.5.3 *Al* + infinitive

**Rule:** **Al** + infinitive is equivalent to English 'on' + '-ing' or 'when' + past tense.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Al ver lo que había pasado se puso a llorar. | On seeing / When he saw what had happened he started crying. |
| Al llegar Pedro se fue María. | When Pedro arrived María left. |

## **17.3 Infinitives in impersonal constructions**

**Rule:** Infinitives frequently appear in impersonal constructions where no individual is the subject. Main patterns:
- **Haber que** + infinitive — see 16.3
- **Ser** + obligation adjective (*preciso*, *obligatorio*, *necesario*) + infinitive
- **Se ruega** / **se prohíbe** + infinitive (formal notices)

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Fue preciso llamar a un médico. | It was necessary to call a doctor. |
| Es obligatorio reservar una plaza. | It is compulsory to reserve a place. |
| Se ruega no tocar las mercancías. | Customers are asked not to touch the merchandise. |
| Se prohíbe fumar. | Smoking is forbidden. |

## **17.4 Infinitive as the subject of a verb**

**Rule:** An infinitive (with its object if any) can function as the **subject of a finite verb**, and may be placed after it. When the construction is **ser + adjective + de + infinitive**, the **de is only used if the subject of *ser* is a noun or pronoun** — not if the infinitive itself is the subject. Students of French in particular should note this constraint.

**Examples — infinitive as subject:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Cenar con ellos fue una pesadilla. | Having dinner with them was a nightmare. |
| En aquella universidad enseñar era un suplicio. | At that university teaching was a terrible ordeal. |
| No me conviene vivir tan lejos. | Living so far away is not convenient for me. |

**Ser + adjective + de — only when subject is a noun/pronoun:**

| Spanish | English | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Esa puerta es difícil de abrir. | That door is difficult to open. | *de* used — *esa puerta* is subject |
| Eso es imposible de averiguar. | That is impossible to verify. | *de* used — *eso* is subject |
| Es imprescindible tenerlo. | It is essential to have it. | no *de* — infinitive is subject |
| No era posible cruzar el río. | Crossing the river was not possible. | no *de* — infinitive is subject |

## **17.5 Infinitive with an explicit subject**

**Rule:** Unlike in English, the Spanish infinitive may appear with its **own explicit subject** in the form of a noun or pronoun. The explicit subject must **follow** the infinitive. This construction is less common — a conjunction + finite verb is more usual.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Por llegar tú tarde, a mí me echaron una bronca. | Because you arrived late, I got told off. |
| Al llegar Pedro se fue María. | When Pedro arrived María left. |
| (more usual) Me echaron una bronca porque tú llegaste tarde. | I was told off because you arrived late. |

## **17.6 Infinitive as a verbal noun**

**Rule:** The Spanish infinitive can function as a **verbal noun** (where English uses the gerund). It can take the definite/indefinite article (masculine), or be modified by a demonstrative, possessive, or adjective. This usage is characteristic of **literary Spanish**, not everyday speech.

Some infinitives have become established as **true masculine nouns** with plural forms and their own meanings.

**Examples — infinitive as literary noun:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se oye el gemir de muchos heridos. | The groaning of many wounded can be heard. |
| Oímos un chirriar de ruedas dentro de la máquina. | We heard a screeching of wheels inside the machine. |
| Ese continuo pelear no se puede tolerar. | That continuous fighting is intolerable. |
| el lento descargar de los buques | the slow unloading of the ships |

**Infinitives established as true nouns:**

| Infinitive | Noun meaning | Plural | Plural meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| parecer | opinion | — | — |
| amanecer | daybreak | — | — |
| atardecer | dusk | — | — |
| anochecer | nightfall | — | — |
| pesar | sorrow, grief | — | — |
| saber | knowledge | — | — |
| andar | walk, gait | andares | travels, adventures |
| deber | duty | deberes | homework, assignment |
| decir | figure of speech | decires | sayings, rumours |
| haber | credit | haberes | assets, income, earnings |
