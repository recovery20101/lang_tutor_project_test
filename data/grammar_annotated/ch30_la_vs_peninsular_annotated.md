# Chapter 30: Differences between Latin American and Peninsular Spanish

Latin American and Peninsular Spanish are mutually comprehensible but differ in several important respects. Significant variation also exists within Latin American Spanish itself. Key features that distinguish Latin American from Peninsular Spanish are covered in this chapter.

*Note: Throughout the book, Latin American usages are marked [LA] and Peninsular usages are marked [SP]. This chapter collates and expands on those instances.*

## **30.1 Pronunciation**

### **30.1.1 Pronunciation of *z* — *seseo***

**Rule:** In most of the Iberian Peninsula (except Andalusia), **z** and **c** before *i/e* are pronounced like 'th' in 'think'. In Latin America this sound does not exist — replaced by 's' of 'six'. This phenomenon is called **seseo** and is also normal in much of southern Spain and the Canary Islands.

**Effect:** Word pairs that are distinct in Peninsular Spanish become homophonous in Latin American Spanish:

| Word pair | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *rosado* / *rozado* | pink / worn-grazed |
| *masa* / *maza* | dough / meat tenderizer |

### **30.1.2 Pronunciation of *ll* — *yeísmo***

**Rule:** In conservative central/northern Peninsular Spanish, **ll** is pronounced like 'lli' in 'million'. Speakers in Latin America (except Paraguay and parts of the Andes), the Canaries and much of southern Spain pronounce **ll** like 'y' in 'yes'. This is called **yeísmo** — these speakers make no distinction between *ll* and *y*.

**River Plate variant (*žeísmo/rehilamiento*):** In Buenos Aires, Montevideo and their hinterland, both **ll** and **y** are pronounced like 's' in English 'pleasure' or 'sh' in 'shut'.

**Effect:** Word pairs that are distinct in conservative Peninsular Spanish become homophonous for yeísta speakers:

| Word pair | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *pollo* / *poyo* | chicken / stone bench |
| *halla* / *haya* | he/she finds / present subjunctive of *haber* |

### **30.1.3 Pronunciation of *j***

**Rule:** In most of the Iberian Peninsula (except Andalusia), **j** (and **g** before *i/e*) is pronounced like 'ch' in Scottish 'loch'. In large parts of Latin America (especially Caribbean basin and Pacific coast), **j** and **g** before *i/e* are pronounced like 'h' in 'hat'. Example: *paja* → pronounced *paha*.

## **30.2 Forms of address — *vosotros* vs *ustedes* and *voseo***

**Rule:** Two major differences in address forms:

1. **Vosotros is not used in Latin America** (or in the Canaries and much of southern Spain). Its place is taken by **ustedes** with 3rd person plural verb forms.
2. In large parts of Latin America, **vos** is used as familiar 2nd person singular instead of **tú** (**voseo**) — normal in River Plate and Central America; absent in Mexico (except far south), Caribbean and most of Peru.

**Vosotros [SP] → ustedes [LA]:**

| Spain (except south) | Latin America |
|---|---|
| Vosotros habláis | Ustedes hablan |
| Vosotros coméis | Ustedes comen |
| Vosotros vivís | Ustedes viven |

### **30.2.1 Verb forms associated with *vos***

**Rule:** *Vos* verb forms differ from *tú* only in the **present indicative** and **imperative** (see 19.1.2). All other tenses are identical to *tú* forms.

**Present indicative:** *Vos* forms derived from *vosotros* forms — the unstressed *-i-* of *-ar/-er* endings is deleted. For *-ir* verbs, *vos* and *vosotros* present indicative are identical.

**Present indicative — River Plate / Central America pattern:**

| Conjugation | *Vos* form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -ar | *cantás* | *vos cantás* |
| -er | *comés* | *vos comés* |
| -ir | *vivís* | *vos vivís* |
| ser | *sos* | *vos sos* |

**Present subjunctive** (used by some speakers — delete *-i-* from *vosotros* form):

| Conjugation | *Vos* form |
|---|---|
| -ar | *vos cantés* |
| -er | *vos comás* |
| -ir | *vos vivás* |

**Future tense** (less widely accepted):

| Conjugation | *Vos* form |
|---|---|
| -ar | *vos cantarés* |
| -er | *vos comerés* |
| -ir | *vos vivirés* |

**Alternative pattern** (parts of Venezuela, northern Chile, Bolivia): *vos* forms identical to *vosotros* forms; *-éis* in -er present indicative and future of all conjugations often replaced by *-ís*: *vos comís*, *vos cantarís*.

### **30.2.2 Object pronouns and possessives associated with *vos***

**Rule:** The weak object pronoun and possessives for *vos* are the **same as for *tú***: *te*, *tu*, *tuyo*. However, **vos** (not *ti*) is used as the object of a preposition.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Acordáte que fue idea tuya. | Remember it was your idea. |
| ¿Creés que venderás tu carro? | Do you think you will sell your car? |
| Estaba pensando en vos. | I was thinking about you. |

## **30.3 *Le* and *lo***

**Rule:** Many Peninsular speakers (especially central/northern Spain) use **le** as the masculine singular direct object pronoun for a man (leísmo). In Latin America (except Ecuador and Paraguay), **lo** is almost always used instead — as in Andalusia and the Canaries.

| Peninsular usage | Latin American usage | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Le hice entrar. | Lo hice entrar. | I made him go in. |
| Le llamé ayer. | Lo llamé ayer. | I called him yesterday. |

*For full discussion of leísmo, see 8.6.*

## **30.4 Uses of tenses**

### **30.4.1 Sequence of tenses with the subjunctive**

**Rule:** In Peninsular Spanish, a preterite or imperfect in the main clause requires the **imperfect subjunctive** in the subordinate clause (see 12.4). In many varieties of Latin American Spanish, the **present subjunctive** is acceptable even with a past main clause.

**Examples:**

| Peninsular | Latin American | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Les pidió que entraran. | Les pidió que entren. [LA] | He asked them to come in. |
| — | Quería que lo hagamos hoy. [LA] | He wanted us to do it today. |

### **30.4.2 Use of preterite instead of perfect**

**Rule:** In much of Latin America (except possibly the Andes), the **preterite** is used where Peninsular speakers would use the **perfect**. Comparable to British English 'Have you done it yet?' vs American English 'Did you do it yet?'

**Examples:**

| Peninsular | Latin American | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Todavía no han llamado. | Todavía no llamaron. [LA] | They haven't called yet. |
| ¿Nunca has visto *Lo que el viento se llevó*? | ¿Nunca viste *Lo que el viento se llevó*? [LA] | Have you never seen *Gone with the Wind*? |
| Me tengo que ir. He quedado con Luis. | Me tengo que ir. Quedé con Luis. [LA] | I have to go. I've arranged to meet Luis. |

## **30.5 *El que*, *quien* and *que* in focus constructions**

### **30.5.1 Mainstream Latin American tendencies**

**Rule:** In Peninsular Spanish, cleft sentence focus constructions require *el que*, *quien*, *cuando*, *donde* or *como* — and any preposition must be repeated before the relative (see 25.6). In many Latin American varieties, when the focused element is a **place, time** or **precedes a preposition**, plain **que** is used instead, with no preposition duplication.

**Examples:**

| Peninsular | Latin American | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Fue de política de lo que hablaron. | Fue de política que hablaron. [LA] | It was politics they talked about. |
| Fue en la fiesta donde la vi. | Fue en la fiesta que la vi. [LA] | It was at the party that I saw her. |
| Fue ayer cuando llegaron. | Fue ayer que llegaron. [LA] | It was yesterday that they arrived. |

### **30.5.2 Intensive *ser* — Caribbean/Colombian/Ecuadorian**

**Rule:** A more geographically restricted variant (Caribbean, Colombia, Ecuador) omits *donde/cuando/quien/el que* entirely — leaving just *ser* + focused element:

**Examples:**

| Peninsular | Caribbean/Colombian/Ecuadorian | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Quien lo hizo fue Pedro. | Lo hizo fue Pedro. | It was Pedro who did it. |
| Con la que hablé fue con María. | Hablé fue con María. | It was with María I spoke. |
| Donde la vi fue en casa de José. | La vi fue en casa de José. | It was at José's that I saw her. |
| Cuando llegaron fue ayer. | Llegaron fue ayer. | It was yesterday that they arrived. |

### **30.6.3 Items requiring special attention**

**Rule:** A number of common words have shifted meaning in parts of Latin America, creating potential for misunderstanding with Peninsular Spanish. These are key items for any learner who will encounter Latin American Spanish.

**(30.6.3.1) *Mero* [LA]** — Peninsular: 'mere/simple/only'. Latin American (especially Mexico and Central America) additional uses:
- (a) 'very'/'him/herself' (like *mismo*): *en el mero centro* 'in the very centre'
- (b) 'exact/precise': *a la mera hora* 'at the precise time'
- (c) adverb 'just/right' (like *mismo*): *ya mero* 'right now', *aquí mero* 'just here'
- (d) 'almost/nearly': *y merito me ahogaba* 'and I was almost drowning'

**(30.6.3.2) *Puro* [LA]** — Peninsular: 'pure/sheer/simple'. Latin American additional uses:
- (a) 'only': *Hay puras mujeres* 'There are only women'
- (b) 'very/him/herself' (like *mero* and *mismo*): *Es del puro Cusco* 'He is from Cusco itself'
- (c) adverb 'very': *Su hija es puro traviesa* 'Their daughter is very naughty'

**(30.6.3.3) *Hasta* [LA]** — Peninsular: 'until'. Mexico/Central America/Colombia in speech and writing: 'not until': *Hasta las dos almuerzo* 'I do not have lunch until two o'clock', *Hasta ayer vino* 'He did not come until yesterday'.

**(30.6.3.4) *Coger*** — Best avoided outside the Peninsula; in Latin America widely means 'to have sex'. Use *tomar* or *agarrar* as alternatives.

**(30.6.3.5) *Demasiado* [LA]** — Peninsular: 'too much'. Latin America: often means 'very' (like *muy/mucho*): *Carmen es demasiado inteligente* 'Carmen is very intelligent'.

**(30.6.3.6) *De repente* [LA]** — Peninsular: 'suddenly'. Andes/River Plate: 'perhaps': *De repente se durmió* 'Perhaps he fell asleep'.

**(30.6.3.7) *Despacio* [LA]** — Peninsular: 'slowly'. Latin America: also 'quietly/in a low voice': *¡Hablen ustedes despacio!* 'Speak in a low voice'. *Despaciosamente* used for 'slowly' to avoid confusion.

**(30.6.3.8) *No más/nomás* [LA]** — Not used as such in the Peninsula. Multiple Latin American meanings:
- (a) 'just/only': *hace una semana no más* 'only a week ago'
- (b) strengthens adjectives/adverbs: *Es un jardín lindo no más* 'It's a pretty garden'
- (c) emphasizes verbs/commands: *Siga no más* 'Please continue', *Siéntense no más* 'Just sit down'

**(30.6.3.9) *Recién* [LA]** — Peninsular: only before past participles (see 7.2.3). Latin American (especially South America) additional uses:
- (a) 'just now/recently' (like *acabar de*): *Recién salió* 'She just left'
- (b) 'only (then)/not before/until': *Recién entonces todos concurrieron* 'Only then did everyone agree'
- (c) 'hardly/barely/no sooner': *Recién terminó cuando empezó a llover* 'No sooner had he finished than it started to rain'
