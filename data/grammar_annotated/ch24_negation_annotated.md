# Chapter 24: Negation

Negation in Spanish differs from English in two key ways:
1. Spanish **never** uses *hacer* 'to do' as a negation support verb (contrast English 'John doesn't drive').
2. **Double negatives** are standard and required in Spanish — not incorrect as in English: *No vimos a nadie* (literally 'We didn't see nobody').

## **24.1 *No***

### **24.1.1 *No* used with verbs**

**Rule:** *No* **precedes** the verb (and its auxiliary in compound constructions). The only item that may be placed between *no* and the verb is a **weak object pronoun**. If a subject is mentioned, it must come **before** *no* or **after** the verb.

**Exception:** *No* can also negate a word other than the verb (e.g. *todos*, *siempre*) — in this case it precedes only that word.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No quieren venir. | They do not want to come. |
| No has puesto tu edad. | You haven't filled in your age. |
| No lo veo muy claramente. | I can't see it very clearly. |
| Luis no se presentó. / No se presentó Luis. | Luis didn't turn up. |
| ¿No te gusta? | Don't you like it? |
| No todos podemos tener un sueldo como el suyo. | Not all of us can have a salary like his. |
| No siempre ayuda. | He does not always help. |
| Le interesa el fútbol pero no (le interesa) el rugby. | He likes football but not rugby. |

### **24.1.2 *No* used to negate words other than verbs**

**Rule:** *No* can negate adjectives or the adverb *muy*. As a complement to other words, *no* is usually placed **after** them.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| una huelga no oficial | an unofficial strike |
| una casa no muy grande | a not very big house |
| — ¿Vendrá pronto? — Mañana no. | 'Will he come soon?' 'Not tomorrow.' |
| ¡Corridas no! | No (to) bullfights! |

## **24.2 Other negative words**

**Rule:** The following negative words may be used **before** the verb alone, or **after** the verb in a double-negative construction (the verb must then be preceded by *no*). Both patterns are correct:

*Nunca viene* = *No viene nunca* = 'He never comes'.

### **24.2.1 *Nadie* and *nada***

**Rule:**
- **Nadie** 'no one', 'not . . . anyone' — refers only to persons; may precede or follow the verb.
- **Nada** 'nothing', 'not . . . anything' — more common as the verb's object, so usually follows. When qualified by an adjective, *nada* takes **masculine** gender.

**Additional uses of *nada*:**
- Adverb: 'not at all', 'by no means' — *No me gusta nada lo que estoy escuchando*
- Noun: 'nothing', 'nothingness' — *La amenaza surgió de la nada*
- Fixed phrases: *pues nada* (well then), *de nada* (you're welcome), *nada más* (nothing else)
- *Nada más* + infinitive (colloquial) = *en cuanto* 'as soon as' — *Nada más levantarse salen de casa*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No descubrió nada. | He found nothing / He didn't find anything. |
| ¿No le felicitó nadie ayer? | Did no one congratulate him yesterday? |
| Nadie quiere ser asociado con ella. | No one wants to be associated with her. |
| La teoría no tiene nada novedoso. | The theory has nothing new. |

### **24.2.2 *Nunca* and *jamás***

**Rule:** Both mean 'never', 'not ever'. May precede or follow the verb. **Jamás** is more emphatic and less common. The subject cannot be placed between either word and a following verb. *Nunca* (not *jamás*) is used in comparisons. Used together (*nunca jamás*) for maximum emphasis.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Rocío nunca viene a cenar a nuestra casa. | Rocío never comes to dinner at our house. |
| No he probado el gazpacho nunca. | I have never had gazpacho. |
| mejor tarde que nunca | better late than never |
| Nunca jamás volveré a prestarle mi diccionario. | I will never ever lend him my dictionary again. |

### **24.2.3 *Ni ... ni* and *ni (siquiera)***

**Rule:** *Ni ... ni* = 'neither ... nor'. May precede or follow the verb. *Ni (siquiera)* = 'not even' — *siquiera* is optional reinforcement. *Ni* is followed by *nadie/nada/ninguno* (NOT *alguien/algo/alguno*). *Ni que* + imperfect subjunctive = emphatic negative exclamation ('as if').

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ni el queso ni las cebollas me gustan. | I like neither cheese nor onions. |
| No fueron ni María, ni su esposo, ni sus hijos. | Neither María, nor her husband, nor their children went. |
| Ni (siquiera) en la clase lo entendí. | Not even in class did I understand it. |
| No me contestó siquiera. | He did not even answer me. |
| ¡Ni que ella fuera la más lista de la clase! | As if she was the cleverest in the class! |

### **24.2.4 *Ninguno***

**Rule:** *Ninguno* = 'no', 'not any', 'none' — used as adjective or pronoun, alone or in double-negative construction. Agrees in gender with the associated noun. Shortens to **ningún** before a masculine singular noun. Rarely used in the plural as an adjective (only with nouns that are always plural — see 2.1.8). Placed **after** the noun for emphatic effect. Note: *alguno* after the noun is even stronger — see 9.3.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ninguna de las chicas me conoce. | None of the girls know(s) me. |
| Ya no se ve a ningún hombre vestido así. | You no longer see any men dressed like that. |
| No encontró ningunos pantalones que le quedaran bien. | He could not find any trousers which suited him. |
| No hay razón ninguna. | There is no reason at all. (emphatic — after noun) |

### **24.2.5 *Tampoco***

**Rule:** *Tampoco* = 'neither', 'not either'. May precede the verb alone, or follow a verb preceded by *no* or *ni*. The associated verb is dropped in short negative replies — like English 'nor' / 'neither'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tampoco vino Isabel. | Isabel didn't come either. |
| No vieron a los jugadores tampoco. | They did not see the players either. |
| — No me gustó nada. — A mí tampoco. | 'I didn't like it at all.' 'Nor did I.' |
| — No han traído la comida. — La cerveza tampoco. | 'They have not brought the food.' 'Nor the beer either.' |

### **24.2.6 Combinations of negatives**

**Rule:** Two or more negative words may combine in one sentence. **No** (or another negative word) **must** precede the verb. Order generally follows English order. Avoid strings of more than two negatives after the verb.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No me trajeron nunca nada. | They never brought me anything. |
| Nunca me trajeron nada. | They never brought me anything. (alt. order) |

## **24.3 Expressions using *no***

**Rule:** Several fixed constructions use *no* with specific meanings.

**(a) More emphatic negation:** *claro que no*, *desde luego que no*, *por supuesto que no* = 'of course not'.

**(b) *No* + verb + *más que* / *sino* = 'no more than', 'only', 'nothing but':**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No hace sino emborracharse. | He does nothing but get drunk. |

**(c) *No ... sino* — denying one idea and affirming another ('not ... but'); *no solo/solamente ... sino (también)* = 'not only ... but (also)'. *Sino que* required before a clause:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No fue el viento sino el agua lo que lo destruyó. | It was not the wind but the water that destroyed it. |
| Vino no solo su madre sino también su abuela. | Not only her mother but also her grandmother came. |
| No solo va a la escuela sino que va con muchas ganas. | He not only goes to school, but he goes willingly. |

**(d) Superfluous *no*:** In some constructions a seemingly redundant *no* is used where no negation is intended. Most common after **hasta que** when the main clause is negative, and sometimes in comparisons after *que*:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No les des nada hasta que no terminen el trabajo. | Don't give them anything until they finish the work. |
| Es mejor que esperes más tiempo que no que te vayas sin verlo. | It's better for you to wait longer than go off without seeing him. |

## **24.4 Spanish negatives with English affirmative meaning**

**Rule:** In certain contexts, Spanish uses a negative word where English uses no negative. This is particularly common after **sin**, **sin que**, **antes de**, **antes que**, **apenas**; in comparisons; after phrases expressing impossibility/doubt/denial; and in questions expecting a negative answer.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Context |
|---|---|---|
| sin afirmar nunca nada | without ever stating anything | after *sin* |
| Antes que nada debo ver el documento original. | First of all I must see the original document. | after *antes que* |
| Jugó mejor que nadie. | He played better than anyone. | comparison |
| Dudo que nadie viva en este lugar. | I doubt whether anyone lives in this place. | after doubt |
| Era imposible que ninguno de ellos lo robara. | It was impossible that any of them should steal it. | after impossibility |
| ¿Es que jamás le molestó a nadie? | Did it ever upset anyone? | question expecting negative |

## **24.5 Affirmative phrases with negative meaning**

**Rule:** Some seemingly affirmative phrases are used without a negative word to convey negative meaning. Most common: **en mi/la vida** 'never in my life' and **en absoluto** 'absolutely not', 'not at all'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| En mi/la vida he estado en un hotel tan malo. | Never in my life have I been in such a terrible hotel. |
| — ¿Vas a consultarla? — En absoluto. | 'Are you going to consult her?' 'Absolutely not.' |
