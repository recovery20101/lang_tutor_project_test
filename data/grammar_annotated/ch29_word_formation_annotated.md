# Chapter 29: Word formation

This chapter covers the structure of Spanish words: **suffixes**, **prefixes**, and **compound words**. Understanding these patterns helps learners decode unfamiliar vocabulary and form new words.

**Key notes on suffix addition:**
- A final vowel is often modified or deleted before a suffix: *sereno* → *serenidad*
- Spelling changes apply per the general rules (see 1.2): *pedazo* but *pedacito*, *cerca* but *cerquita*
- When a suffix shifts stress away from *ie* or *ue*, these often revert to *e* and *o*: *caliente* → *calentito*, *puerta* → *portezuela*

## **29.1 Suffixes**

### **29.1.1 Suffixes forming abstract nouns**

**Rule:** These suffixes create **feminine** abstract nouns. Most transform adjectives; **-anza** changes verbs. The base word is typically slightly modified before the suffix is added.

| Suffix | Base word | Meaning | New word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **-ancia** | arrogante | arrogant | arrogancia | arrogance |
| **-dad** | curioso | curious | curiosidad | curiosity |
| **-dumbre** | manso | gentle | mansedumbre | gentleness |
| **-encia** | inocente | innocent | inocencia | innocence |
| **-ez** | rígido | rigid | rigidez | rigidity |
| **-eza** | limpio | clean | limpieza | cleanliness |
| **-ura** | largo | long | largura | length |
| **-anza** | desconfiar | to distrust | desconfianza | mistrust |

*Note: **-ura** also forms words with English '-ure': **superestructura**, **arquitectura**.*

### **29.1.2 Suffixes that indicate places**

**Rule:** Several suffixes indicate locations or places.

- **-al / -ar** → grouping, abundance, or the place where something is found (common with plants/trees)
- **-era / -ero** → place where something is stored/put; *-dero* = where something is done
- **-ero** → also designates persons who make, own or trade in something; **-ía** adds to give the shop/place of trade

| Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning | Suffix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chaparro | dwarf oak | chaparral | dwarf oak thicket | -al |
| pino | pine tree | pinar | pine grove | -ar |
| llave | key | llavero | key ring | -ero (place) |
| papel | paper | papelera | waste-paper basket | -era |
| desembarcar | to disembark | desembarcadero | landing stage | -dero |
| pescado | fish | pescadero | fishmonger | -ero (person) |
| rancho | ranch | ranchero | rancher | -ero (person) |
| fruta | fruit | frutero → frutería | fruit seller → fruit shop | -ero + -ía |

### **29.1.3 Suffixes forming adjectives**

**Rule:** Two productive adjective-forming suffixes:
- **-oso** → possessing the quality of the base noun (affective)
- **-udo** → possessing it to an exaggerated/excessive degree
- **-(d)izo** → susceptible or likely to act as indicated by the base verb or adjective

| Suffix | Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **-oso** | cariño | affection | cariñoso | affectionate |
| **-oso** | talento | talent | talentoso | talented |
| **-udo** | pelo | hair | peludo | hairy/shaggy |
| **-udo** | barriga | belly | barrigudo | pot-bellied |
| **-(d)izo** | pegar | to stick | pegadizo | sticky/catchy |
| **-(d)izo** | enfermo | ill | enfermizo | sickly |
| **-(d)izo** | escurrirse | to slip | escurridizo | slippery |

### **29.1.4 Action suffixes**

**Rule:** Several suffixes derive nouns and verbs from action concepts.

- **-mento / -miento** → nouns of action (from verbs)
- **-ador / -edor / -idor** → person who performs the action (-ar/-er/-ir verbs); feminine **-dora** often used for appliances
- **Infinitive minus ending + -a/-o/-e** → action noun (no definable rule)
- **-ada / -ida** (past participle feminine) → action or result of an action
- **-ada** (added to nouns) → blow/action with an instrument; amount contained; period of time
- **-azo** → blow/action with instrument; abbreviated political events (colloq.)

| Suffix | Base | New word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| -miento | hundir | hundimiento | sinking |
| -mento | salvar | salvamento | rescue |
| -ador | navegar | navegador | navigator/browser |
| -edor | beber | bebedor | drinker |
| -idor | seguir | seguidor | follower/fan |
| -dora | secar | secadora | tumble dryer |
| (stem) | cerrar | cierre | closing/closure |
| (stem) | volar | vuelo | flight |
| -ada | llegar | llegada | arrival |
| -ada | lavar | lavada | wash |
| -ada | puñal | puñalada | stabbing |
| -ada | cuchara | cucharada | spoonful |
| -ada | tiempo | temporada | season |
| -azo | espalda | espaldazo | pat on the back |
| -azo | teléfono | telefonazo | call/ring |

### **29.1.6 Affective suffixes (diminutives and augmentatives)**

**Rule:** Affective suffixes are attached to nouns (and sometimes adjectives/adverbs) to convey **smallness** (diminutives), **largeness** (augmentatives), **affection**, or **scorn**. Their use shows significant regional variation across Spain and Latin America. Learners should use them with caution until confident.

#### **29.1.6.1 Diminutive suffixes**

**(a) Main diminutives: *-ito*, *-illo*, *-uelo***

**Formation rules:**
- Simple **-ito/-illo/-uelo** (+ fem. **-a**) → added after removing final **-o/-a**
- **-cito/-cillo/-zuelo** → used when base has more than one syllable and ends in **-n**, **-r**, or **-e**
- **-ecito/-ecillo/-ezuelo** → added (after removing **-o/-a**) to two-syllable words with *ie/ue* in first syllable, or to monosyllables ending in consonant or *y*

**Nuances:**
- **-ito** (most frequent): friendliness, affection, 'nice', 'pretty'; used with children, proper names, adjectives, adverbs
- **-illo**: smallness without emotion; may convey lesser importance; sometimes changes meaning
- **-uelo**: smallness, insignificance, often pejorative; mainly used with nouns

| Base | Meaning | Diminutive | Meaning | Suffix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clavo | nail | clavito | small nail | -ito |
| bolso | bag | bolsillo | pocket | -illo |
| pollo | chicken | polluelo | chick | -uelo |
| ladrón | thief | ladronzuelo | petty thief | -zuelo |
| autor | author | autorcillo | small-time author | -cillo |
| peine | comb | peinecillo | fine comb | -cillo |
| nuevo/a | new | nuevecito/a | nice and new | -ecito |
| piedra | stone | piedrecita | little pebble | -ecita |
| rey | king | reyezuelo | petty king | -ezuelo |
| abuela | grandmother | abuelita | granny | -ita |
| ventana | window | ventanilla | ticket office window | -illa |
| pintor | painter | pintorzuelo | second-rate painter | -zuelo |

**With adjectives/adverbs:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¡habla bajito! | speak quite low! |
| cerquita | quite close |
| tempranito | nice and early |
| un poquito | a little bit |

**(b) Other regional diminutive suffixes:**

| Suffix | Region | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-ín** | Asturian (changes fem. → masc.) | calabacín (courgette) |
| **-ino/a** | Extremaduran | tesina (dissertation) |
| **-iño/a** | Galician | botellín (small beer bottle) |
| **-ico** | Aragonese (now mainly Central America/Colombia) | vasico (small glass) |
| **-ete/-eta** | General (sometimes pejorative) | avioneta (light plane), historieta (short story) |
| **-ejo** | General (smallness + scorn) | papelejo (scrap of paper), animalejo (nasty little animal) |

#### **29.1.6.2 Augmentative suffixes**

**Rule:** Augmentatives indicate **large size**, often with implications of **clumsiness** or **coarseness**. Some have exclusively **pejorative** meaning. Occasionally they change the meaning of the base word without indicating size.

**(a) *-ón/-ona* → large size; attached to feminine nouns → often become masculine; also from verb stems (excessive action):**

| Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| cuchara | spoon | cucharón | ladle |
| pimienta | pepper | pimentón | paprika |
| cintura | waist | cinturón | belt |
| dulce | sweet | dulzón | sickly sweet |
| faltar | be absent | faltón | regular absentee |
| mirar | look | mirón | starer |

*Note: **-ón** sometimes denotes DIMINUTIVE meaning: **rata** → **ratón** (mouse), **torre** → **torreón** (turret).*

**(b) *-azo/-aza* → increased size; agrees with gender; also used as superlative in parts of Latin America:**

| Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| cupón | lottery ticket | cuponazo | special lottery |
| bueno/a | good | buenazo/a | very kindly |

*For other uses of **-azo**, see 29.1.4.*

**(c) *-ote/-ota* → excessive size, often coarseness:**

| Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| libro | book | librote | boring tome |
| palabra | word | palabrota | swear word |

*Note: **-ote** can denote DIMINUTIVE meaning: **isla** → **islote** (islet).*

**(d) Exclusively pejorative suffixes** (*-aco*, *-acho*, *-ajo*, *-astro*, *-uco*, *-ucho*):

| Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| casa | house | casucha | hovel |
| rico | rich | ricacho | filthy rich person |
| blanco | white | blancucho | dirty white |
| cama | bed | camastro | rickety old bed |

### **29.1.7 Combinations of suffixes**

**Rule:** Suffixes can sometimes be stacked. Not widespread, but some combinations are established.

**Examples:**

| Base | Steps | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| chico | chico → chiquit- | chiquitito/chiquitillo/chiquitín | very small |
| calle | calle → callejón | callejón | alley |
| guapo/a | guapo → guapetón | guapetón/ona | very handsome |
| puño | puño → puñetazo | puñetazo | punch/blow with fist |
| río | río → riachuelo | riachuelo | small stream |

*In Latin America the suffix is sometimes repeated for emphasis: **Me han dejado aquí solitita** 'They've left me here all alone'.*

## **29.2 Prefixes**

**Rule:** Prefixes are less common than suffixes in Spanish word formation but easier for English speakers since Spanish and English often share the same prefixes with similar functions. Spanish generally does **not** use a hyphen to link a prefix to the following word.

### **29.2.1 *De(s)-***

**Rule:** The most common Spanish prefix. Primarily indicates **negative or opposite** meaning. Also forms verbs meaning something is **removed** — even without a corresponding base verb.

| Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| animar | to encourage | desanimar | to discourage |
| montar | to assemble | desmontar | to dismantle |
| congelar | to freeze | descongelar | to defrost |
| nata | cream | desnatar | to skim |
| cafeína | caffeine | descafeinar | to decaffeinate |
| pelo | hair | depilar(se) | to remove hair |

### **29.2.2 *En-***

**Rule:** Denotes 'placing inside' or 'covering'. Becomes **em-** before **b** and **p**. Can also simply form a verb from a noun without the enclosing sense. Can be preceded by **des-** to reverse the action.

| Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| cal | lime | encalar | whitewash |
| cárcel | prison | encarcelar | imprison |
| bala | bundle | embalar | pack |
| paquete | package | empaquetar | to package |
| camino | path | encaminar | to channel/direct |
| foco | focus | enfocar | to focus (on) |

*Note: **des-** + **en-** → reverses the action: **desenterrar** 'to unearth', **desenredar** 'to unravel'.*

### **29.2.3 *In-***

**Rule:** Negates an adjective (sometimes a noun). Becomes **i-** before **l**, **im-** before **b** and **p**, **ir-** before **r**.

| Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| alambre | wire | inalámbrico | cordless |
| decisión | decision | indecisión | indecision |
| lícito | legal | ilícito | illicit |
| borrar | to rub out | imborrable | indelible |
| regular | regular | irregular | irregular |

### **29.2.4 *Re-***

**Rule:** Indicates **repetition** or doing again (like English 're-'). In popular speech also an **intensifier** — strengthened as **rete-** [mainly LA] and **requete-**.

| Base | Meaning | Derived | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| elección | election | reelección | reelection |
| orientar | to direct | reorientar | to redirect |
| bronceado | suntanned | rebronceado | very suntanned |
| lindo [LA] | pretty | relindo | wonderful |
| — | — | requetebién/retebién [LA] | brilliant |

### **29.2.6 *Anti-*, *super-*, *contra-*, *tra(n)s-*, *pos(t)-***

**Rule:** Modern prefixes — increasingly productive in media Spanish, especially to form new nouns and adjectives.
- **anti-** = against, opposed to
- **super-** = extra, excess; also used as an adverb
- **contra-** = counter, contra
- **tras-/trans-** = across, through (not always interchangeable)
- **pos(t)-** = after

*No hyphen in Spanish. Some *súper-* words may abbreviate to just the prefix: **el súper** (supermarket), **la super** (four-star petrol).*

| Prefix | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| anti- | crema antiarrugas | anti-wrinkle cream |
| anti- | superficie antiadherente | non-stick surface |
| super- | supercarburante | high-octane fuel |
| super- | super ambicioso | very/over ambitious |
| contra- | contraoferta | counter offer |
| contra- | a contraluz | against the light |
| trans-/tras- | traspasar | to pierce/go beyond |
| trans- | translúcido | translucent |
| post- | postraumático | post-traumatic |

## **29.3 Combinations of words**

### **29.3.1 Pairs of nouns**

**Rule:** English freely juxtaposes two nouns (adjective + noun order). This is rare in Spanish. When borrowed from English, the order is often **reversed** (noun + adjective order).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| página web | web page |
| horno microondas | microwave oven |
| videojuego | computer game |
| satélite espía | spy satellite |
| placa madre | motherboard |

### **29.3.2 Nouns linked by prepositions**

**Rule:** The most common Spanish strategy for compound nouns — linking nouns and other parts of speech with a preposition, most commonly **de** (possession/type) or **para** (purpose).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| jugo de piña | pineapple juice |
| ropa de playa | beachwear |
| hoja de cálculo | spreadsheet |
| jarra para cerveza | beer mug/tankard |
| mesa para ordenador [SP] | computer desk |
| expertos en desarme | disarmament experts |

### **29.3.3 Compound nouns**

**Rule:** Formed by combining various parts of speech. **Gender rules:** usually **masculine** if beginning with a verb or preposition (even if the second element is plural): *el abrelatas*. **Feminine** if specifically referring to a female person or ending in a feminine noun not linked to a verb/preposition.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| el cortocircuito | short circuit |
| el limpiabarros | boot scraper/doormat |
| el paraguas | umbrella |
| el apoyacabezas | headrest |
| el pinchadiscos [SP] | disk jockey |
| el sabelotodo | know-all |
| el/la sietemesino/a | premature baby |
| la drogadicción | drug addiction |
| los sinsabores | troubles |

### **29.3.4 Compound adjectives**

**Rule:** A productive group of compound adjectives is formed from **body part + adjective or past participle**. These typically describe a person's physical appearance.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| boquiabierto/a | open-mouthed |
| puntiagudo/a | bony/sharp-pointed |
| paticorto/a | short-legged |
| pelirrojo/a | red-haired |
