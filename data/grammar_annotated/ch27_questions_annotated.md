# Chapter 27: Questions and exclamations

**Two types of questions:**
- **Direct** ('Where is Pedro?') — a complete sentence requiring an answer
- **Indirect** ('Nobody knew where Pedro was') — part of a sentence, no answer required

**Two types of direct questions:**
- Introduced by an **interrogative word** (Table 27.1)
- **Yes/no** questions: *¿Es difícil leerlo?*

**Key differences from English:**
1. Spanish never uses *hacer* as a question auxiliary — no 'do/did' support.
2. Questions are marked by **subject-verb inversion** and inverted question mark **¿** at the start.
3. A preposition can **never** be stranded at the end — it must go immediately before the interrogative word: *¿En qué habitación estás?* (never ~~¿Qué habitación estás en?~~)

**Table 27.1 — Spanish interrogative words (all carry written accents):**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿qué? | what / which? |
| ¿cuál / cuáles? | which one/s? |
| ¿quién / quiénes? | who / whom? |
| ¿de quién? | whose? |
| ¿cuándo? | when? |
| ¿dónde? | where? |
| ¿cómo? | how? |
| ¿cuánto/a/os/as? | how much / how many? |
| ¿por qué? | why? |

## **27.1 Subject-verb inversion**

**Rule:** In both direct and indirect questions the subject is frequently placed **after** the verb. Exception: when the interrogative word itself **is** the subject (or modifies the subject), Spanish — like English — does not invert word order.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ¿Qué disco ha comprado Javier? | What disk has Javier bought? | inversion — subject after verb |
| Nadie sabe dónde está el hospital. | No one knows where the hospital is. | indirect — inversion |
| ¿Quién ha hecho esto? | Who has done this? | no inversion — *quién* is subject |
| ¿Cuánta gente asistió? | How many people attended? | no inversion — *cuánta* modifies subject |

## **27.2 Spanish interrogative words**

### **27.2.1 *Qué* — 'what'**

**Rule:** *Qué* functions as a **pronoun** or **adjective**. As a pronoun it refers only to **things**, never persons. As an adjective there is no restriction. In indirect questions, *lo que* commonly replaces *qué*.

*Notes:*
- *¿Qué tal?* = common conversational opener for opinions/assessments: *¿Qué tal las vacaciones?* 'How was your holiday?'
- For *cuál/es* to translate 'what', see 27.2.2.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Function |
|---|---|---|
| ¿Qué vais a ver en el cine? | What are you going to see at the cinema? | pronoun — thing |
| ¿De qué hablaron Julio y Luis? | What did Julio and Luis talk about? | pronoun — thing |
| Me preguntaron qué hacía. | They asked me what I was doing. | indirect question |
| ¿Qué vestido has comprado al final? | Which dress did you buy in the end? | adjective — no restriction |
| ¿A qué chica te refieres? | What girl are you referring to? | adjective — person |
| No sé lo que/qué voy a comprar. | I don't know what I'm going to buy. | indirect — *lo que* alternative |

### **27.2.2 *Cuál/Cuáles* — 'which one/s', 'what'**

**Rule:** *Cuál/cuáles* is a **pronoun** (not an adjective in standard Peninsular Spanish) referring to persons or things. Agrees in number. Used instead of *qué* with the verb *ser* + abstract noun (unless requesting a definition). In Latin America *cuál* is also used as an adjective (*¿A cuál hora?*) — best avoided in Europe.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cuáles son los que más te gustan? | Which are the ones that you like most? |
| ¿A cuál vamos? | Which one shall we go to? |
| ¿Cuál de los tres hermanos es el más accesible? | Which of the three brothers is the most approachable? |
| ¿Cuál es la razón por la que huyó? | What is the reason for which she fled? |
| ¿Cuáles fueron las causas del descalabro bursátil? | What were the causes of the stockmarket crash? |
| ¿Qué es el estoicismo? | What is stoicism? (definition — use *qué*) |

### **27.2.3 *Quién* — 'who', *de quién* — 'whose'**

**Rule:** *Quién/quiénes* refers **only to persons**. Agrees in number. As a direct object, preceded by the personal **a**. *De quién/quiénes* = 'whose' (direct or indirect question).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Quién vive allí? | Who lives there? |
| ¿A quién has visto? | Who did you see? |
| ¿Quiénes son los dueños del bar? | Who are the owners of the bar? |
| ¿De quiénes son esos asientos? | Whose seats are those? |
| No sé de quién eran los documentos. | I don't know whose the documents were. |

### **27.2.4 *Cuándo*, *dónde* and *cómo***

**Rule:** These are interrogative **adverbs**. A preposition may be required before *cuándo* and *dónde* depending on the verb. *A + dónde* → **adónde**. Note: the interrogative *cuándo* (with accent) does NOT follow the subjunctive rule that applies to the conjunction *cuando* (no accent).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cuándo cerraron el hospital? | When did they close the hospital? |
| ¿Hasta cuándo te quedaste? | Till what time did you stay? |
| ¿Adónde va Juan? | Where is Juan going to? |
| Se desconoce cómo entraron los ladrones. | It isn't known how the thieves entered. |
| ¿Cómo se llama tu hermana? | What is your sister called? |
| Cuando vea a Lola se lo diré. | When I see Lola I'll tell her. (conjunction — no accent, subjunctive) |
| No sé cuándo veré a Lola. | I don't know when I'll see Lola. (interrogative — accent, no subjunctive) |

### **27.2.5 *Cuánto/a/os/as* — 'how much/many'**

**Rule:** *Cuánto* is a quantifying adjective or pronoun — agrees in number and gender with the noun it refers to. The masculine singular form can also be a **neuter pronoun or adverb** meaning 'how much'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cuánta gente había? | How many people were there? |
| No sé cuántos niños tienen. | I don't know how many children they have. |
| ¿Cuántos tienen su propia casa? | How many of them have their own house? |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta una habitación? | How much does a room cost? |

### **27.2.6 *Por qué* and *para qué* — 'why'**

**Rule:** Both translate 'why' but with different nuances. *Por qué* = 'for what **reason/motive**'. *Para qué* = 'to what **end/for what purpose**'. *Por qué* is by far the more common.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| ¿Por qué no me has llamado? | Why didn't you call me? | reason |
| ¿Por qué han cerrado el puente? | Why has the bridge been closed? | reason |
| ¿Para qué necesitas otro perro? | What do you need another dog for? | purpose |

### **27.2.7 Translating 'how' + adjective or adverb**

**Rule:** There is no single neat translation for English 'how + adjective/adverb' in questions. A variety of strategies are required, typically using *cuánto*, *cómo*, *cuántos años*, *con qué frecuencia*, *a qué velocidad*, *de qué + noun*, *cuánto tiempo*.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cuánto mides? | How tall are you? |
| ¿Cómo es tu casa de grande? | How big is your house? |
| ¿Cuántos años tienes? | How old are you? |
| ¿Con qué frecuencia se reúnen? | How frequently do they meet? |
| ¿A qué velocidad ibas? | How fast were you going? |
| ¿De qué gravedad fueron sus lesiones? | How badly was he injured? |
| ¿Cuánto tiempo tardaste en hacerlo? | How long did it take you to do it? |

## **27.3 Question tags**

**Rule:** Spanish has no direct equivalent to English question tags ('isn't it?', 'didn't I?'). Three common alternatives:

| Spanish tag | Meaning | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| **¿verdad?** | 'truth' | general, speaker confident |
| **¿no?** | 'no' | less certainty on speaker's part |
| **¿no es cierto? / ¿no es verdad?** [LA] | 'isn't it true?' | common in Latin America |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hace frío, ¿verdad? | It's cold, isn't it? |
| El frailecillo es una especie de pájaro, ¿no? | The puffin is a kind of bird, isn't it? |
| Prefieres hablar primero, ¿no es cierto? [LA] | You prefer to speak first, don't you? |

## **27.4 Exclamations**

**Rule:** Spanish exclamations have an inverted exclamation mark **¡** at the start and **!** at the end. Exclamatory words always carry a **written accent**.

### **27.4.1 *Qué* in exclamations**

**Rule:** *Qué* + noun = 'what (a)' — **no indefinite article** in Spanish. A qualifying adjective after the noun is preceded by *más* or *tan*. When a noun corresponds to an English adjective, *qué* translates 'so'. With adjectives and adverbs, *qué* translates 'how' or 'so'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¡Qué lío! | What a mess! |
| ¡Qué mala suerte! | What bad luck! |
| ¡Qué viaje más/tan horrible! | What a horrible journey! |
| ¡Qué vergüenza me da! | I'm so ashamed! (noun → 'so') |
| ¡Qué calor hace! | It's so hot! (noun → 'so') |
| ¡Qué tozuda es Lola! | Lola is so stubborn! (adjective → 'how/so') |
| ¡Qué despacio va este autobús! | How slowly this bus is going! (adverb → 'how') |
| ¡Qué de regalos has tenido! | What a lot of presents you've received! (*qué de* = 'what a lot') |

### **27.4.2 *Cómo* in exclamations**

**Rule:** *Cómo* is used only with verbs as an adverb meaning 'how'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¡Cómo trata a sus amigos! | How he treats his friends! |

### **27.4.3 *Cuánto* in exclamations**

**Rule:** *Cuánto* + verb = 'how (much)', 'what a lot' — stresses intensity or extent of the action. As an adjective *cuánto/a/os/as* + noun = 'how much/many', 'what a lot of'. Note: the archaic *cuán* may be encountered in literature before adjectives/adverbs as an alternative to *qué*.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¡Cuánto hemos escrito! | What a lot we've written! |
| ¡Cuánto pesa la maleta! | This suitcase is so heavy! |
| ¡Cuánta gente lo felicitó! | How many people congratulated him! |
| ¡Cuántas cosas ocurren en la ciudad! | What a lot of things happen in the city! |
| ¡Cuán bella es! (literary) | How beautiful she is! |
