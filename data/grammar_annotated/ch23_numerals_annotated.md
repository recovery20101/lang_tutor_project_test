# Chapter 23: Numerals and numerical expressions

Numerals occur in four forms:
- **Cardinals** — count: 'one', 'two', 'three'
- **Ordinals** — sequence: 'first', 'second', 'third'
- **Fractions** — parts: 'half', 'two thirds'
- **Collectives** — groups: 'a couple', 'a dozen'

## **23.1 Cardinal numerals**

**Rule:** Spanish cardinals are invariable as standalone numbers. When quantifying nouns, some adopt different forms (23.1.1–23.1.4).

**Key writing and speaking rules:**
1. Thousands and millions separated by a **full stop** (not comma); decimals by a **comma**: *3,2* = 3.2 (except Mexico, which follows UK/US practice).
2. **Y** 'and' is used **only between tens and units**: *cincuenta y nueve*, but *doscientos setenta y siete*, *mil dos*. (Note: *mil y uno/a* is widely encountered informally.)
3. Telephone numbers are read in **pairs**; odd digits start with hundreds or a single digit.
4. Apart from *uno/a*, numerals are plural: *Son veinte euros* 'It's 20 euros'.
5. Cardinal numbers are masculine nouns: *Escribí un dos en vez de un cuatro* 'I wrote a two instead of a four'.

**Approximate numbers:** Use *unos/as* + cardinal, or *aproximadamente*, *alrededor de*, *más o menos*, *cerca de*, *cosa de*, *como*:
*Está a unos cien kilómetros de la costa* 'It's about a hundred kilometres from the coast.'

### **23.1.1 *Uno***

**Rule:**
- **(a) Gender agreement:** *uno* agrees with a following noun: *una manzana*, *treinta y una páginas*. Exception: before *mil*, feminine *una* is not widely used: *cincuenta y un mil personas*.
- **(b) Masculine shortening:** masculine becomes **un** before a masculine noun: *un diccionario*, *sesenta y un libros*. Written accent required on *veintiún*: *veintiún años*.
- **(c) No article before *ciento* and *mil*:** *cien/mil libros* 'a hundred/a thousand books'. Exception when needed for clarity: *doscientos un mil cuarenta* '201,040'.
- The feminine *una* is NOT shortened before feminine nouns, except before stressed *a-* or *ha-*: *cuarenta y un águilas*.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| una manzana | one apple |
| treinta y una páginas | thirty-one pages |
| un diccionario | one dictionary |
| sesenta y un libros | sixty-one books |
| veintiún años | twenty-one years |
| cuarenta y un águilas | forty-one eagles |
| cincuenta y un mil personas | 51,000 persons |
| cien/mil libros | a hundred/a thousand books |

### **23.1.2 *Ciento***

**Rule:** *Ciento* is shortened to **cien** before any noun (including a preceding adjective) and before *mil*, but **not** before numbers less than 100. The shortened form is also used when the following noun is understood from context.

**Notes:**
- **Percentages:** *ciento* retains its full form: *El treinta por ciento son hindúes*.
- **Multiples of hundreds cannot express thousands:** *mil quinientos* = 'fifteen hundred' (not ~~quince cientos~~).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| cien pesados kilómetros | a hundred boring kilometres |
| cien mil habitantes | a hundred thousand inhabitants |
| ciento veinte libras | a hundred and twenty pounds |
| Le di diez pesos y me quedan cien. | I gave him ten pesos and I have a hundred left. |
| El treinta por ciento son hindúes. | Thirty per cent are Hindus. |

### **23.1.3 *Doscientos*, *trescientos* etc.**

**Rule:** The compounds formed with **-cientos** and *quinientos* (500) **agree in gender** with what is being counted.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| doscientas mujeres | two hundred women |
| quinientas plazas | five hundred places |

### **23.1.4 *Mil*, *millón*, *billón***

**Rule:**
- **Millón** and **billón** are nouns — followed by **de** before what is counted.
- **Mil** as an adjective is **invariable**; as a noun it is pluralised (*miles de*).
- Note: Spanish *un billón* = one **trillion** (10¹²), not one billion.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| un millón de dólares | one million dollars |
| varios billones de pesos | several trillion pesos |
| Mil personas la saludaron. | A thousand people greeted her. |
| Miles de personas la saludaron. | Thousands of people greeted her. |

### **23.1.5 Uses of cardinal numbers**

#### 23.1.5.1 Time of day

**Rule:** Hours expressed with *ser* + *la/las* + cardinal number. Singular *ser* for one o'clock, plural for all others. Minutes past the hour: **y** + cardinal (use *minuto(s)* only for less than 5 minutes; *cuarto* = quarter past, *media* = half past). Minutes to the hour: **menos** + cardinal or *cuarto*. Alternatives: *faltar* + *para* or *ser* + *para*. For exact digital time: *las seis cuarenta y tres* (6.43). *Horas* included when quoting full 24-hour times from 13 onwards.

**Divisions of the day (with *de* when a specific time is named; *por* otherwise):**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| de la madrugada | in the early morning (until sunrise) |
| de la mañana | in the morning |
| de la tarde | in the afternoon/evening |
| de la noche | at night |

*When no specific time:* *Fueron **por** la tarde* 'they went in the afternoon' → but *Fueron a las dos **de** la tarde* 'They went at two o'clock in the afternoon'. Combinations: *ayer por la tarde* 'yesterday afternoon', *mañana por la mañana* 'tomorrow morning'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué hora es? | What time is it? |
| Son las tres (en punto). | It is three o'clock (exactly). |
| Era la una. | It was one o'clock. |
| las siete y veinte | twenty past seven |
| la una y trece minutos | thirteen minutes past one |
| las diez y cuarto | quarter past ten |
| las once y media | half past eleven |
| la una menos veinticinco | twenty-five to one |
| las cuatro menos cuarto | quarter to four |
| Faltan tres minutos para las cinco. | It's three minutes to five. |
| Son diez para las dos. | It's ten to two. |
| las veinte cuarenta y ocho | 20.48 / 8.48 p.m. |
| las catorce horas | 14.00 / 2.00 p.m. |
| Eran las doce y pico. | It was just after twelve. |
| Están dando las dos. | It's striking two o'clock. |

#### 23.1.5.2 Dates

**Rule:** **Cardinal numbers** are always used for days of the month, except that **primero** frequently replaces *el (día) uno* for 'the first'. Full date format: **el** + cardinal + **de** + month + **de** + year. At the head of a letter, *el* is omitted. Spanish word order is fixed — English alternatives ('28th March', 'March 28th') are not used. Decades: *los (años) sesenta* 'the sixties'. Years are always read as full numbers.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué fecha era? El (día) siete. | What was the date? The seventh. |
| el primero de abril | the first of April |
| el 15 de agosto de 1534 | 15th August 1534 |
| los (años) sesenta | the sixties |
| mil cuatrocientos noventa y dos | 1492 (always read as full number) |

#### 23.1.5.3 Basic arithmetical calculations

**Rule:** *Ser* is used in the **plural** to mean 'equals'. The division symbol in Spanish is **:**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Cinco y/más seis son once. | 5 + 6 = 11 |
| Veinte menos trece son siete. | 20 − 13 = 7 |
| Diez (multiplicado) por cuatro son cuarenta. | 10 × 4 = 40 |
| Cien dividido por veinte son cinco. | 100 ÷ 20 = 5 |

## **23.2 Ordinal numbers**

**Rule:** Ordinals are **adjectives agreeing in number and gender** with the noun they modify. *Primero* and *tercero* drop **-o** before a masculine singular noun. Ordinals usually **precede** the noun, except in titles (*Carlos V*).

**Ordinal forms:**

| Spanish | Abbrev. | English |
|---|---|---|
| primer(o)/a | 1.º/1.ª | 1st |
| segundo/a | 2.º/2.ª | 2nd |
| tercer(o)/a | 3.º/3.ª | 3rd |
| cuarto/a | 4.º/4.ª | 4th |
| quinto/a | 5.º/5.ª | 5th |
| sexto/a | 6.º/6.ª | 6th |
| sé(p)timo/a | 7.º/7.ª | 7th |
| octavo/a | 8.º/8.ª | 8th |
| noveno/a | 9.º/9.ª | 9th |
| décimo/a | 10.º/10.ª | 10th |
| undécimo/a | 11.º/11.ª | 11th |
| duodécimo/a | 12.º/12.ª | 12th |
| décimo tercer(o)/a | 13.º/13.ª | 13th |
| vigésimo/a | 20.º/20.ª | 20th |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| la segunda pregunta | the second question |
| el tercer punto | the third point |
| Carlos V (quinto) | Charles V |

### **23.2.1 Use of ordinals**

**Rule:** Beyond *décimo* (10th), it is common practice to replace ordinals with a **cardinal number** in all but extremely formal/technical language. Beyond 'tenth', only *centésimo* (100th) and *milésimo* (1000th) are regularly used. Exception: formal references to **anniversaries** regularly use ordinals.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| el siglo XX (veinte) | the twentieth century |
| el capítulo quince | the fifteenth chapter |
| el vigésimo aniversario | the twentieth anniversary |

## **23.3 Fractions**

### **23.3.1 1/2 to 1/10**

**Rule:** From 1/4 to 1/10, fractions use the same forms as ordinals. Special forms: *un medio* = 1/2, *un tercio* = 1/3. In everyday use, fractions beyond 1/2 are often expressed by the **feminine ordinal + *parte/s***: *la tercera parte* (a third), *siete décimas* (seven tenths).

**Special uses of *medio*:**
- **Noun:** 'middle/centre', 'means' — *en medio del cuarto*, *los medios necesarios*
- **Adjective:** 'half', 'average' — *media luna*, *la temperatura media*
- **Adverb:** 'half' — *Ella está medio dormida*; in Latin America also 'rather': *Ella es medio linda* [LA]

**La mitad** = 'half' in non-arithmetical contexts: *Le regaló la mitad de sus libros*.

### **23.3.2 Fractions from 1/11 onwards**

**Rule:** In technical language, fractions from 1/11 onwards are formed by adding **-avo** to cardinal numbers (or **-vo** if the cardinal ends in **-a**): *un catorceavo* (1/14), *nueve dieciseisavos* (9/16), *un sesentavo* (1/60). Special: *centavo/centésimo* (1/100), *milésimo* (1/1000). In Latin America (rarely in Spain), *-avo* forms are also used as ordinals: *el doceavo tomo* [LA].

## **23.4 Collective and multiple numerals**

### **23.4.1 Collective numerals**

**Rule:** Collective numerals are nouns specifying groups. Most express approximate rather than exact numbers, except *par*, *docena* and *quincena*.

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| un par | a pair / a couple | exact |
| una docena | a dozen | exact |
| una quincena | a fortnight, two weeks | exact; also 'fifteen' |
| una decena | ten | approximate |
| una veintena | twenty, a score | approximate |
| una treintena | thirty | approximate |
| una cuarentena | forty | approximate; also 'quarantine' |
| un centenar / una centena | a hundred | approximate |
| un millar | a thousand | approximate |

*Note: **ciento** is sometimes used as an alternative to **centenar** in quantities of money or rate expressions: **500 pesos el ciento** [LA], **centenares de delfines**.*

### **23.4.2 Multiple births and multiples**

**Rule:** Spanish has no exact equivalent of English '-fold'. Only *múltiple*, *doble* and *triple* are regularly used. For higher multiples, use **cardinal number + *veces*** 'times'.

**Multiple births:** *mellizos/gemelos* (twins), *trillizos* (triplets), *cuatrillizos* (quadruplets), *quintillizos* (quintuplets), *sextillizos* (sextuplets), *septillizos* (septuplets).

**Multiple expressions:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| múltiple | multiple |
| doble | double |
| triple | triple |
| un examen de elección múltiple | a multiple-choice examination |
| aparcar en doble fila | to double park |
| el triple salto | the triple jump |
| La población es diez veces más que la de Inglaterra. | The population is tenfold that of England. |
