# Chapter 7: Adverbs

Adverbs qualify verbs, adjectives (including past participles) and other adverbs. They fall into four broad categories: **manner**, **place**, **time**, and **degree/quantity**. Additionally, **sentence adverbs** (*afortunadamente*, *necesariamente*) apply to the whole sentence rather than qualifying a single item.

**Adverbs are invariable** — they have no masculine/feminine or singular/plural forms.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se movían lentamente. | They moved slowly. |
| Es sumamente inepto. | He is completely inept. |
| Lo explicó admirablemente bien. | He explained it admirably well. |

As regards form, Spanish adverbs divide into those based on an adjective + **-mente** (see 7.1), and the remainder with diverse forms (see 7.2).

## **7.1 Adverbs ending in *-mente***

**Rule:** Formed by attaching **-mente** to the **feminine form** of the corresponding adjective or past participle (or to the invariable form if the adjective has no distinct feminine). Corresponds to English '-ly'. Mostly adverbs of manner, but not exclusively.

The **-mente** suffix can also be added to absolute superlative forms of adjectives (see 26.6.3).

**Examples:**

| Adjective | Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| lenta + mente | lentamente | slowly |
| decidida + mente | decididamente | resolutely |
| feliz + mente | felizmente | happily |
| clarísima + mente | clarísimamente | extremely clearly |

**Not all -mente adverbs are adverbs of manner:**

| Spanish | English | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Hablaron solamente dos personas. | Only two people spoke. | quantity adverb |
| Posteriormente se casaron. | They got married later. | time adverb |
| Políticamente ha sido un desastre. | Politically it's been a disaster. | sentence adverb |

### **7.1.1 Stress and written accents on -mente adverbs**

**Rule:** Both the **-mente** suffix and the base adjective are stressed independently. If the adjective is normally written with an accent, the accent is retained in the adverb form.

**Examples:**

| Adjective | Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| fácil | fácilmente | easily |
| rápido | rápidamente | quickly |
| último | últimamente | lately |

### **7.1.2 Two or more -mente adverbs in succession**

**Rule:** In written Spanish and formal spoken Spanish, when two or more **-mente** adverbs are joined by **y**, **pero**, **o** or **ni**, the **-mente** suffix is **omitted from all but the last** adverb in the sequence.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hablaron lenta y gravemente. | They spoke slowly and gravely. |
| Lo explicó clara y concisamente. | He explained it clearly and concisely. |

## **7.2 Adverbs not ending in *-mente***

### **7.2.1 Adverbs of manner**

**Rule:** These include items such as **bien** 'well', **mal** 'badly', **despacio** 'slowly', **deprisa** 'quickly', **así** 'like this/that', **mejor** 'better', **peor** 'worse'. They describe *how* an action is carried out.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No se puede entrar vestida así. | You can't go in dressed like that. |
| Mario nada despacio y mal. | Mario swims slowly and badly. |

### **7.2.2 Adverbs of place**

**Rule:** State *where* — either **positional** (static location) or **directional** (movement). Several pairs make a positional/directional distinction. Adverbs of place can be preceded by prepositions (especially **por**) or follow a noun to form adverbial phrases.

**Key distinctions:**

| Adverb | Use | Spanish example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **aquí** | here (near speaker) | *este aquí* | this one here |
| **ahí** | there (near listener) | *ese ahí* | that one near you |
| **allí** | there (far from both) | *aquel allí* | that one over there |
| **acá** | here (vague / motion) [esp. LA] | *Ven acá* | Come here |
| **allá** | there (vague / motion) [esp. LA] | *más allá* | further away |
| **dentro / adentro** | inside (adentro = [LA] or motion) | *Tu padre está fuera* | Your father is outside |
| **fuera / afuera** | outside (afuera = [LA] or motion) | *¡Ven adentro!* | Come inside! |
| **detrás** | behind (position) | *¿Has mirado detrás?* | Have you looked behind? |
| **atrás** | behind / backwards (direction) | *Dio un paso atrás.* | He took a step backwards. |
| **delante** | in front (position) | *El general marchaba delante.* | The general marched in front. |
| **adelante** | forward / onwards (direction) | *Decidió seguir adelante.* | He decided to continue onwards. |

**Preposition + adverb of place:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Saltó por encima. | He jumped on top. |
| Se arrastró por debajo. | He crawled underneath. |
| hacia arriba/abajo | upwards/downwards |

**Adverb of place after a noun:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| cuesta arriba/abajo | uphill/downhill |
| río arriba/abajo | upstream/downstream |
| tierra adentro | inland |

*For adverbs of place with **de** forming compound prepositions (e.g. **delante de**), see Chapter 21.*

### **7.2.3 Adverbs of time**

**Rule:** State *when*. Several individual adverbs require special attention.

**Key notes on individual time adverbs:**

**(a) Ya** — 'already'; with a negative word: 'no longer'. Also has rhetorical/emphatic uses expressing irritation or impatience.

| Spanish | English | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ya no vive aquí. | He doesn't live here any more. | ya + negative = no longer |
| Nadie quiere estudiar ya. | No one wants to study any more. | ya + negative = no longer |
| ¡Cállate ya! | Be quiet! | emphatic irritation |
| ¡Ya era hora! | It's about time! | emphatic |
| ¡Ya empezamos! | Here we go again! | ironic frustration |

**(b) Entonces vs luego** — both can mean 'then' but differ:
- **entonces**: 'at that moment', 'at that time'
- **luego**: 'next', 'afterwards', 'later'

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Desde entonces no lo he vuelto a ver. | Since then I haven't seen him again. |
| Luego se mudaron a Córdoba. | They later moved to Córdoba. |

**(c) Recién** [SP] — used **exclusively before a past participle** in Peninsular Spanish, meaning 'newly', 'recently'. *For Latin American usage, see 30.6.3.9.*

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| recién casado | newly wed |
| recién llegado | newly arrived |

### **7.2.4 Adverbs of degree or quantity**

**Rule:** Include indefinite adjectives used as adverbs (**mucho**, **poco**, **demasiado**, **bastante** — see Chapter 9), comparative words (**más**, **menos**, **tan/tanto** — see Chapter 26), and the following:

| Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| muy | very |
| casi | almost |
| apenas | scarcely, hardly |

*Note: **Apenas si** ('scarcely/barely') is found in literary Spanish: *Apenas si se reconocieron* 'They barely recognised one another'.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Apenas come. | He hardly eats anything. |
| Casi hemos terminado. | We've almost finished. |

### **7.2.5 Adverbs meaning 'even'**

**Rule:** This category falls outside the four main types. **Hasta** or **incluso** are most frequently used for 'even'. In negative sentences, use **ni** or **ni siquiera** (see 24.2.3). With comparatives, use **aún** or **todavía**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Hasta/incluso los abuelos bailaban. | Even the grandparents danced. | affirmative — hasta/incluso |
| Ni siquiera me llamó. | He didn't even call me. | negative — ni siquiera |
| Es aún más difícil con un bebé en los brazos. | It's even more difficult with a baby in your arms. | with comparative — aún |

## **7.3 Adverbial phrases**

**Rule:** Fixed phrases with an adverbial function. An adverb ending in **-mente** can also be replaced by **con** + abstract noun, or by **de (una) manera / de (un) modo / de forma / de carácter** + adjective.

**Common adverbial phrases:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| a ciegas | blindly / in the dark |
| a escondidas | in secret |
| a mano | by / at hand |
| a menudo | often |
| a pie | on foot |
| a propósito | on purpose |
| a veces | sometimes |
| de memoria | from memory |
| de noche | at night |
| de repente | suddenly / perhaps [LA] |
| en el acto | on the spot |
| en serio | seriously |

**Alternatives to -mente adverbs:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Visita a sus padres con frecuencia. | He visits his parents frequently. |
| Prefiero hablar de (un) modo menos formal. | I prefer to speak less formally. |
| Esto me interesa de forma especial. | This is especially interesting to me. |

## **7.4 Adjectives used as adverbs**

**Rule:** The **masculine singular** form of certain adjectives may be used as adverbs of manner. This construction is invariable.

*For an adjective in agreement with the subject or object of a verb (where English would use an adverb), see 6.6.*

**Common adjectives used as adverbs:**

| Adjective | Adverbial meaning |
|---|---|
| barato | cheap |
| caro | dear |
| claro | clear |
| directo | direct |
| duro | hard |
| fatal | terrible |
| fuerte | strong, hard |
| hondo | deep |
| lento | slow |
| limpio | clean |
| rápido | quick |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Puedes ir un poco más rápido? | Can you go a bit faster? |
| Antonio canta fatal. | Antonio sings terribly. |
| Me agarró fuerte. | He grabbed me roughly. |
