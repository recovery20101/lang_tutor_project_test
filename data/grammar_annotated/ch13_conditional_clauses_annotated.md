# Chapter 13: Conditional clauses

A conditional clause refers to a hypothetical event on which something else depends. Typically introduced by **si** 'if'.

**Core rule:** The mood in the *si*-clause depends on the type of condition:
- **Closed condition** (contrary to fact or unlikely) → **subjunctive**
- **Open condition** (genuine possibility) → **indicative**

The only subjunctive tenses permitted in a *si*-clause are the **imperfect** and **pluperfect**. A present or perfect subjunctive after *si* is almost always incorrect.

## **13.1 Use of the subjunctive after *si***

### **13.1.1 Pluperfect subjunctive — condition not fulfilled in the past**

**Rule:** Used when the condition was **not** fulfilled in the past ('if I had been rich, but I wasn't'). The main clause takes the **conditional** or **conditional perfect**. Note: *-ra* form of the pluperfect subjunctive may also be used in the main clause instead of the conditional perfect.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Si no hubieras dejado la ventana abierta, la habitación no estaría llena de mosquitos. | If you hadn't left the window open, the room wouldn't be full of mosquitoes. |
| Si nos hubiera consultado, habría sido posible discutirlo con él. | If he had consulted us, it would have been possible to discuss it with him. |
| Si hubieran tenido más cuidado no hubiera pasado lo que pasó. | If they had been more careful what did happen wouldn't have happened. |

### **13.1.2 Imperfect subjunctive — condition not fulfilled in the present or improbable future**

#### 13.1.2.1 Condition not fulfilled in present time

**Rule:** Used when the condition is **not fulfilled in present time** ('if I had money, but I haven't'). The main clause takes the **conditional** tense. In speech, the conditional in the main clause is often replaced by the **imperfect** indicative.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Estaría más a gusto si hiciera mejor tiempo. | I would be happier if the weather was better. |
| Si la casa fuera más grande podríamos tener invitados. | If the house were bigger we could have friends to stay. |
| Pues yo me iba (si pudiera). | Well I would go (if I could). [speech — imperfect instead of conditional] |

#### 13.1.2.2 Future events considered unlikely or improbable

**Rule:** The imperfect subjunctive is also used for future events considered **unlikely or improbable** (though not strictly contrary to fact). If English uses 'were to' (or could insert it), this signals a closed condition requiring the imperfect subjunctive.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué harías si te ofrecieran el puesto? | What would you do if they offered you the job? |
| Si ofrecieran más, ¿venderías? | If they were to offer more, would you sell? |

### CMP: Closed vs open *si*-conditions — full reference

**Rule:** Summary of all tense patterns for *si*-clauses and their main clauses.

| Condition type | si-clause | Main clause | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unfulfilled past | pluperfect subjunctive | conditional or conditional perfect | *Si hubiera llegado…, habría visto…* |
| Unfulfilled present | imperfect subjunctive | conditional | *Si tuviera dinero, lo compraría.* |
| Improbable future | imperfect subjunctive | conditional | *Si viniera, estaría contento.* |
| Open / possible | indicative (any tense) | indicative or future | *Si llegas temprano, iremos juntos.* |
| 'as if' (como si) | imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive | — | *Gasta como si fuera millonario.* |

**Key constraints:**
1. The **only** subjunctive tenses in the *si*-clause are imperfect and pluperfect.
2. The **-ra** form of the imperfect subjunctive is more common than **-se**.
3. Present or perfect subjunctive after *si* is almost always incorrect. Rare exception [LA]: *No sé si sea cierto* (formal, after *no saber*).
4. Future and conditional after *si* only when *si* means 'whether': *No sé si iré al trabajo*.
5. English conditional without 'if' → Spanish still uses *si*: *Had he behaved properly* → *Si se hubiera comportado apropiadamente*.

## **13.2 Indicative tenses after *si***

**Rule:** All *si*-clauses that are **not** closed conditions use the indicative. This includes habitual conditions, open future conditions, and reported speech where the original was an open condition.

**Future and conditional after *si***: only when *si* means 'whether': *No sé si iré al trabajo* / *No sabía si ella vendría*.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Si hablo mucho, me canso. | If I speak a lot, I get tired. |
| Si llegas temprano, iremos juntos. | If you arrive early, we shall go together. |
| Si se reían, no estaban enfadados. | If they were laughing, they weren't angry. |
| Me afirmó que me ayudaría si era posible. | He stated he would help me if it was possible. |
| Prometió que lo devolvería si todavía lo tenía. | He promised he would return it if he still had it. |
| No sé si iré al trabajo. | I don't know if/whether I'll go to work. |
| No sabía si ella vendría. | I didn't know if/whether she would come. |
