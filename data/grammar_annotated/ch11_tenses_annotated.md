# Chapter 11: Uses of tenses

The tenses in Spanish can be divided into three categories as follows:

| | |
|---|---|
| Simple tenses | Present, preterite, imperfect, future, conditional |
| Compound tenses | Perfect, pluperfect, future perfect, conditional perfect, past anterior |
| Progressive/continuous tenses | Present progressive, imperfect progressive, preterite progressive, future progressive, progressives of compound tenses |

## **11.1 Simple tenses**

### **11.1.1 Present tense**

**Rule:** The Spanish present tense corresponds to English 'I run' etc., but does not exclusively denote present time.

#### 11.1.1.1 Present tense - Habitual sentences

**Rule:** The present tense is used to express an action that happens habitually or is generally considered to be true.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Andrés vende coches. | Andrés sells cars. |
| El sol se pone por el oeste. | The sun sets in the west. |

#### 11.1.1.2 Present tense - State sentences

**Rule:** The present tense describes a state or condition which exists at the present moment.

**Examples:**

|---|---|
| Luis quiere a Andrea. | Luis loves Andrea. |

#### 11.1.1.3 Present tense - With reference to future time

**Rule:** The present tense is also commonly used to refer to imminent events or pre-arranged future intentions. It is frequently used in conditional sentences that confirm an agreed arrangement, or in questions asking for advice or approval for some imminent action.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Venga, cenamos aquí. | OK, we'll have dinner here. |
| Le pago mañana. | I'll pay you tomorrow. |
| ¿Qué hago si no está? | What shall I do if he isn't there? |
| ¿Compro pan? | Shall I buy some bread? |

#### 11.1.1.4 Present tense - With reference to past time

**Rule:** The present tense can also be used to refer to the past to make past events more vivid.

**Examples:**

### **11.1.2 The preterite tense**

**Rule:** The Spanish preterite corresponds to the simple past in English: 'I ran' etc. The preterite talks about events that took place in the past and are now complete, or states of affairs that existed in the past but have now ended.

#### 11.1.2.1 Preterite tense - Completed actions or events

**Rule:** The preterite describes actions that singly or in sequence were completed in the past.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me levanté, me lavé y salí. | I got up, washed and went out. |
| Construyeron la urbanización en cuatro meses. | They built the housing complex in four months. |
| Nicolás me llamó cinco veces anoche. | Nicolás called me five times last night. |

#### 11.1.2.2 Preterite tense - With states that have come to an end

**Rule:** The preterite also refers to states of affairs that lasted during specified (possibly very long) time limits in the past and have now ended. Often, but by no means always, the prepositions **durante** 'for' or **hasta** 'until' signal this usage.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Jugaron durante media hora. | They played for half an hour. |
| Felipe González fue presidente del gobierno desde 1982 hasta 1991. | Felipe González was president of the government from 1982 to 1991. |
| Lo negó desde el principio. | He denied it from the start. |

### **11.1.3 The imperfect tense**

**Rule:** Characteristically the imperfect tense deals with actions or states of affairs that were in progress in the past, or were habitual or customary in the past. The English phrases 'was happening' or 'used to happen' suggest the use of the imperfect tense in Spanish.

#### 11.1.3.1 Imperfect tense - To express actions in progress

**Rule:** The imperfect tense is used to talk about events or actions that **were** taking place at some time in the past. The beginning and end of these occurrences is unspecified. A typical narrative strategy involves using the imperfect to describe the setting for an event that is narrated using the preterite.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Alberto y Juan se reían a carcajadas. | Alberto and Juan were roaring with laughter. |
| Me caí mientras bajaba la escalera. | I fell while I was going down the stairs. |

##### 11.1.3.1.1 Note on imperfect vs progressive

**Rule:** In this usage, the imperfect has a similar meaning to the imperfect progressive (see section 11.3.2). The progressive is preferred to emphasize being "in the middle" of an activity.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No pude contestar al teléfono porque estaba dando de comer al bebé. | I couldn't answer the telephone because I was feeding the baby. |

#### 11.1.3.3 Imperfect tense - To describe habitual actions

**Rule:** The imperfect tense expresses actions or events that were repeated habitually, events to which the English phrase **used to** may be applicable. Habitual actions are sometimes signalled as such by phrases like **a menudo** 'frequently', **de vez en cuando** 'from time to time', **siempre** 'always', **cada día** 'every day', **todas las semanas** 'every week'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Antes Lole trabajaba en un hospital. | Lole used to work in a hospital. |
| Íbamos todos los jueves al cine. | We used to go to the cinema every Thursday. |
| Cenábamos fuera a menudo. | We frequently dined out. |

##### 11.1.3.3.1 Note on 'would' and 'never'

**Rule:** The Spanish imperfect can correspond to the English habitual 'would' (actions that used to happen). The word **nunca** (never) requires the imperfect when describing a habitual state in the past, but requires the preterite when denying that a specific event ever took place.

**Examples:**

| Context | Spanish | English | Tense used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Habitual | Cuando era niño, nunca comía verduras. | When I was a child, I never used to eat vegetables. | Imperfect |
| Specific denial | Ayer nunca llegó el paquete. | Yesterday the package never arrived. | Preterite |
| Habitual 'would' | En verano íbamos a la playa. | In summer we would go to the beach. | Imperfect |

### 11.1.4 CMP: Preterite vs Imperfect

**Rule:** Spanish uses the preterite for completed actions and the imperfect for ongoing states or descriptions, whereas English often uses the same form for both. The preterite denotes that something happened and concluded; the imperfect describes how things were or what was happening.

---

#### 11.1.4.1 Preterite vs Imperfect - With *ser* and *estar*

**Rule:** The preterite refers to states that have ended or distinct periods of time. The imperfect describes a state that simply existed at some point in the past.

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Adriana estuvo enferma la semana pasada. | Adriana was ill last week. | preterite — completed, bounded episode |
| Cuando fui a su casa Adriana estaba enferma. | When I went to her house Adriana was ill. | imperfect — ongoing background state |
| Sánchez fue presidente tres veces. | Sánchez was president three times. | preterite — countable, completed terms |
| Sánchez era presidente en aquella época. | Sánchez was president at the time. | imperfect — state at unspecified past moment |

---

#### 11.1.4.2 Preterite vs Imperfect - Preterite = action, imperfect = state

**Rule:** The preterite denotes that an action took place and was completed. The imperfect describes a condition or state without implying completion.

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Fue fácil hacerlo. | It was easily done (and it was done). | preterite — action happened and concluded |
| Era fácil hacerlo. | It was an easy thing to do. | imperfect — general description |
| Tuve que pagar la cena yo. | I had to pay for the dinner (and I did). | preterite — obligation fulfilled |
| El niño tenía que acostarse a las ocho. | The boy's bedtime was eight o'clock. | imperfect — standing rule, no specific event |

---

#### 11.1.4.3 Preterite vs Imperfect - Psychological verbs

**Rule:** With psychological verbs the preterite indicates an act (recognition, learning, understanding) while the imperfect indicates a state of mind. Note: **saber** and **conocer** have different English translations in each tense.

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Después de hablar con él supe hacerlo. | After speaking to him I understood how to do it. | preterite of saber = to find out |
| Sabía cómo hacerlo antes de hablar con él. | I knew how to do it before talking to him. | imperfect of saber = to know (state) |
| Conocí a Susana en la fiesta. | I met Susana at the party. | preterite of conocer = to meet for the first time |
| Ya conocía a Ana antes de ir a la fiesta. | I knew Ana before going to the party. | imperfect of conocer = to know someone |

#### 11.1.6.2 Conditional tense - Supposition about the past

**Rule:** The conditional tense is used to indicate possibility, probability, or supposition about **past** states (equivalent to "must have been"). In journalism and media, it is also used to express rumours or unconfirmed facts.

| El huracán se localizaría ahora en el sur del golfo. | The hurricane now appears to be in the southern gulf. |
| El objetivo sería eliminar las dudas. | The aim seems to be to remove the doubts. |

## **11.2 Compound tenses**

As in English 'I have run', the compound tenses in Spanish consist of an auxiliary verb (**haber**) + past participle.

**General rules for all compound tenses:**

- A past participle **never** agrees with the subject or object of the auxiliary verb **haber**: *María y Andrea se han ido*, *No la he visto nunca*.
- No words may be inserted between **haber** and a past participle, except object pronouns when **haber** occurs as an infinitive or gerund: *después de haberlo dicho*, *habiéndoles escrito*.
- English 'have/had just done' is expressed using **acabar de** + infinitive: *Alberto acaba/acababa de llegar* 'Alberto has/had just arrived'.
- With transitive verbs, **haber** can be replaced by **tener** to emphasize the result of the action — the past participle then agrees with the direct object: *Tengo corregidos los ensayos* 'I've got the essays marked'.

### **11.2.5 The past anterior**

**Rule:** The past anterior is now confined to literature and extremely rare in speech. When used, it refers to an action that had occurred before another past action. It is found primarily after temporal conjunctions (**después de que**, **cuando**, **luego que**, **apenas**) when the main verb is in the preterite. In modern Spanish it is replaced by the preterite or pluperfect.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Después de que hubo salido, empezaron a criticarlo. | When he had left, they began to criticize him. |
| Apenas la hube visto, se fue. | Scarcely had I seen her when she left. |
| En cuanto vino, comenzó la reunión. | As soon as she had arrived, the meeting began. (modern replacement) |

## **11.3 Progressive or continuous tenses**

**Rule:** Like their English counterparts, the Spanish progressive tenses are used primarily with action verbs to indicate that an action is taking place. However, there are two key differences from English usage:

1. Spanish progressive tenses are **not** used to refer to future time: *El sábado llega Angela* (not ~~está llegando~~) 'Angela is coming on Saturday'.
2. Spanish progressive tenses are **not** used with **ir** (except for recurrent events), **llevar** 'to wear', or with verbs of physical posture: *¿Adónde va Luis?* (not ~~está yendo~~), *Ana llevaba puesta una camisa* (not ~~estaba llevando~~), *Estaban sentados en el sofá* (not ~~estaban sentando~~).

#### 11.5.2 Verbs of needing, lacking and remaining

**Rule:** Verbs expressing need, lack, or remainder follow the same structure as **gustar**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Nos hace falta consultar el mapa. | We need to look at the map. |
| Me faltan dos etiquetas. | I am short of two labels. |
| Me quedan dos. | I have two left. |
| Nos sobra comida. | We have more than enough food. |

#### 11.5.3 Verbs of interest, appreciation and impersonal expressions

**Rule:** Verbs expressing interest or appreciation, and certain impersonal verbs where the English subject is 'it', follow the same structure as **gustar**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me apetece una copa de cava. | I fancy a glass of cava. |
| ¿Qué te parece Carlota? | What do you think of Carlota? |
| Me tocó pagar. | It was my turn to pay. |
| No te conviene hacer eso. | It isn't in your interests to do that. |
