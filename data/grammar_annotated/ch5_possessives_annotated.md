# Chapter 5: Possessive adjectives

Possessive adjectives indicate a relationship of possession or association between a person and a thing. Each corresponds to a subject pronoun: English 'my' → 'I', 'your' → 'you'. Spanish has two sets: **weak** (before the noun) and **strong** (after the noun).

## **5.1 Possessive adjectives before the noun (weak forms)**

**Rule:** Weak possessive adjectives are placed before the noun. Another adjective may come between them and the noun. All agree in **number** with the noun possessed (not with the possessor), but only **nuestro** and **vuestro** [SP] have distinct **feminine** forms.

*Note: Unlike English, the Spanish possessive adjective is repeated before each noun in a list — except when the nouns refer to the same person/thing or form parts of one overall concept: **Tu primo y tu tío vivieron en Jaén** (repeated), but **mi colega y amigo** (shared reference).*

**Weak possessive adjective forms:**

| Subject pronoun | Possessive adjective | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| yo | mi / mis | my |
| tú, vos [LA] | tu / tus | your |
| él / ella / usted | su / sus | his / her / its / your |
| nosotros/-as | nuestro/-os / nuestra/-as | our |
| vosotros/-as [SP] | vuestro/-os / vuestra/-as [SP] | your |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | su / sus | their / your |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| mis hijas | my daughters |
| nuestra maleta | our suitcase |
| ¿Cantás (vos) tu propia canción? | Are you singing your own song? |

## **5.2 Possessive adjectives after the noun (strong forms)**

**Rule:** Strong possessive adjectives are placed **after** the noun. They agree in **both number and gender** with the noun possessed. Used after a noun they mean 'of mine', 'of yours' etc. After **ser** the indefinite article may be omitted unless the noun is qualified. Without a noun (especially after *ser*) they mean 'mine', 'yours' etc.

**Strong possessive adjective forms:**

| Subject pronoun | Possessive adjective | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| yo | mío / míos / mía / mías | mine |
| tú, vos [LA] | tuyo / tuyos / tuya / tuyas | yours |
| él / ella / usted | suyo / suyos / suya / suyas | his / hers / yours |
| nosotros/-as | nuestro / nuestros / nuestra / nuestras | ours |
| vosotros/-as [SP] | vuestro / vuestros / vuestra / vuestras [SP] | yours |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | suyo / suyos / suya / suyas | theirs / yours |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| unos libros míos | some books of mine |
| aquella propuesta tuya | that proposal of yours |
| Es amigo nuestro. | He's a friend of ours. |
| Es un amigo nuestro que no conoces. | He's a friend of ours you don't know. |
| Ese bolso es mío. | That bag is mine. |
| Vamos a abrirlos — son nuestros. | Let's open them — they're ours. |

*Note: Strong possessives are used in formal correspondence (*Muy señor mío* 'Dear Sir') and set phrases: *a costa mía* 'to my cost', *por culpa suya* 'because of him', *al lado mío* 'by my side'.*

## **5.3 Definite and neuter articles with possessives**

### **5.3.1 Definite article + strong possessive as pronoun**

**Rule:** Strong possessive adjectives function as **pronouns** when preceded by **el/la/los/las**. The article and possessive agree in number and gender with the implied noun. Key distinction: **mío** (after *ser*, no prior reference assumed) vs **el mío** (identifies something previously mentioned as belonging to the person).

- Only forms **with** the article can be subject/object of a verb or object of a preposition.
- Both with and without article can follow **ser**, but with different meaning.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Necesito tu llave, ya que he perdido la mía. | I need your key as I've lost my own. |
| ¿Cuál es la mejor: la tuya o la de Andrea? | Which is the best: yours or Andrea's? |
| El mío está aquí. | Mine is here. (subject of verb — needs article) |
| Han encontrado el tuyo. | They have found yours. (object of verb — needs article) |
| Está debajo del nuestro. | It's under ours. (object of preposition — needs article) |
| Este libro es mío. | This book is mine. (simple ownership — no article) |
| Este libro es el mío. | This book is mine. (identifying a previously mentioned book) |

*Note: **salirse con la suya** = to get one's own way: *José siempre se sale con la suya*.*

### **5.3.2 Neuter article lo + possessive**

**Rule:** The sequence **lo** + masculine singular possessive adjective often refers to something **abstract** — a situation, state of affairs, or matter connected with the person — rather than a concrete noun.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Lo tuyo es impresionante. | That business of yours is amazing. |
| Lo mío es el trabajo. | My thing is work. |

## **5.4 Avoidance of ambiguity with *su/sus* and *suyo/a/os/as***

**Rule:** **su/sus** and **suyo/a/os/as** are ambiguous — they can mean 'his', 'her(s)', 'your(s)', 'its', 'their(s)'. Ambiguity is avoided by replacing them with **de** + the appropriate prepositional object pronoun (de él, de ella, de usted, de ellos, etc.). This construction also emphasises different possessors.

In Latin America, **su/sus** is typically assumed to mean *usted/ustedes*, so **de él/ella/ellos/ellas** replaces it when 'his/her/their' is intended.

**Examples:**

| Ambiguous | Unambiguous alternative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sus zapatos | los zapatos de ella | her shoes |
| su casa | la casa de ellos | their house |
| la bufanda de usted no la de él | — | your scarf not his |

## **5.5 Parts of the body and personal effects**

**Rule:** In contrast to English, Spanish uses the **definite article** rather than a possessive adjective for parts of the body, clothing and personal possessions — when context makes ownership clear. A possessive adjective is required where its omission could cause **ambiguity**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ponte los zapatos. | Put your shoes on. |
| Enséñame las manos. | Show me your hands. |
| Tengo que depilarme las piernas. | I have to shave my legs. |
| ¿Dónde están mis zapatos? | Where are my shoes? (possessive needed — ambiguous without it) |

## **5.6 Possessive adjectives with adverbs and prepositions**

**Rule:** After an adverb or compound preposition, the sequence **de** + prepositional object pronoun (*mí*, *ti*, *él* etc.) may be reduced to the possessive adjective form **mío/tuyo/suyo** etc. The masculine singular form is the default; if the adverb or preposition ends in **-a**, the feminine singular form is used.

This is a feature of popular speech in the Iberian Peninsula and widely used in Latin America. It is considered informal by some native speakers and is best avoided by learners in favour of **de** + prepositional object pronoun.

**Examples:**

| Possessive form | Full equivalent | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| encima tuya | encima de ti | on top of you |
| alrededor nuestro | alrededor de nosotros | all around us |
| en contra suya | en contra de él/ella etc. | against him/her etc. |
| en torno suyo | en torno de él/ella etc. | all around him/her etc. |
