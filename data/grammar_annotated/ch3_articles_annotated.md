# Chapter 3: Definite and indefinite articles

Articles in Spanish may be categorized as **definite**, **indefinite** and **neuter**. Definite articles designate what is already known or specific (English 'the'). Indefinite articles refer to something not yet known or not specifically identified (English 'a/an', 'some'). The neuter article **lo** refers to abstract concepts without gender.

## **3.1 Forms of the articles**

**Formation note:** Articles agree with the noun in gender and number.

|            | Masculine singular | Masculine plural | Feminine singular  | Feminine plural |
|------------|--------------------|------------------|--------------------|---|
| Definite   | **el**             | **los**          | **la**             | **las** |
| Indefinite | **un**             | **unos**         | **una**            | **unas** |

The neuter article is **lo** (no plural form).

### **3.1.1 El used with *a* and *de***

**Rule:** When preceded by **a** or **de**, the masculine singular article **el** contracts: **a** + **el** → **al**; **de** + **el** → **del**. No contraction is made when **el** forms part of a proper title, or before the subject pronoun **él**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| al banco | to the bank |
| del puerto | from the port |
| Es reportera de *El Universo*. | She is a reporter on *El Universo*. |
| Esta copa es de él. | This glass is his. |

### **3.1.2 El and un before feminine nouns**

**Rule:** Immediately before singular feminine nouns beginning with a **stressed a** or **ha**, the forms **el** and **un** are used instead of *la/una*. This does not change the noun's gender — plural still takes **las/unas**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| el ancla oxidada | the rusty anchor |
| un águila blanca | a white eagle |
| el aula nueva | the new classroom |
| unas aulas viejas | some old classrooms |

Common words in this category: **agua**, **alma**, **área**, **arma**, **asma**, **haba**, **habla**, **hambre**.

**When NOT to use el/un:**
- If the article is not immediately before the noun: *la bella arpa del siglo XVI* (not ~~el arpa~~ in this position).
- If initial **a/ha** is unstressed: *una alerta*, *la hamburguesa*.
- Before adjectives or names beginning with stressed **a**: *una alta galería*, *la Ana que conozco*.

Exceptions: *La Haya*, *la 'a'*, *la 'hache'*, *la árabe*.

**Rule:** **El** is the required article before an infinitive used as a noun.

## **3.2 The definite article**

In general, use of the definite article to refer to something specific is predictable from English. However, Spanish uses the definite article in many contexts where English does not.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Esto es jerez pero no el jerez que me gusta. | This is sherry but not the sherry I like. |
| Juan trajo rosas y María tiró las flores que compró ayer. | Juan brought roses and María threw away the flowers she bought yesterday. |

### **3.2.1 Contexts where the definite article is used**

#### 3.2.1.1 Definite article with generic nouns

**Rule:** Spanish requires the definite article before a noun used to refer to an entire category in general. English omits the article for plural generic nouns.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El plomo es un metal muy blando. | Lead is a very soft metal. |
| Los vinos de Chile son magníficos. | Chilean wines are splendid. |
| La jirafa es un animal extraño. | The giraffe is a strange animal / Giraffes are strange animals. |

#### 3.2.1.2 Definite article with abstract nouns

**Rule:** The definite article is used with abstract nouns when they have a general sense. However, it is **not** used after verbs like **tener** and **faltar**, or when abstract nouns appear in a list.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hay que reducir la pobreza en este país. | We have to reduce poverty in this country. |
| un aumento de la delincuencia | an increase in crime |
| ¡Hay que tener paciencia para hacer esto! | You need patience to do this! |
| Sinceridad, franqueza y honradez son cualidades que le faltan. | Sincerity, openness and honesty are qualities he lacks. |

#### 3.2.1.3 Definite article with general concepts and practices

**Rule:** Unlike English, Spanish uses the article for any noun referring to a general idea, phenomenon or practice — including colours, diseases, games, fields of activity and meals. Exception: colours after **de** and **en** do not take the article.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No me gusta el rojo como color. | I don't like red as a colour. |
| Casi se ha erradicado la viruela. | Smallpox has almost been eradicated. |
| Están jugando al béisbol. | They are playing baseball. |
| No sigo la política. | I don't follow politics. |
| ¿A qué hora es la cena? | What time is dinner? |
| El cuarto está pintado de verde. | The room is painted green. |

#### 3.2.1.4 Definite article with days, seasons and years

**Rule:** **El/los** are used with days of the week — **el** for a single occasion, **los** for habitual practice. The article is dropped after **ser** (identifying the day) and after **de** in routine-practice phrases. Seasons take the article except in adjectival phrases with **de**; after **en** the article is optional.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es el lunes cuando vamos. | It's on Monday that we are going. |
| No abren los sábados. | They don't open on Saturdays. |
| Vienen del sábado al lunes. | They are coming from Saturday until Monday. |
| Hoy es viernes. | Today is Friday. |
| Trabajo de lunes a viernes. | I work from Monday to Friday. |
| El verano es la mejor época del año. | Summer is the best time of the year. |
| Ya ponen a la venta ropa de otoño. | They are already selling autumn clothes. |
| No vamos a la playa en (el) invierno. | We do not go to the beach in winter. |

*The article is not used in letter dates: Domingo, 16 de enero de 1943.*
*The article is omitted before ordinal/cardinal numbers following a title: Alfonso XIII (trece).*

**Rule:** The definite article is used where English would use a possessive adjective with body parts, clothing and personal possessions. *See 5.5 for full coverage.*

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me duele la cabeza. | My head hurts. |
| Se quitó el abrigo. | He took off his coat. |

#### 3.2.1.6 Definite article in place of a noun

**Rule:** The definite article occurs before adjectives, past participles, prepositions and relative clauses to stand in for an understood noun — translating English 'the one(s)', 'that/those'. It agrees in gender and number with the unexpressed noun.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El rubio me cae mal. | I don't like the blond one. |
| estos asuntos y los discutidos ayer | these matters and those discussed yesterday |
| La de tu casa es mejor. | The one at your house is better. |
| Las que se vendían en España eran más sabrosas. | The ones they were selling in Spain were tastier. |

#### 3.2.1.7 Definite article with numbers and numerical expressions

**Rule:** The definite article is used with numbered nouns and with expressions of rate, weight, measure and quantity. With percentages, the indefinite article is an alternative.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| a los 60 años | at 60 years of age |
| en la casa nº 3 | in house no. 3 |
| el 60 por ciento de la población | 60 per cent of the population |
| dos veces al mes | twice a month |
| mil dólares la consulta | a thousand dollars per consultation |

| en el espacio | in space |

#### 3.2.1.9 Definite article with titles

**Rule:** The definite article is used with most titles when referring to someone in the third person. The article is **dropped** when addressing the person directly. The article is not used with **don**, **doña**, **fray**, **san(to)**, **santa**, **sor**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Les presento al comandante y a la señora de Paredes. | May I introduce Commander and Mrs Paredes? |
| Buenos días, doctor Sánchez. | Hello, Dr Sánchez. |
| Pertenece a don Miguel. | It belongs to don Miguel. |
| La abuela está sentada en el jardín. | Granny is sitting in the garden. |

#### 3.2.1.10 Definite article with geographical features

**Rule:** The definite article is used before names of unique geographical features — rivers, mountains, lakes, seas, oceans, straits, volcanoes, deserts — and before street names and other locations.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| el Tajo | the Tagus |
| el Teide | Mt Teide |
| el golfo de Vizcaya | the Bay of Biscay |
| el mar Adriático | the Adriatic Sea |
| el Atacama | the Atacama Desert |
| la calle Alcalá | Alcalá Street |
| el Parque María Luisa | María Luisa Park |

*Also: el cielo, el infierno, la Tierra.*

### **3.2.2 Contexts where the definite article is omitted**

#### 3.2.2.1 Names of countries

**Rule:** Most country names do not take the definite article unless the name is qualified by an adjective or adjectival phrase. Descriptive-title countries and a small set of exceptions retain the article. *Estados Unidos* generally omits the article; the verb is then singular.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Millones de turistas visitan España. | Millions of tourists visit Spain. |
| Hay muchos vestigios de la España romana. | There are many remains from Roman Spain. |
| los Países Bajos | the Netherlands |
| el Reino Unido | the United Kingdom |
| la India | India |
| Estados Unidos apoya la última decisión. | The USA supports the latest decision. |

*Countries where article is now optional: (el) Brasil, (el) Canadá, (el) Ecuador, (el) Japón, (el) Paraguay.*

#### 3.2.2.3 Omission with nouns in apposition

**Rule:** In formal Spanish, the definite article is often omitted before a second noun that adds explanatory information about the preceding noun (apposition). The article is always retained before comparative and superlative phrases.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Bogotá, capital de Colombia | Bogotá, the capital of Colombia |
| Raúl, marcador del gol | Raúl, the scorer of the goal |

### **3.2.3 The definite article with names of languages**

#### 3.2.3.1 When the article is used with language names

**Rule:** The definite article is used when referring to a language as a whole, and after **de** and certain other prepositions.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El vasco es una lengua de origen incierto. | Basque is a language of uncertain origin. |
| Esa palabra viene del alemán. | That word comes from German. |
| los problemas del ruso | the problems of Russian |
| Lo van a traducir al español. | They are going to translate it into Spanish. |

*Note: after **de** 'of' when not referring to the language as a whole, no article: un curso de inglés.*

#### 3.2.3.2 When the article is omitted with language names

**Rule:** The definite article is omitted after **en** 'in' and after verbs **aprender**, **hablar**, **saber**. It is commonly (but not obligatorily) omitted after **entender**, **enseñar**, **estudiar**. A language name always requires an article if it is qualified.

**Examples:**

|---|---|
| Está escrito en quechua. | It is written in Quechua. |
| Está aprendiendo holandés. | He is learning Dutch. |
| entiende (el) inglés | he understands English |
| No hablo correctamente (el) portugués. | I don't speak Portuguese correctly. |
| en el francés antiguo | in old French |
| Habla un español tradicional. | She speaks a traditional sort of Spanish. |

## **3.3 The indefinite article**

### **3.3.1 General use of the indefinite article**

**Rule:** The singular indefinite article **un/una** refers to someone or something not yet known or not specifically identified — similar to English 'a/an'. The plural forms **unos/unas** translate English 'some', and must also be used with nouns that only exist in the plural.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Un niño se me acercó. | A boy came up to me. |
| He comprado unos libros. | I've bought some books. |
| unas vacaciones en Italia | a holiday in Italy |
| unas tijeras | a pair of scissors |

### **3.3.3 Omission of the indefinite article**

**Rule (general):** When the indefinite article is omitted and the resulting bare noun becomes the subject, the noun must follow the verb.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Caían gotas de lluvia en el tejado. | Rain drops were falling on the roof. |
| Sale humo del motor. | Smoke is coming from the engine. |

**Rule:** The singular indefinite article cannot translate 'some' before unqualified mass nouns. Compare with 3.3.2 where a qualifier is present.

#### 3.3.3.2 Unos/as omitted

**Rule:** **Unos/unas** tends to be omitted only when the noun does not refer to particular persons or things — especially in sentences describing habitual actions. The omission of *unos/unas* before a subject noun is uncommon.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Por aquí pasan trenes cada cinco minutos. | Trains pass through here every five minutes. |
| Jorge repara televisiones. | Jorge repairs televisions. |
| Unos científicos han identificado el gen. | Scientists have identified the gene. |

#### 3.3.3.3 Unos/as replaced by algunos/as

**Rule:** **Unos/unas** is sometimes replaced by the plural form of **alguno** 'some'. The choice is contextual — *algunos/as* is more emphatic and suggests a specific (though unidentified) subset.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Algunas madres se preocupan por eso. | Some mothers worry about that. |

*Alternatives to unos/unas: ciertos/as, varios/as, unos/as cuantos/as, unos/as pocos/as.*

#### 3.3.3.4 Omission with nouns classifying professions and affiliations

**Rule:** The indefinite article is not used before nouns indicating profession, occupation, religious or political affiliation after verbs like **ser**, **elegir**, **hacerse**, **llamar**, **nombrar**, **calificar de**. However, the article is used when the noun is qualified.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ella es doctora y él es amo de casa. | She is a doctor and he is a househusband. |
| Le calificaron de dictador. | They described him as a dictator. |
| Es conservador y además un conservador intransigente. | He is a Conservative and moreover a diehard Conservative. |

#### 3.3.3.5 Omission with nouns in apposition

**Rule:** Like the definite article (see 3.2.2.3), the indefinite article is often not used in formal Spanish before a second noun offering explanatory information about the preceding noun.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Pasamos la noche en Medinaceli, aldea ahora casi desierta. | We spent the night in Medinaceli, now an almost deserted village. |
| Trabaja como televendedora, empleo que odia. | She works as a telesales person, a job she hates. |

#### 3.3.3.6 Omission with certain indefinite adjectives and numerals

**Rule:** The indefinite article is omitted with **otro**, **tal/semejante**, **medio**, **mil**, **cien(to)**, **qué** (exclamatory), and **cierto**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hoy la vi con otro chico. | Today I saw her with another boy. |
| ¡Qué imbécil es Paco! | What an idiot Paco is! |
| Esperamos media hora. | We waited half an hour. |
| Cuesta mil euros. | It costs a thousand euros. |

*For cases where the article is used with cierto and tal/semejante, see 9.15 and 9.16.*

#### 3.3.3.7 Omission after certain prepositions

**Rule:** The indefinite article is almost always omitted after **sin** 'without', and often omitted after **con** 'with/wearing'. It is also omitted after **como**, **por**, **a modo/manera de** when placing someone or something in a category. Exception: when the article is needed to emphasize 'one'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Salió sin chaqueta. | He went out without a jacket. |
| Nunca se le ve con sombrero. | You never see him wearing a hat. |
| Me dejaron sin un (solo) peso. | They left me without a (single) peso. |
| Como persona liberal no puedo apoyar esto. | As a liberal I can't support this. |
| Ella pasa por buena directora. | She passes for a good director. |

#### 3.3.3.8 Omission with common verb + noun combinations

**Rule:** The indefinite article is often omitted after verbs like **buscar**, **emplear**, **encontrar**, **hay**, **llevar**, **tener**. Exceptions: when the noun is qualified, or when it is necessary to indicate 'one'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Estamos buscando casa. | We are looking for a house. |
| No llevaba abrigo. | He wasn't wearing a coat. |
| No tienen garaje. | They don't have a garage. |
| ¿Hay una radio que funciona? | Is there a radio that works? |
| Encontró una rueda pero no la otra. | He found one wheel but not the other. |

*Note: Spanish is less likely than English to omit an article before the second or subsequent nouns in a list, especially when nouns are of different genders: Los CDs y las cintas no están aquí.*

## **3.4 The neuter article *lo***

**Rule:** **Lo** refers to beliefs, concepts, thoughts, opinions or ideas that have no gender. It has no plural form.

### **3.4.1 Lo with adjectives and past participles**

**Rule:** **Lo** + masculine singular adjective or past participle creates a noun phrase expressing 'the ... thing/matter/aspect'. When what follows is a plural noun, **ser** is plural.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Lo más importante es informar al banco. | The most important thing is to inform the bank. |
| Todo lo mío está en esa maleta. | All that is mine is in that suitcase. |
| Lo convenido ayer no son más que las líneas generales. | What was agreed yesterday was only the general outlines. |

*Notes:*
- **a lo** + adjective/noun = *a la manera de* 'in the manner of': *a lo antiguo* 'in an old-fashioned way'.
- **de lo más/menos** + adjective = superlative construction: *de lo más fina* 'one of the finest'.

### **3.4.2 Lo + adjective/past participle/adverb + que**

**Rule:** This construction often translates English 'how' + adjective or adverb (sometimes exclamatory). Adjectives agree with the noun they refer to. Also used after **con**, **para**, **por**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hasta entonces no me había dado cuenta de lo alta que era. | Until then I hadn't realized how tall she was. |
| Hay que ver lo despacio que conduce. | You have to see how slowly he drives. |
| Con lo listo que es, ¡mira qué mal ha hecho el examen! | He's so clever but look how badly he did in the exam! |
| Tiene bastantes canas para lo joven que es. | He has a lot of grey hairs for someone so young. |

*For **qué** + adjective/adverb to translate 'how', see 27.4.1.*

### **3.4.3 Lo de + noun**

**Rule:** **Lo de** + noun succinctly expresses 'the matter of', 'the business about', 'the situation with'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Lo del agua es un problema grave. | The water situation is a serious problem. |
| Lo de ayer me sorprendió. | The business about yesterday surprised me. |

### **3.4.4 Lo que**

**Rule:** **Lo que** translates 'the thing which', 'what' — it introduces a noun clause referring to an entire idea or situation.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Lo que nos sorprendió fue su dominio del inglés. | What surprised us was her excellent English. |
| No entiendo lo que dices. | I don't understand what you're saying. |

*For **lo que** and **lo cual** as relative pronouns, see Chapter 25.*

**Rule:** **Lo** appears in a number of fixed phrases, usually involving **a**, **de** or **por**.
