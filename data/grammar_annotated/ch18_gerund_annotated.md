# Chapter 18: Uses of the gerund

Spanish gerunds are **invariable** and always end in **-ndo** (for forms, see 10.8). Broadly, the gerund conveys 'in doing', 'while doing' or 'by doing'.

**Critical rule:** English nouns and adjectives ending in '-ing' are normally **not** translated by a Spanish gerund — see 18.5.

The gerund also combines with **estar** to form the progressive tenses (see 10.10 and 11.3).

## **18.1 Basic use of the gerund**

**Rule:** The gerund indicates an action occurring **simultaneously** (more or less) with the main verb's action. As an extension, it can also indicate **method** ('by' + gerund), **cause** ('as/since'), or **purpose** ('to' + verb).

*Note: Adding **como** before the gerund makes the simultaneous action imaginary rather than real — similar to **como si** + subjunctive: **Movió la cabeza como diciendo no** 'He shook his head as if saying no'.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Antonio contestó sonriendo. | Antonio smiled as he replied. | simultaneous action |
| Lo esperé leyendo el periódico. | While I waited for him I read the paper. | simultaneous action |
| Ganaron haciendo trampas. | They won by cheating. | method — 'by' |
| Llamándola por teléfono la vas a espantar. | You'll frighten her off by phoning her. | method — 'by' |
| Estando en Granada tienes que visitar la Alhambra. | As you are in Granada you have to visit the Alhambra. | cause — 'as/since' |
| Me gritaron pidiendo ayuda. | They shouted to ask for help. | purpose — 'to' |

## **18.2 Gerund and main verb with different subjects**

**Rule:** The gerund and the main verb can have **different subjects**. This is especially common with **perception verbs** and verbs of depicting, visualising or encountering. In this case, the **subject of the gerund is the direct object of the main verb**.

*Notes:*
- *The gerund's subject can be explicitly stated — often corresponding to English 'with': **Es difícil quedar, viviendo ellos tan lejos** 'It's difficult to meet up, with them living so far away'.*
- *After the **preterite** of **ver**, an **infinitive** (not gerund) implies a completed action: **Lo vi tirar unas piedras** (completed) vs **Lo vi tirando piedras** (in progress).*
- ***Venir** and **ir** are generally used with the **infinitive**, not the gerund, after perception verbs: **Lo miramos venir hacia nosotros** 'We watched him come towards us'.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La vi bailando con Paco. | I saw her dancing with Paco. |
| Lo recuerdo jugando al fútbol. | I remember him playing football. |
| Sorprendimos a Pilar fumando. | We caught Pilar smoking. |

**Verbs commonly used this way:** *describir*, *fotografar*, *imaginar*, *mirar*, *mostrar*, *pintar*.

## **18.3 Gerund in place of a relative clause**

**Rule:** The gerund is increasingly accepted in informal speech and writing to **modify a noun** in place of a relative clause — but only with **action verbs**. With non-action verbs a relative clause must be used. The relative clause remains the safest option for foreign learners. Common in official language and press captions.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Un coche circulando a la izquierda sería parado casi inmediatamente. | A car being driven on the left-hand side would be stopped almost immediately. | action verb — gerund OK |
| un libro que describe su viaje (not: ~~un libro describiendo su viaje~~) | a book describing his journey through the jungle | non-action — relative clause required |
| Publicaron un comunicado pidiendo la paz. | They published a communiqué asking for peace. | official language |
| el primer ministro besando a un bebé | the Prime Minister kissing a baby | press caption |

### **18.4.1 *Ir* and *andar* + gerund**

**Rule:**
- **Ir** + gerund (literal): 'going about' performing an action.
- **Ir** + gerund (figurative): stresses **gradual or slow progression** / repetition — *ir* loses its motion meaning.
- **Andar** + gerund: 'going around' doing something — often has a **dismissive or critical** tone.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Iba cantando por las calles. | He went singing through the streets. | ir — literal motion |
| Ve echando monedas. | Keep putting coins in. | ir — gradual progression |
| La población del pueblo fue reduciéndose. | The village's population declined (gradually). | ir — gradual progression |
| Ese chico anda diciendo tonterías. | That boy goes around talking nonsense. | andar — dismissive |

### **18.4.2 *Venir* + gerund**

**Rule:** **Venir** + gerund expresses what someone **has/had been doing** or what **has/had been happening** over a period of time leading to the present or past moment.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Viene quejándose de su mala salud. | He's been complaining about his ill health. |
| Vienen diciendo eso desde hace años. | They've been saying that for years. |

### **18.4.3 *Pasar* + gerund**

**Rule:** **Pasar** + gerund indicates **how time is spent**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Pasó la tarde durmiendo. | He spent the afternoon sleeping. |

### **18.4.4 *Seguir* and *continuar* + gerund**

**Rule:** Only the **gerund** (never the infinitive) can be used with *seguir* and *continuar* to express 'to continue to do' or 'to carry on doing'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Seguimos/Continuamos hablando. | We carried on talking. |

### **18.4.5 *Acabar* and *terminar* + gerund**

**Rule:** **Acabar** and **terminar** + gerund express **'ending up doing'** something (not to be confused with *acabar de* + infinitive = 'to have just').

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Siempre acaban peleándose. | They always end up quarrelling. |
| Terminarás rompiéndolo. | You'll end up breaking it. |

### **18.4.6 *Salir* + gerund**

**Rule:** **Salir** + gerund has a variety of idiomatic meanings depending on the gerund used. Best learned phrase by phrase.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Fue Ignacio quien salió perdiendo. | It was Ignacio who came off worst. |
| Salieron volando. | They were off like a shot. |

## **18.5 Cases where the gerund is NOT used**

**Rule:** English '-ing' forms are frequently **not** translated by a Spanish gerund. Four key contexts where the gerund cannot be used.

### **18.5.1 After prepositions → use the infinitive**

**Rule:** After any preposition, the **infinitive** (never the gerund) must be used in Spanish. See 17.2.

| English (gerund) | Spanish (infinitive) |
|---|---|
| after speaking | después de hablar (never ~~después de hablando~~) |
| without eating | sin comer |
| before leaving | antes de salir |

### **18.5.2 As a verbal noun → use the infinitive**

**Rule:** Where English uses a gerund as a verbal noun ('I like swimming'), Spanish uses the **infinitive** instead.

| English (gerund) | Spanish (infinitive) |
|---|---|
| He doesn't like swimming. | No le gusta nadar. |
| Swimming is good for you. | Nadar es bueno para la salud. |

### **18.5.3 As an adjective → use a Spanish adjective, prepositional phrase, or other solution**

**Rule:** English '-ing' adjectives (e.g. 'interesting', 'floating') generally cannot be translated by a Spanish gerund. Three alternatives:

**(a) Spanish adjective** — often ending in **-ante** (from -ar verbs) or **-iente/-ente** (from -er/-ir verbs):

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| an unidentified flying object | un objeto volador no identificado |
| a floating exchange rate | un tipo de cambio flotante |
| Sleeping Beauty | la Bella Durmiente |

**(b) Prepositional phrase** — *de* + related noun or infinitive:

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| a swimming lesson | una clase de natación |
| riding boots | botas de montar |

**(c) Other solutions:**

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| rising damp | humedad de paredes |

*Exceptions: **ardiendo** 'burning' and **hirviendo** 'boiling' remain invariable and follow the noun: **aceite hirviendo** 'boiling oil', **una casa ardiendo** 'a burning house'.*

### **18.5.4 To describe position or condition → use the past participle**

**Rule:** When '-ing' describes a **position** or **condition** in English, Spanish uses the **past participle**, not the gerund.

| English ('-ing') | Spanish (past participle) |
|---|---|
| kneeling | arrodillado |
| sitting | sentado |
| boring | pesado |
| amusing | divertido |
