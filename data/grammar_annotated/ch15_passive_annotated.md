# Chapter 15: Passive constructions

A passive construction is created when the object of an active sentence becomes the subject of the passive verb. The original subject (if mentioned) is linked to the passive verb by a preposition — **por** in Spanish.

**Active → Passive examples:**

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Mary wrote the book. | The book was written by Mary. |
| Everyone loves John. | John is loved by everyone. |
| (someone) posted the letters | The letters have been posted. |

Although passive constructions can be transposed directly into Spanish, in practice Spanish **frequently replaces them with alternatives** — see 15.2.

## **15.1 *Ser* and *estar* with the past participle**

**Rule:** Understanding the passive in Spanish requires distinguishing between **ser** and **estar** with the past participle.

**Agreement rule:** Past participles are invariable when dependent on **haber**, but **always agree in number and gender** when dependent on **ser** or **estar**.

**Agent ('by whom'):** Usually introduced by **por**. However, **de** is found with certain past participles that function adjectivally: *rodeado de*, *seguido de*, *acompañado de*, *cubierto de*, *forrado de*, *adornado de*.

**Tenses of *ser*:** All tenses are possible, though present and imperfect are rare except for sustained/repeated actions.

### **15.1.1 *Estar* + past participle — state or condition**

**Rule:** **Estar** + past participle indicates a **state or condition** — often the end result or consequence of a prior action. It describes *how things are*, not *what is being done to them*.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Cuando llegamos la puerta estaba abierta. | When we arrived the door was open. |
| Las entradas ya estaban vendidas. | The seats were already sold. |
| El informe está preparado. | The report is prepared (i.e. ready). |
| Estaba detenido. | He was detained (i.e. under arrest — state). |

### **15.1.2 *Ser* + past participle — true passive (action)**

**Rule:** **Ser** + past participle forms the **true passive**, focusing on an **action** being performed. Use this when reporting the agent or 'doer'. The past participle agrees in number and gender with the subject.

*Note: English can form a passive where the subject corresponds to an indirect object ('Mary was awarded a scholarship'). This is **not possible** with *ser* in Spanish — see 15.2.2 and 15.2.3.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Cuando llegamos la puerta fue abierta por una criada. | When we arrived the door was opened by a maid. |
| Las entradas fueron vendidas antes de que llegáramos. | The seats were sold before we arrived. |
| El informe fue preparado por mí. | The report was prepared by me. |
| Fui detenido. | I was detained (i.e. the action of placing under arrest). |
| La lección había sido explicada bien. | The lesson had been well explained. |
| Los textos han sido discutidos. | The texts have been discussed. |
| Las cuentas son pagadas diariamente. | The accounts are paid every day. |

**Agent with *de* (adjectival past participles):**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| rodeado de | surrounded by |
| seguido de | followed by |
| acompañado de | accompanied by |
| cubierto de | covered with |
| Enviar el artículo acompañado de su CV. | Send the article with your CV. |

### CMP: *Estar* vs *ser* + past participle

**Rule:** The same past participle after *estar* describes a **state**; after *ser* it describes an **action** (the true passive).

| | *estar* + participle | *ser* + participle |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | state / result | action / event |
| Question answered | What is it like? | What was done (by whom)? |
| Agent (*por*) | ✗ not used | ✓ possible and expected |
| Agreement | agrees with subject | agrees with subject |

| Spanish | English | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Estaba detenido. | He was detained (under arrest — state). | estar |
| Fui detenido. | I was detained (the act of arresting). | ser |
| El informe está preparado. | The report is ready (result). | estar |
| El informe fue preparado por mí. | The report was prepared by me (action). | ser |

## **15.2 Alternatives to passive constructions**

**Rule:** Passive constructions with *ser* are common in formal and written Spanish, but native speakers frequently use these alternatives — especially when no agent is expressed, in informal writing, or in everyday speech.

### **15.2.1 *Se* passive — inanimate subject**

**Rule:** When the subject is not a person, the verb is used reflexively with **se** and agrees with the subject. **Cannot** be used with **por** + agent — if an agent must be expressed, use the *ser* passive (15.1.2).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se construyó la casa en diez meses. | The house was built in ten months. |
| Se podan las rosas en primavera. | Roses are pruned in spring. |
| ✗ La casa se compró por los Martínez. | ✗ (incorrect — use *ser* when agent expressed) |
| ✓ La casa fue comprada por los Martínez. | The house was bought by the Martínezes. |

### **15.2.2 *Se* passive — indirect object involved**

**Rule:** When an English passive corresponds to a Spanish indirect object ('Mary was awarded a scholarship'), Spanish uses a reflexive construction with **se** in the 3rd person. The verb agrees with the grammatical subject (the thing awarded/returned).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se le concedió a María una beca. | María was awarded a scholarship. |
| Se le devolverán las muestras. | The samples will be returned to him. |

### **15.2.3 *Se* passive — personal subject**

**Rule:** When the English subject is a **person**, using *se* + plural verb risks being interpreted as reflexive or reciprocal. To avoid this, use *se* in the **3rd person singular only**, and introduce the person as a **direct object preceded by personal *a***. When the object is a pronoun, use **le/les** (not **lo/los**) for masculine.

*Note: Where ambiguity is not an issue (e.g. notices/ads), a standard reflexive construction is used: **Se busca niñera** 'Nanny wanted'.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se registra a todos los que entren. | All who come in are searched. |
| Se vio a las chicas en la calle. | The girls were seen in the street. |
| Se consultó a los autores del artículo. | The authors of the article were consulted. |
| Se nos acusó. | We were accused. |
| Se le/les acusó. | He was accused. / They were accused. |
| Se le interrogó. | Someone questioned him. (= He was questioned.) |
| Se busca niñera. | Nanny wanted. |
| Se necesitan cocineros. | Cooks needed. |

### **15.2.4 Converting to an active construction**

**Rule:** English passive → Spanish **active** sentence. Particularly useful in informal speech.

**Examples:**

| English passive | Spanish active |
|---|---|
| I was handed the files by a colleague. | Un colega me entregó los archivos. |
| She was not invited by her friends. | Sus amigas no la invitaron. |

### **15.2.5 Indeterminate 3rd person plural**

**Rule:** When no agent is expressed, use an **active verb with an indeterminate 3rd person plural subject** ('they', 'people') — if context permits.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Lo nombraron director. | He was appointed director. |

### **15.2.6 When the verb is already reflexive — *uno* or *la gente***

**Rule:** If the verb is already reflexive (e.g. *darse cuenta*), a second *se* cannot be added to form a passive. Use **uno** 'one' or **la gente** 'people' as the active subject instead.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Uno no se da cuenta de su importancia. | Its importance is not realized. |
| La gente se olvida pronto de cantantes como él. | Singers like him are soon forgotten. |
