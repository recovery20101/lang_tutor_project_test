# Chapter 4: Demonstrative adjectives and pronouns

Demonstratives are the equivalent of English 'this'/'these' and 'that'/'those'. Spanish goes further by dividing the 'that'/'those' category into two — giving **three** demonstratives instead of two.

**Complete forms table:**

| | Masc. sg. | Fem. sg. | Masc. pl. | Fem. pl. | Neuter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 'this/these' | **este** | **esta** | **estos** | **estas** | **esto** |
| 'that/those' (near you) | **ese** | **esa** | **esos** | **esas** | **eso** |
| 'that/those' (far from both) | **aquel** | **aquella** | **aquellos** | **aquellas** | **aquello** |

The masculine and feminine forms can be used as both **adjectives** and **pronouns**. The neuter forms are **pronouns only**.

*Note on accents: Native speakers still commonly use a written accent to distinguish pronouns from adjectives (e.g. **éste** vs **este libro**). Accented forms are no longer officially recommended.*

## **4.1 Demonstrative adjectives**

**Rule:** Demonstrative adjectives are normally placed **before** the noun and agree with it in number and gender.

**Key usage notes:**
1. Never confuse masculine **este/ese** with neuter **esto/eso**: *este libro* — never ~~esto libro~~.
2. Unlike English, demonstrative adjectives in Spanish are generally **repeated before each noun**, especially nouns of different gender: *Este caballo y esta oveja viven juntos*.
3. Avoid using **este/ese/aquel** before feminine nouns beginning with stressed **a** or **ha** — see 3.1.2.

### **4.1.1 Este, esta, estos, estas — proximity to speaker**

**Rule:** Used to identify what is physically near to or associated with the **speaker**. Also signifies proximity in time (this afternoon, this week, etc.).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Estas botas que acabo de limpiar. | These boots that I have just cleaned. |
| Lo voy a ver esta tarde. | I am going to see him this afternoon. |

### **4.1.2 Ese vs aquel — distance from speaker**

**Rule:** Both translate English 'that'/'those', but they mark a distinction:

- **ese/esa/esos/esas**: what is connected with or known by the **person being addressed** — 'that near you' or 'associated with you'.
- **aquel/aquella/aquellos/aquellas**: what is distant from **both** speaker and listener — 'that distant from us both' or 'not associated with either of us'.

In practice, native speakers may alternate between the two, except where the deliberate intention is to stress distance. The same distinction applies to **time**: *ese* for relatively recent past, *aquel* for a more distant past.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ese pueblo donde estás | that village where you are | near the listener — ese |
| El documento está en aquel archivo en Madrid. | The document is in that archive in Madrid. | distant from both — aquel |
| esos días que pasamos juntos el verano pasado | those days we spent together last summer | recent past — ese |
| En aquellos tiempos no existían armas de fuego. | In those days firearms did not exist. | distant past — aquel |

### **4.1.3 Demonstrative adjective placed after the noun**

**Rule:** Although the normal position is before the noun, the demonstrative adjective can be placed **after** the noun (especially in speech). When it is postposed, the noun is preceded by the **definite article**. Foreign learners should use this construction with care — it easily conveys a **critical or dismissive** attitude.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| el libro ese | that book (dismissive tone) |
| los días aquellos | those days (distant/nostalgic tone) |

## **4.2 Masculine and feminine demonstrative pronouns**

**Rule:** Demonstrative pronouns stand in place of a specific noun — they agree in number and gender with the noun they replace, translating 'this one', 'that one', 'these ones', 'those ones'.

**este = 'the latter'** (the last-mentioned); **aquel = 'the former'** (mentioned earlier).

*Note: 'Those who' is not expressed by **esos/aquellos** but by **los/las que**: *No perdona a los que lo critican* 'He does not forgive those who criticize him'.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No me des esa papaya, prefiero esta. | Don't give me that papaya, I prefer this one. |
| Ese es el cuadro que más me gusta. | That's the picture I like most. |
| ¿Cuál quieres: el de tu padre o aquel que vimos ayer? | Which one do you want: your father's or the one we saw yesterday? |
| De las dos propuestas, esta es la más original y aquella la más práctica. | Of the two proposals, the latter is the most original and the former the most practical. |

## **4.3 Neuter demonstrative pronouns**

**Rule:** **Esto**, **eso**, **aquello** refer to things bearing no grammatical gender — such as statements, actions, situations, and items requiring identification. They follow the same three-way distance distinction as the adjectives.

**Common constructions:**
- **esto/eso/aquello + de**: 'this/that matter of', 'that business about'
- **y eso que**: 'although', 'even though'
- Fixed expressions: **eso sí** (yes indeed), **eso no** (certainly not), **eso es** (that's it/right), **eso sí que no** (no way), **por eso** (therefore, for that reason)

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Esto es una pesadilla. | This is a nightmare. |
| Todo aquello es bosque virgen. | All of that is virgin forest. |
| ¿Qué es eso? | What is that? |
| eso de no poder ver la tele | that business of not being able to watch TV |
| Decidió ir y eso que le dije que no. | He decided to go even though I told him not to. |
| por eso | therefore, for that reason |
