# Chapter 6: Adjectives

As in English, adjectives assign a property or characteristic to a noun. They can appear next to a noun (*un niño listo* 'a clever boy') or after verbs such as *ser/estar* (*El niño es listo* 'The boy is clever').

**Key differences from English:**
- Spanish adjectives agree with nouns in gender and number: *un cojín rojo* but *unas camisetas rojas*.
- Spanish adjectives are commonly placed **after** the noun: *una montaña alta*.

## **6.1 Gender**

### **6.1.1 Adjectives ending in -o**

**Rule:** If the masculine form ends in **-o**, the feminine form ends in **-a**.

**Examples:**

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| blanco | blanca | white |
| rojo | roja | red |
| nuevo | nueva | new |

### **6.1.2 Adjectives ending in -ón, -án, -ín and -or**

**Rule:** Usually an **-a** is added to the masculine form to create the feminine. Exceptions without a distinct feminine form: adjectives with comparative sense (**anterior**, **exterior**, **inferior**, **interior**, **mayor**, **mejor**, **menor**, **peor**, **posterior**, **superior**, **ulterior**) and isolated cases like **marrón**, **afín**.

**Examples:**

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| llorón | llorona | crybaby |
| holgazán | holgazana | lazy |
| emprendedor | emprendedora | enterprising |
| parlanchín | parlanchina | chatty |

**Exceptions without feminine form (comparative adjectives):**

| Adjective | Meaning |
|---|---|
| anterior | previous |
| exterior | outer |
| inferior | lower/inferior |
| interior | inner |
| mayor | greater/older |
| mejor | better |
| menor | lesser/younger |
| peor | worse |
| posterior | later |
| superior | upper/superior |
| ulterior | further/later |

*Note: **superior** uniquely takes a feminine form in **la madre superiora** 'mother superior'.*

### **6.1.3 Adjectives of nationality and regional origin**

**Rule:** Masculine forms ending in **-o** follow the pattern of 6.1.1. Masculine forms ending in a consonant add **-a**. Adjectives ending in **-a**, **-ense**, **-í** or **-ú** have the same form for both genders.

**Examples:**

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| chino | china | Chinese |
| francés | francesa | French |
| andaluz | andaluza | Andalusian |
| belga | belga | Belgian |
| canadiense | canadiense | Canadian |
| marroquí | marroquí | Moroccan |
| hindú | hindú | Hindu/Indian |

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Su padre es belga y su madre canadiense. | Her father is Belgian and her mother Canadian. |

### **6.1.4 Adjectives with no distinct feminine form**

#### 6.1.4.1 General adjectives with no feminine form

**Rule:** Adjectives not falling into categories 6.1.1–6.1.3 generally have no distinct feminine form. This includes adjectives ending in other consonants or in the vowels **-e** and **-a** (endings **-ista**, **-ita**).

**Examples:**

| Adjective | Meaning |
|---|---|
| feliz | happy |
| fácil | easy |
| cortés | polite |
| triste | sad |
| realista | realistic |
| cosmopolita | cosmopolitan |

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| una chica feliz y cortés | a happy and polite girl |

*Note: **burgués** 'middle class' is a common exception with feminine form **burguesa**.*

#### 6.1.4.2 Adjectives of colour

**Rule:** Colours that are true adjectives agree normally. However, when **nouns** are used to designate colour, they are invariable in gender (representing omission of **color de**). Compound colour adjectives are also invariable in gender, even if the individual adjectives would normally agree.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| una flor roja | a red flower |
| una hoja verde | a green leaf |
| un mantel naranja | an orange tablecloth |
| una camisa (de) color lila y malva | a lilac and mauve shirt |
| una gabardina azul marino | a navy blue raincoat |
| una corbata rojo oscuro | a dark red tie |

Common invariable colour nouns: **café**, **cereza**, **lila**, **paja**, **rosa**, **turquesa**, **violeta**.

*Note: Single-word compounds formed from two adjectives do agree: **verdiblanco/a** 'greenish white'.*

### **6.1.5 Gender agreement with nouns of mixed gender**

**Rule:** When an adjective relates to two or more nouns of the **same** gender, it adopts that gender and is plural. When the nouns are of **mixed** gender, the adjective is **masculine and plural**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| una camisa y una corbata bonitas | a nice shirt and tie |
| El sofá y la cama están sucios. | The sofa and the bed are dirty. |

## **6.2 Singular and plural of adjectives**

**Rule:** Most adjectives agree in number with their noun or with the subject of *ser*, *estar*, *parecer* etc. Plurals are formed like nouns: add **-s** to vowel endings, **-es** to consonant endings.

**Rule:** Nouns used as colour adjectives (see 6.1.4.2) are invariable not only in gender but also in number (though in speech and Latin America this is not always observed). Compound colour adjectives are also invariable in number.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| unos guantes rosa | some pink gloves |
| rayos ultravioleta | ultraviolet rays |
| blusas azul marino | navy blue blouses |
| ojos verde claro | light green eyes |

### **6.2.2 Special cases of number agreement**

#### 6.2.2.1 Two nouns treated as a single item

**Rule:** When two or more nouns are considered a single item, the qualifying adjective may be singular and agrees with the nearest noun. When the nouns are clearly separate items, the adjective is plural.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Estudia lengua y cultura francesa. | He studies French language and culture. |
| el diseño y la moda italiana | Italian design and fashion |
| una inteligencia y dedicación tremendas | tremendous intelligence and dedication |

## **6.3 Shortening of adjectives**

### **6.3.1 Adjectives shortened before masculine singular nouns**

**Rule:** **bueno**, **malo**, **primero**, **tercero**, **uno**, **alguno**, **ninguno** drop the final **-o** before masculine singular nouns. **algún** and **ningún** take a written accent. Shortening also occurs when another adjective immediately follows before the noun. Shortening does **not** occur when the adjective is joined by a conjunction or is separated from the noun.

**Examples:**

| Full form | Shortened | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| bueno | buen | un buen libro | a good book |
| malo | mal | mal tiempo | bad weather |
| primero | primer | el primer día | the first day |
| tercero | tercer | el tercer día | the third day |
| ninguno | ningún | ningún hombre | no man |
| — | — | el primer nuevo caso | the first new case |
| — | — | el primero y último caso | the first and last case (no shortening) |

### **6.3.2 Grande → gran**

**Rule:** **Grande** is shortened to **gran** before nouns of either gender. **Grande** is not shortened when preceded by **más** or **menos**, when joined to another adjective by a conjunction, or when otherwise separated from the noun.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| una gran pena | a great sadness |
| un gran apetito | a great appetite |
| la más grande ocasión | the greatest occasion (no shortening) |
| el grande aunque desconocido escritor | the great although unknown writer (no shortening) |

| Santo Tomás | Saint Thomas |
| Santo Domingo | Saint Dominic |

### **6.4.2 Adjectives that may precede or follow the noun**

#### 6.4.2.1 Restrictive vs. non-restrictive position

**Rule:** Most adjectives can appear on either side of the noun. The **default position is after**, where the adjective is **restrictive** — it identifies particular items within the noun's category. An adjective placed **before** the noun is **non-restrictive** — it denotes an incidental property without restricting the noun's reference.

Adjectives expressing a **subjective opinion** or emotional judgement commonly precede the noun. Adjectives describing **categorically** (technical/scientific classifications, colour, shape, condition) generally follow.

*Note: An adjective qualified by an adverb is likely used objectively — placement after the noun is safest: **un cantante muy popular**, **ropa muy moderna**.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hay que cambiar las podridas maderas. | We have to replace the rotten wood. | non-restrictive — all wood needs replacing |
| Hay que cambiar las maderas podridas. | We have to replace the rotten wood. | restrictive — only the rotten pieces |
| la odiada/temida policía secreta | the hated/feared secret police | subjective — before |
| su oportuna intervención | your timely intervention | subjective — before |
| este maldito hotel | this wretched hotel | subjective — before |
| un análisis sociológico | a sociological analysis | categorical — after |
| una cinta azul | a blue ribbon | colour — after |
| una forma redonda | a round shape | shape — after |
| una silla rota | a broken chair | condition — after |

#### CMP: Adjective position — meaning change

**Rule:** Some adjectives have **different meanings** depending on whether they come before or after the noun. Position before the noun tends to give a figurative or subjective meaning; position after gives a literal or descriptive one.

| Adjective | After noun | Meaning | Before noun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| antiguo | la capital antigua | the ancient capital | la antigua capital | the former capital |
| medio | el hombre medio | the average man | medio vaso | half a glass |
| nuevo | una casa nueva | a brand-new house | una nueva casa | a new (another) house |

#### 6.4.2.3 Bueno and malo — position

**Rule:** **bueno** and **malo** are more commonly placed **before** the noun, unless the meaning is 'good'/'bad' in the moral sense.

**Examples:**

|---|---|
| una buena memoria | a good memory |
| un niño malo | a bad (naughty/morally bad) boy |

#### 6.4.2.4 Grande and alto — position

**Rule:** **grande** and **alto** are more commonly placed **before** the noun when an abstract sense is intended. When used literally, they follow.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| una gran cantidad | a large amount | abstract — before |
| una casa grande | a big house | literal size — after |
| un alto cargo | a senior official | abstract — before |
| una montaña alta | a high mountain | literal height — after |

## **6.6 Verb + adjective sequences**

**Rule:** A common construction combines a verb with an adjective. In one type, the adjective is linked to the verb's **subject** and agrees with it. In the second type, the adjective is linked to the verb's **object** and agrees with that. Two word orders are possible in the second type: the adjective can follow the verb or follow the noun.

**Examples — adjective agreeing with subject:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ana compró el caballo entusiasmada. | Ana was thrilled to buy the horse. |
| Mi padre se casó joven. | My father married young. |

**Examples — adjective agreeing with object (both word orders shown):**

| Order 1 | Order 2 | English |
|---|---|---|
| Toma muy caliente su café. | Toma su café muy caliente. | He takes his coffee very hot. |
| Tiene rota la pierna. | Tiene la pierna rota. | His leg is broken. |
