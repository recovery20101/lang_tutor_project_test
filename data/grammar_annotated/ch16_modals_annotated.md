# Chapter 16: Modal auxiliary verbs

Modal verbs are auxiliaries expressing attitudes or feelings in relation to an infinitive — 'you can go', 'you must go', 'you want to go', 'you have to go'.

**Main Spanish modals:**
- **Obligation:** *deber*, *tener que*, *haber (que)*
- **Ability / permission / possibility:** *poder*, *saber*
- **Desire:** *querer*
- **Habitual action:** *soler*

## **16.1 *Deber***

### **16.1.1 *Deber* vs *deber de***

**Rule:** **Deber** (alone) expresses **obligation**: 'must', 'have to', 'should', 'ought to'. **Deber de** expresses **supposition or inference**: 'must (have)', 'probably'.

In practice, native speakers do not always maintain this distinction and sometimes use *deber* alone for supposition. However, **deber de should never be used for obligation**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Debe traerlo mañana. | She must bring it tomorrow. | obligation — *deber* |
| Ustedes deben exigir un reembolso. | You should demand a refund. | obligation — *deber* |
| Deben de haber viajado ayer. | They must have travelled yesterday. | supposition — *deber de* |
| El río no debe ser muy profundo. | The river can't be very deep. | supposition — *deber* (colloquial) |

### **16.1.2 Tenses of *deber***

**Rule:** Tense selection in *deber* follows these patterns:

- **Conditional** (*debería*) or **-ra imperfect subjunctive** (*debiera*) [formal SP / general LA] → 'ought to'
- Both can refer to the **past** with a **perfect infinitive** → 'ought to have'
- **Preterite or imperfect of *deber*** (without *de*) → 'should have' / 'must have', based on which tense the underlying verb would use without *deber*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Construction |
|---|---|---|
| Deberías vestirte de etiqueta. | You ought to dress formally. | conditional |
| Debieran confesar su error. | They ought to own up to their mistake. | *-ra* subj. [formal SP / LA] |
| Deberíamos/Debiéramos haberle negado el permiso. | We ought to have refused him his permit. | conditional/subj. + perfect inf. |
| Debía (de) tener tres hijos. | He must have had three children. | imperfect (← *tenía*) |
| Debí eligir la otra opción. | I should have chosen the other option. | preterite (← *eligí*) |
| Debía (de) ser medianoche cuando volvieron. | It must have been midnight when they returned. | imperfect (← *era*) |

## **16.2 *Tener que***

**Rule:** Interchangeable in most contexts with *deber*, **tener que** states a **strong obligation viewed as a necessity** — comparable to 'have to'. In the **preterite**, it means 'had to' (and did). Also used without the sense of obligation to state that you have something 'to do'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tendrás que cambiar de avión en París. | You will have to change planes in Paris. |
| Tuvo que presentar la tesis de nuevo. | He had to present the thesis again. |
| Tengo mucho que leer. | I have a lot to read. (no obligation) |

## **16.3 *Haber***

**Rule:** Beyond its use as an auxiliary in compound tenses (see 10.9), *haber* has two modal uses:

**(a) Hay que / había que / habrá que / hubo que** — **impersonal obligation** ('one must', 'it is necessary to'). The preterite (*hubo que*) states necessity that was fulfilled.

**(b) Haber de** + infinitive — milder personal obligation, or a statement of what *will* or *is expected* to happen. Less common nowadays. In parts of Latin America also used for supposition (like *deber de*).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Hay que lavar las sábanas. | The sheets need washing. | *hay que* — present |
| Había que hacer cola para entrar. | You had to queue to get in. | *había que* — imperfect |
| Habrá que prestar atención. | We'll have to pay attention. | *habrá que* — future |
| Hubo que romper la ventana para entrar. | It was necessary to break the window to get in. | *hubo que* — preterite (done) |
| ¿Qué hemos de hacer? | What are we to do? | *haber de* — mild obligation |
| Juan ha de partir después de la cena. | Juan will leave after dinner. | *haber de* — expectation |
| Han de ser las seis. [LA] | It must be six o'clock. | *haber de* — supposition [LA] |

## **16.4 *Querer***

**Rule:** As a modal before an infinitive, *querer* means 'to want'. Polite requests use the **-ra imperfect subjunctive** (*quisiera*) or the **conditional** (*querría*).

**Tense meaning distinctions:**
- **Imperfect** (*quería*) → 'wanted to' (state)
- **Positive preterite** (*quise*) → 'tried to' (attempted action)
- **Negative preterite** (*no quiso*) → 'refused to'

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ¿Quieres consultar el índice? | Do you want to consult the index? | present — want |
| Quisiera/querría ver el último modelo. | I should like to see the latest model. | polite request |
| Siempre queríamos ir a España. | We always wanted to go to Spain. | imperfect — wanted |
| Quise pedirle un autógrafo pero no lo permitieron. | I wanted (and tried) to ask her for an autograph, but they did not allow it. | preterite — tried |
| No quiso entrar en detalles. | He refused to give details. | negative preterite — refused |

## **16.5 *Poder***

### **16.5.1 Ability and permission**

**Rule:** *Poder* corresponds to 'can', 'may', 'be able to'. The tense carries precise meaning — this is one of the most common translation traps in Spanish.

| Tense | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present | can, may | *Usted puede pagar mañana.* — You may pay tomorrow. |
| Perfect | has been able to | *No ha podido encontrar a su mamá.* |
| Conditional | could (future/hypothetical) | *¿Podríamos dormir aquí?* — Could we sleep here? |
| Imperfect | could, was able to (state, no result implied) | *No podía desmentir la noticia.* |
| Positive preterite | managed to | *Pudimos atravesar el río nadando.* |
| Negative preterite | failed to / could not (and didn't) | *No pudimos abrir la puerta.* |

**Translating English 'could':** English 'could' maps to three different Spanish forms — preterite (*pudo*), imperfect (*podía*), and conditional (*podría*). The conditional refers to future time only and cannot replace the imperfect for past states.

| English | Spanish | Note |
|---|---|---|
| I could go tomorrow. | Podría ir mañana. | conditional — future |
| He was so drunk he couldn't stay on his feet. | Estaba tan borracho que no podía mantenerse de pie. | imperfect — past state |
| We managed to cross the river. | Pudimos atravesar el río. | preterite — achieved |

*Other uses:* **poder con** = 'to cope with / manage'; **no poder menos que** = 'cannot fail to / have no choice but'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No sé si pueden salvarlos. | I don't know if they can rescue them. |
| ¿Se puede (entrar)? | Can I come in? |
| Podrían hacerlo si quisieran. | They could do it if they wanted. |
| ¿Puedes con todos esos libros? | Can you manage all those books? |
| No pude menos que sentirme profundamente agradecido. | I could not fail to be profoundly grateful. |

### **16.5.2 Possibility and supposition**

**Rule:** The **conditional** of *poder* also expresses **possibility** — 'might', 'could'. Both the conditional and the preterite of **poder + haber + past participle** speculate on what *could have happened* but didn't.

**Puede ser que** / **puede que** + subjunctive = 'it is possible that', 'may'. *Ser* may be omitted unless *puede* is negated.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No hagas eso, podrías caer. | Don't do that, you might fall. |
| ¡Qué susto! Nos podríamos haber salido de la carretera. | What a fright! We could have come off the road. |
| Pudiste haberte desviado del sendero. | You could have strayed from the path. |
| Puede ser que hayan estado intentando llamarnos. | They may have been trying to call us. |
| Puede que vengan mañana. | They may come tomorrow. |

## **16.6 *Saber***

**Rule:** As a modal auxiliary, **saber** corresponds to 'can' / 'could' but **only for learned abilities** — it often translates 'to know how to'. It does not express physical ability or permission (use *poder* for those).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Sabes jugar al ajedrez? | Can you play chess? |
| Mario no sabía nadar. | Mario couldn't swim. |

### CMP: *Saber* vs *poder* — 'can'

**Rule:** Both *saber* and *poder* can translate English 'can', but they are not interchangeable.

| | *saber* | *poder* |
|---|---|---|
| Type of ability | learned skill ('know how to') | physical / circumstantial ability |
| Permission | ✗ | ✓ |
| Possibility | ✗ | ✓ |
| Context | permanent acquired skill | situational |

| Spanish | English | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| ¿Sabes nadar? | Can you swim? (do you know how?) | *saber* — learned skill |
| ¿Puedes nadar hoy? | Can you swim today? (are you able to right now?) | *poder* — circumstances |
| No sé conducir. | I can't drive. (never learned) | *saber* |
| Hoy no puedo conducir. | I can't drive today. (leg injury etc.) | *poder* |

## **16.7 *Soler***

**Rule:** Followed by an infinitive, **soler** means 'usually (do)', 'to be accustomed to'. Normally found **only in the present and imperfect tenses**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Suelen alojarse en la Pensión Angelita. | They usually stay in the Pensión Angelita. |
| Solía dar un paseo antes de cenar. | I normally went for a stroll before dinner. |
