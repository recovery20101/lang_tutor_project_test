# Chapter 25: Relative clauses

A relative clause plays a **modifying role**, qualifying the noun or pronoun known as the **antecedent**. Spanish relative clauses differ from English in two key ways.

**Table 25.1 — Spanish relative words:**

| Type | Word(s) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Pronoun (invariable) | **que** | that / who / which |
| Pronoun | **quien, quienes** | who / whom (persons only) |
| Pronoun | **el que, la que, los que, las que** | who / whom / which |
| Pronoun | **el cual, la cual, los cuales, las cuales** | who / whom / which |
| Pronoun (neuter) | **lo que, lo cual** | what / which |
| Adverb | **donde** | where |
| Adverb | **cuando** | when |
| Adjective | **cuyo, cuya, cuyos, cuyas** | whose |
| Adj./pronoun | **cuanto, cuanta, cuantos, cuantas** | as much/many as |

*Note: **como** 'how' can also be a relative adverb, but only after **manera**: *No le gustaba la manera como le hablaban* (usually replaced by *la manera en que* in speech).*

## **25.1 Differences between Spanish and English relative clauses**

### **25.1.1 The relative pronoun can never be omitted**

**Rule:** In English, the relative pronoun is often omitted ('The man we saw'). In Spanish, it can **never** be omitted — *que* is always required.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El hombre que vimos en la playa está aquí. | The man (that) we saw at the beach is here. |

### **25.1.2 No stranded prepositions**

**Rule:** In English, a preposition can be 'stranded' at the end of a relative clause ('the man we were talking about'). In Spanish, the preposition must be placed **immediately before** the relative pronoun.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Allí está el hombre de quien hablábamos. | There's the man (who) we were talking about. |

## **25.2 Restrictive and non-restrictive relative clauses**

**Rule:** A **restrictive** clause defines a subgroup within the antecedent group — 'only those who are tired'. A **non-restrictive** clause adds information without restricting — 'all children, who happen to be tired'. Non-restrictive clauses are set off by **commas** (written) or **pauses** (spoken). English 'that' is only used in restrictive clauses.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Los niños que estaban cansados tuvieron que ir a la cama. | The children who were tired had to go to bed. | restrictive — only the tired ones |
| Los niños, que estaban cansados, tuvieron que ir a la cama. | The children, who were tired, had to go to bed. | non-restrictive — all children |

## **25.4 A basic system**

### **25.4.1 Translating 'that' (restrictive clauses)**

**Rule:** English 'that' (not with a stranded preposition) = **que**. If 'that' introduces a clause with a stranded preposition, restructure first: 'the house that he lived in' → 'the house in which he lived' → see 25.4.3.1(b).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La comida que te dan es asquerosa. | The food that they give you is horrible. |
| Me lo dijo el abogado que lleva el caso. | The lawyer that is handling the case told me. |

### **25.4.2 Translating 'who' and 'whom'**

**Rule summary:**

| Context | Use |
|---|---|
| 'who' = subject, restrictive | **que** |
| 'who/whom' = direct object, restrictive | **a + el/la/los/las que** (safer than *que*) |
| 'whom' after preposition, restrictive | **quien/quienes** or **el que** series |
| 'who/whom' in non-restrictive clause | **que** (as above); *quien/quienes* possible in formal Spanish |

**Warning:** Do NOT automatically translate 'who' as *quien*. *Quien* without a preposition is almost always wrong: ~~el niño quien vimos~~, ~~la chica quien quiere hablar~~.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| No conozco a los inquilinos que viven abajo. | I don't know the tenants who live downstairs. | who = subject → *que* |
| Esta es la chica a la que detuvieron. | This is the girl who(m) they arrested. | who = object → *a la que* |
| La chica con quien iba a compartir el piso ha perdido su trabajo. | The girl with whom I was going to share the flat has lost her job. | whom after preposition → *quien* |
| La persona de la que están hablando es un vecino mío. | The person they are talking about is a neighbour of mine. | whom after preposition → *de la que* |
| Ellos, que siempre han vivido en el centro, se han comprado una casa en las afueras. | They, who have always lived in the centre, have bought a house on the outskirts. | non-restrictive → *que* |
| Al final pude localizar a Pedro, quien en aquella época trabajaba en Sevilla. | In the end I managed to locate Pedro, who at that time was working in Seville. | non-restrictive formal → *quien* |

### **25.4.3 Translating 'which'**

**Rule:**

| Context | Use |
|---|---|
| 'which' without preposition, restrictive | **que** |
| 'which' after preposition, restrictive | **el que** series |
| 'which' after genderless antecedent (*algo*, *nada*, *lo + adj.*) | **lo que** (neuter) |
| 'which' without preposition, non-restrictive | **que** |
| 'which' after preposition, non-restrictive | **el que** series |
| 'which' referring to whole preceding clause | **lo que** or **lo cual** |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No sé dónde he metido los pendientes que me compraste. | I don't know where I've put the earrings which you bought me. |
| Son cosas con las que la mayoría de la gente solamente puede soñar. | They are things about which most people can only dream. |
| No hay nada por lo que tienes que estar preocupado. | There is nothing about which you need to be worried. |
| Lo único con lo que está contento es con sus trenecitos. | The only thing he is happy with is his toy trains. |
| Todavía recuerdo esos sermones, que tuvimos que aguantar durante una semana. | I still remember those lectures, which we had to put up with for a week. |
| Han retrasado todos los vuelos, lo que hace pensar que hay un problema grave. | They've delayed all the flights, which makes you think there is a serious problem. |
| Les han dado permiso para construir, con lo cual no estamos muy conformes. | They've given them planning permission, which we aren't very happy about. |

### **25.4.4 Translating 'when'**

**Rule:**
- **Restrictive clause:** *cuando* cannot be used. Use **en + que**, or just **que** colloquially.
- **Non-restrictive clause:** *cuando* is normal.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Type |
|---|---|---|
| El mes (en) que más llueve aquí es octubre. | The month when it rains most here is October. | restrictive — *en que* |
| Incluso las noches que refrescaba no podía dormir. | Even on those nights when it got cooler I couldn't sleep. | restrictive — *que* |
| Lo haremos la semana que viene, cuando vengan los primos. | We'll do it next week, when your cousins come. | non-restrictive — *cuando* |

### **25.4.5 Translating 'where'**

**Rule:** *Donde* translates 'where' in both restrictive and non-restrictive clauses. A preposition may precede *donde* to indicate direction (*a*, *hacia*), source (*de*, *desde*), or location (*en*, *por*). *A + donde* → **adonde**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cómo se llama la tienda donde compramos tus gafas de sol? | What is the name of the shop where we bought your sunglasses? |
| Como él era del pueblo adonde iba, ofreció llevarme en su furgoneta. | As he was from the village I was going to, he offered to take me in his van. |
| Por fin llegamos a la cima, desde donde había unas vistas espectaculares. | We finally reached the summit, from where there were spectacular views. |

### **25.4.6 Translating 'why' (restrictive only)**

**Rule:** 'Why' as a relative adverb is translated by **por + la que** (for antecedent *la razón*).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No se sabe la razón por la que se fue. | The reason why he went away is not known. |

### **25.4.7 Translating 'whose'**

**Rule:** English 'whose' = **cuyo** — a possessive adjective agreeing in number and gender with the **thing possessed** (not the possessor). *Cuyo* can have human or non-human antecedents. *En cuyo caso* = 'in which case'. In informal speech, *cuyo* is replaced by *que* + possessive adjective.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El dueño, cuyo mayor interés era no ofender a la clientela, no pudo oponerse. | The owner, whose main concern was not to offend the customers, could not object. |
| Vi varias casas a cuyas ventanas les faltaba el cristal. | I saw several houses the windows of which had no glass. |
| La policía ha detenido a un hombre cuya identidad y domicilio se desconocen. | The police have arrested a man whose identity and abode is not known. |
| Este es el chico que su padre es un ministro. | [informal] This is the boy whose father is a minister. |
| Este es el chico cuyo padre es un ministro. | [formal] This is the boy whose father is a minister. |

## **25.5 Alternatives to the basic system**

### **25.5.1 *El que* and *el cual***

**Rule:** *El que* and *el cual* series are virtually identical grammatically but differ in frequency and register. In the Iberian Peninsula, *el cual* is much less common in speech; in Latin America the reverse may apply. *El cual* can replace *el que* in all the uses of 25.4 — it tends to be more formal or literary.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cómo se llama el hombre con el cual hemos hablado esta mañana? | What is the name of the man we spoke to this morning? |
| Hubo un intento de calmar a los manifestantes, muchos de los cuales desconfiaban de la policía. | An attempt was made to pacify the protesters, many of whom distrusted the police. |
| Hojeé las páginas del libro, entre las cuales encontré una postal escrita por ella. | I flicked through the pages of the book, among which I found a postcard written by her. |

### **25.5.2 *Que* after a preposition**

**Rule:** With *en* (and to a lesser extent *a*, *con*, *de*), a preposition may be directly followed by *que*. The safest option in the Peninsula is the *el que* series. Exception: after nouns denoting periods of time (*vez*, *día*, *año*), *en que* (or just *que*) is used directly.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es una obra de teatro en que nadie tiene un papel agradable. | It's a play in which nobody has a nice role. |
| Esta es la casa con que llevaban tanto tiempo soñando. | This is the house they had been dreaming of for so long. |
| La primera vez que la vi fue el verano pasado. | The first time I saw her was last summer. |
| el día (en) que nació Luis | the day Luis was born |

## **25.6 Focusing on words or phrases (cleft sentences)**

**Rule:** English uses 'it was X who/that ...' (cleft sentences). Spanish expresses this with *el que* / *quien* / relative adverbs. Structure: 'the one who did it was Juan' → **El que lo hizo fue Juan** — or more commonly with *ser* moved front: **Fue Juan el que lo hizo**. *Quien/quienes* is interchangeable with *el que* here. In the Peninsula, if the focused element follows a preposition (including personal *a*), the preposition must be **repeated** before the relative pronoun.

*Notes: ~~Fue Juan que lo hizo~~ is ungrammatical. If subject/complement is *nosotros/vosotros*, *ser* agrees with the pronoun. For Latin American alternatives, see 30.5.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Fue Juan el que lo hizo. | It was Juan who did it. |
| Fue Juan quien lo hizo. | It was Juan who did it. |
| Son ellos quienes/los que no quieren pagar. | It's they who don't want to pay. |
| Es la tuya la que se ha perdido. | It's yours that has been lost. |
| Es el garaje lo que han pintado. | It's the garage (that) they've painted. |
| Fue con la secretaria con la que hablé. | It was to the secretary that I spoke. |
| Es a María a la que han nombrado. | It's María they've nominated. |

### **25.6.2 *Cuando*, *donde*, *como* in focus constructions**

**Rule:** These relative adverbs work like *el que* / *quien* in cleft sentences. English: 'it was in June that he was born' → Spanish: **Fue en junio cuando nació**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Fue en septiembre cuando nos casamos. | It was in September that we got married. |
| Es en Alemania donde se celebrarán los mundiales. | It's in Germany that the World Cup will take place. |
| Es así como hay que hacerlo. | This is how you do it. |

## **25.7 *El que* and *quien* meaning 'anybody who', 'those who'**

**Rule:** Without an explicit antecedent, *el que* (in appropriate gender/number forms) means 'he/she/those who'. *Quien/quienes* similarly embody an indefinite antecedent ('anyone who', 'those who').

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El que estudie aprobará. | He who studies will pass. |
| Los que digan eso son unos reaccionarios. | Those who say that are reactionaries. |
| No hay quien te aguante. | There isn't anyone who can put up with you. |
| No faltaban quienes envidiaban su buena suerte. | Those who envied his good fortune were not lacking. |

## **25.8 *Cuanto* — 'as much/many as'**

**Rule:** *Cuanto* is a quantifying adjective/pronoun equivalent to *todo el que* / *todos los que* — 'all (those) who/that'. It agrees in number and gender as an adjective. As an invariable neuter pronoun it means 'everything (that)', occasionally reinforced with *todo*.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tomaremos cuantas medidas sean necesarias. | We will take all the necessary measures. |
| Bebe cuanta agua necesites. | Drink all the water you need. |
| Hice cuanto pude. | I did everything I could. |
| Les dimos todo cuanto teníamos. | We gave them everything we had. |

## **25.9 Relative clauses with an infinitive**

**Rule:** Particularly when the antecedent is an indefinite or negative pronoun (*algo*, *nada*), a relative clause may use an **infinitive** as its main verb. If the antecedent is also omitted, a **written accent** must be applied to the relative pronoun or adverb (*lo que* → *lo que* / *qué*, *donde* → *dónde*).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No tengo nada con lo que secarme las manos. | I haven't got anything to dry my hands with. |
| Esto me ha dado mucho en que pensar. | This has given me a lot to think about. |
| No tengo con qué lavarme el pelo. | I haven't got anything to wash my hair with. |
| No encontramos dónde dormir. | We couldn't find anywhere to sleep. |
