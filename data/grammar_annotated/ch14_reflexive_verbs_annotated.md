# Chapter 14: Reflexive verbs

A reflexive verb is used with an object pronoun referring back to the verb's subject (English '-self'/'-selves'). *For reflexive verbs with passive meaning, see 15.2.*

**Formation:** Reflexive pronouns change according to the subject and follow the same position rules as other weak pronouns — they follow and attach to infinitives, gerunds and positive commands (see 8.5).

*Note: Unlike English, the reflexive pronoun cannot be omitted in Spanish: **Me lavé antes de cenar** ('I washed before dinner').*

**Conjugation of *lavarse* (present tense):**

| Person | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| yo | me lavo | I wash myself |
| tú | te lavas | you wash yourself |
| vos [LA] / usted | te lavás [LA] / se lava | you wash yourself |
| él/ella | se lava | he/she washes him/herself |
| nosotros | nos lavamos | we wash ourselves |
| vosotros [SP] | os laváis | you wash yourselves |
| ustedes / ellos | se lavan | you/they wash yourselves/themselves |

## **14.2 Reflexive verbs with a reflexive meaning**

**Rule:** The action genuinely reflects back on the subject. Two sub-types: **(a)** the reflexive pronoun as **direct object** (the subject acts on itself), and **(b)** the reflexive pronoun as **indirect object** (the subject acts for its own benefit or on its own body part).

**(a) Reflexive pronoun as direct object:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Usted tendrá que cuidarse. | You will have to look after yourself. |
| Podía verse en el espejo. | She could see herself in the mirror. |

**(b) Reflexive pronoun as indirect object** (beneficiary / body part):

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ayer nos compramos un perrito. | Yesterday we bought ourselves a puppy. |
| Se ha conseguido un empleo. | He has found himself a job. |
| Andrea se ha torcido el tobillo. | Andrea has twisted her ankle. |

*See also 5.5 for the definite article with body parts.*

## **14.3 Reflexive verbs with a reciprocal meaning**

**Rule:** Plural reflexive verbs can express **reciprocal** action — 'each other' or 'one another'. Because the reflexive and reciprocal forms are identical, ambiguity may arise (e.g. *Se felicitaron* = 'They congratulated themselves' **or** 'They congratulated one another'). To clarify, add: **el uno al otro**, **(los) unos a (los) otros**, or **mutuamente**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Las dos profesoras se odian. | The two teachers hate each other. |
| Nos saludamos al salir de la iglesia. | We greeted one another as we came out of the church. |
| Nos presentamos el uno al otro. | We introduced ourselves to each other. |
| Los jugadores se felicitaron unos a otros. | The players congratulated one another. |

## **14.4 Reflexives with an indirect object pronoun**

**Rule:** In the 3rd person, **se** + indirect object pronoun creates a pattern where the Spanish indirect object corresponds to the English subject. Often used for **unplanned or unexpected** actions. The indirect object can also indicate an interested party (not the subject of the action). This construction is an alternative to the body-part reflexive in 14.2(b).

**Verbs commonly used this way:** *caerse*, *olvidarse*, *ocurrirse*, *perderse*, *romperse*.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Se le cayeron las botellas que llevaba. | He dropped the bottles he was carrying. | unplanned |
| Se nos ha agotado el aceite. | We've run out of oil. | unplanned |
| Se me han perdido los guantes. | I've lost my gloves. | unplanned |
| Se le llenaron los ojos de lágrimas. | Her eyes filled with tears. | interested party |
| Se te ha subido la temperatura. | Your temperature has risen. | interested party |
| Se me ha torcido el tobillo. | I've twisted my ankle. | alt. to 14.2(b) |

## **14.5 *Se* as an indefinite subject**

**Rule:** **Se** can act as an impersonal/indefinite subject equivalent to English 'one', 'we', 'they', 'you', 'people'. The Spanish verb is always **3rd person singular**. If the verb is already reflexive, the indefinite subject must be introduced as **uno/a** instead.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se cena muy tarde en España. | People have dinner late in Spain. |
| Se cierra los lunes. | We close on Mondays. |
| ¿Se puede pasar? | Can we come in? |
| Uno se aburre de leer libros largos. | One gets tired of reading long books. |

## **14.6 Reflexive verbs — 'to get/have something done'**

**Rule:** With a few verbs, the reflexive can express the idea of **getting something done** by someone else.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Quiero cortarme el pelo. | I want to get my hair cut. |
| Tuvo que ir a Francia para operarse. | He had to go to France to have an operation. |

## **14.7 Verbs reflexive in form but not in meaning**

**Rule:** Some common verbs are reflexive in form but their meaning is not reflexive. They must be learned individually.

**Common examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| abstenerse de hacer | to abstain from doing |
| arrepentirse de hacer algo | to regret doing something |
| atreverse a hacer | to dare to do |
| (com)portarse | to behave |
| constiparse | to catch a cold [SP] / become constipated [LA] |
| enfermarse [LA] | to fall ill |
| fugarse | to escape / run away |
| jactarse de | to boast about |
| quejarse de | to complain about |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me abstuve de votar. | I abstained from voting. |
| Se quejaron del mal servicio. | They complained about the bad service. |

### **14.8.1 Change-of-state verbs**

**Rule:** In reflexive form these verbs convey a **change of state** — often corresponding to English 'to become', 'to get', 'to go'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se asustan con el ruido de los animales. | They get frightened by the sound of animals. |
| En Canadá los lagos se hielan en invierno. | In Canada the lakes become frozen in winter. |
| La ropa todavía no se ha secado. | The clothes still haven't dried. |

**Table 14.1 — Common change-of-state pairs:**

| Transitive | Meaning | Reflexive | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| aburrir | to bore | aburrirse | to get bored |
| alegrar | to make happy | alegrarse | to become happy |
| asombrar | to amaze | asombrarse | to be amazed |
| asustar | to frighten | asustarse | to get frightened |
| casar | to marry (off) | casarse | to get married |
| dormir | to put to sleep | dormirse | to go to sleep |
| enfadar/enojar | to anger | enfadarse/enojarse | to get angry |
| entristecer | to sadden | entristecerse | to be saddened |
| fundir | to melt | fundirse | to become thawed |
| helar | to freeze | helarse | to freeze (over) |
| mojar | to make wet | mojarse | to get wet |
| molestar | to upset | molestarse | to get upset |
| ofender | to offend | ofenderse | to take offence |
| preocupar | to worry | preocuparse | to become worried |
| secar | to dry | secarse | to become dry |
| tranquilizar | to calm | tranquilizarse | to calm down |

### **14.8.2 Reflexive verbs describing what happens to an inanimate subject**

**Rule:** Particularly with inanimate subjects, the reflexive form of a transitive verb states what happens to the subject. English expresses this with 'get + past participle' or the simple past.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se hundieron las ruedas en el barro. | The wheels sank into/got stuck in the mud. |
| La comida se está quemando. | The food is burning/getting burned. |
| Al sacarlo, el vaso se rompió. | As it was taken out, the glass broke/got broken. |
| Las luces se apagan a medianoche. | The lights are/get switched off at midnight. |

### **14.8.3 Reflexive verbs of movement**

**Rule:** Some transitive verbs of movement and motion have reflexive forms used intransitively.

*Note: **Volverse** 'to turn round' is similar — but the non-reflexive **volver** is already intransitive ('to return'). In parts of Latin America, **regresarse** or **devolverse** are used for 'to return'.*

**Table 14.2 — Movement verb pairs:**

| Transitive | Meaning | Reflexive | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| acercar | to bring closer | acercarse | to approach |
| alejar | to move away | alejarse | to go away |
| meter | to put | meterse | to meddle in |
| mover | to move (something) | moverse | to move |
| parar | to stop (something) | pararse | to stop / stand up [LA] |
| retirar | to withdraw (something) | retirarse | to back away / retire |
| sentar | to seat | sentarse | to sit down |

**Example:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Al oírlo se retiró. | When he heard it he backed off. |

## **14.9 Verbs of becoming**

**Rule:** The main verbs of becoming are all reflexive. Each has specific usage constraints.

| Verb | Used with | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **convertirse en** | nouns only | to turn into | *Se está convirtiendo en una pesadilla.* |
| **hacerse** | nouns/adjectives | to become (deliberate effort) | *Se ha hecho millonaria.* |
| **ponerse** + adj. | adjectives (temporary states) | to get, to become | *ponerse enfadado/gordo/nublado* |
| **quedarse** + adj./part. | disability adjectives, some past participles | to go (permanently) | *quedarse ciego/sordo/dormido* |
| **volverse** + adj. | psychological adjectives | to become | *Andrés se ha vuelto muy antipático.* |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se está convirtiendo en una pesadilla. | It's turning into a nightmare. |
| Se ha hecho millonaria. | She's become a millionaire. |
| ponerse enfadado / gordo / nublado | to get angry / fat / cloudy |
| quedarse ciego / sordo / dormido | to go blind / deaf / to fall asleep |
| Andrés se ha vuelto muy antipático. | Andrés has become very unfriendly. |

## **14.10 Emphatic reflexive verbs**

### **14.10.1 Verbs with a meaning shift**

**Rule:** Making certain verbs reflexive produces a subtle difference in meaning — stressing **suddenness**, **unexpectedness**, or **deliberateness** of the action, or conveying a more emphatic version. Collected below as pairs.

| Non-reflexive | Meaning | Reflexive | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| bajar | to go down | bajarse | to get down/off, bend down |
| caer | to fall | caerse | to fall down/off, fall over |
| callar | to silence | callarse | to be quiet (used as imperative) |
| dejar | to leave/put | dejarse | to leave (behind, accidentally) |
| despedir | to see off, dismiss | despedirse (de) | to say goodbye (to) |
| estar | to be | estarse | to be still (in commands) |
| imaginar | to imagine, envisage | imaginarse | to imagine, suppose |
| ir | to go | irse | to go away |
| llevar | to take/carry | llevarse | to take away |
| marchar | to march | marcharse | to leave, go away |
| morir | to die (accidental) | morirse | to die (figurative or emphatic) |
| negar | to deny | negarse (a) | to refuse (to) |
| parecer | to seem, appear | parecerse a | to look like |
| quedar | to remain | quedarse | to stay on |
| reír | to laugh | reírse | to laugh (at) |
| saber | to know | saberse | to know, be able to recall |
| salir | to leave, come out | salirse | to come out/off, leak |
| subir | to go up, rise | subirse | to get in/on, climb on to |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La lluvia cayó todo el día. | The rain fell all day. |
| Al oírlo se cayó de la silla. | When he heard it he fell off his chair. |
| Bajaba la escalera. | He was going downstairs. |
| (Me) bajo en la próxima esquina. | I'm getting off at the next corner. |
| ¡Déjalo aquí! | Leave it here! |
| Me dejé el libro en el metro. | I left the book on the metro (accidentally). |
| Nunca llegué a despedirme de ella. | I never got to say goodbye to her. |
| ¡Estate quieto! | Be still! |
| Me imagino que sí. | I suppose so. |
| Ya se habían ido los muchachos. | The boys had already gone. |
| Tras el ataque se llevaron un botín enorme. | After the attack they carried off an enormous booty. |
| Nos marchamos. | We are leaving. |
| Me muero si lo ven. | I'll die if they see it. |
| Se niegan a quedar con nosotros. | They refuse to meet us. |
| Martín se parece a Antonio. | Martín looks like Antonio. |
| Decidió quedarse otro mes. | She decided to stay on another month. |
| Se sabe los nombres de todos los jugadores. | She knows the names of all the players. |
| El avión se salió de la pista. | The plane came off the runway. |
| (Nos) subimos al tren en Córdoba. | We got on to the train in Córdoba. |
| ¡Cállate! | Be quiet! |
| Miguel se ríe de todos. | Miguel laughs at everyone. |

### **14.10.2 Emphatic reflexive with verbs of consumption**

**Rule:** The emphatic reflexive is especially common with verbs of **eating and drinking**, stressing **quantity or speed** of consumption.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se lo bebió de un trago. | He drank it in one gulp. |
| ¡Te has zampado el paquete entero! | You've guzzled the entire packet! |
