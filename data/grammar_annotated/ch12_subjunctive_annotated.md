# Chapter 12: The subjunctive mood

The subjunctive mood in modern Spanish is far more alive than in English. Whilst options sometimes exist between indicative and subjunctive, this choice almost always implies a change of meaning.

**Key characteristics:**
- Most frequent in **subordinate clauses** joined to a main clause in the indicative.
- The indicative affirms facts; the subjunctive is required for hypotheses, future possibilities, uncertainty, doubts, and desires — though this distinction is not absolute.
- Four tenses in common use: **present**, **perfect**, **imperfect** and **pluperfect** (for forms, see Chapter 10).

**Two core rules for que clauses:**
1. The subjunctive is generally only used in the subordinate clause when the **subject differs** from the main clause. Same subject → infinitive: *Quiere que yo venga* (subjunctive) vs *Quiere venir* (infinitive).
2. English infinitive constructions like 'I asked him to go' become **main clause + que + subjunctive**: *Le pedí que se fuera ayer*. Similarly **que** + subjunctive may correspond to an English gerund: *Insisto en que lo haga Antonio* = 'I insist on Antonio doing it'.

## **12.1 Subjunctive in subordinate *que* clauses**

**Rule:** The subjunctive occurs most frequently in subordinate clauses introduced by **que**. Compare: *Dudo que vengan* (subjunctive) 'I doubt they will come' vs *Sé que vendrán* (indicative) 'I know they will come'.

### **12.1.1 Subjunctive after verbs of influence**

**Rule:** Used after verbs which **request, command, order, want, desire, propose, suggest, permit, allow, approve, encourage, cause** and their negatives (**avoid, prohibit, prevent, forbid**). The influence may also be expressed by a noun phrase: *el deseo de que*, *la orden de que*.

**Alternative with infinitive:** Some verbs (*mandar*, *impedir*, *permitir*) can take an infinitive even when subjects differ (see 17.1.2). Verbs requiring **a** before an infinitive (*animar a*, *ayudar a*, *forzar a*, *obligar a*, *invitar a*, *persuadir a*) can use the infinitive as an alternative to *a que* + subjunctive.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ordenó que la flota zarpara. | He ordered the fleet to set sail. |
| Quería que lloviera. | I wanted it to rain. |
| Propongo que se anule el contrato. | I propose that the contract be annulled. |
| No se puede permitir que termine. | It cannot be permitted to end. |
| Hay que hacer que cambien de opinión. | We must make them change their mind. |
| No podemos impedir que ella se vaya. | We can't prevent her from going. |
| su deseo de que se separaran | her wish that they should separate |
| Me obligaron a pagarlo. | They forced me to pay it. |
| Les he invitado a venir. | I have invited them to come. |

### **12.1.2 Subjunctive after verbs of doubt, denial or uncertainty**

**Rule:** Used after **dudar** 'to doubt' and **negar** 'to deny'. When these verbs are used **negatively**, they lose their doubt/uncertainty and take the **indicative**. Conversely, other verbs (like *creer*, *decir*, *parecer*) only trigger the subjunctive when used **negatively**. In Latin America, the subjunctive can also follow phrases placing a limitation on certainty.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Dudo que venga el fontanero. | I doubt whether the plumber will come. | subjunctive — doubt |
| Negó que hubiera ocurrido un error. | He denied that an error had occurred. | subjunctive — denial |
| No negó que hubo un error. | He didn't deny there had been a mistake. | indicative — negated *negar* |
| No creo que vaya a quedarse contenta. | I don't think she will be pleased. | subjunctive — negated *creer* |
| No es que hayan aceptado la propuesta. | It is not that they have accepted the proposal. | subjunctive — negated *es que* |
| No me parece que sea importante. | It does not seem important to me. | subjunctive — negated *parecer* |
| Casi es seguro que renuncie. [LA] | It is almost certain that he will give up. | subjunctive — limited certainty [LA] |

**Other negative phrases requiring subjunctive:** *no está claro que*, *no es verdad que*, *no es seguro que*, *no significa que*.

### **12.1.3 Subjunctive after verbs of feeling or personal opinion**

**Rule:** Used after phrases expressing feelings, emotions or opinions. There are four basic patterns. Note: the subjunctive is **not** used after phrases that are factual and express no feeling: *Está claro que está mintiendo* (indicative).

**(a) Ser/estar + adjective + que** (impersonal 'it is...'):

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es aburrido que pongan tanto fútbol en la tele. | It's boring that they have so much football on TV. |
| Era importante que lo leyeran con cuidado. | It was important that they read it carefully. |

Common phrases: *es mejor que*, *está bien/mal que*, *es una pena que*, *es una vergüenza que*, *es imprescindible que*, *es posible/probable que*, *puede ser que*, *más vale que*, *hace falta que*.

**(b) Verb of emotional response + que** (subject expressed as indirect object):

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me gustaría que lo arreglara ahora. | I would like you to fix it now. |
| Me sorprende que lo hagan tan mal. | I am surprised that they are so bad at doing it. |

Other verbs: *alegrar*, *asustar*, *encantar*, *enfadar*, *molestar*.

**(c) Verbs like esperar, odiar, preferir, sentir, temer** (subject is same in both clauses):

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Espero que apruebe el examen. | I hope he passes the exam. |
| Siento que haya estado enferma. | I am sorry she has been ill. |

**(d) De que + subjunctive** — after certain reflexive verbs (*alegrarse*, *sorprenderse*) and *tener miedo*:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se sorprendió de que hubiera tanta gente. | He was surprised that there were so many people. |
| Tendrá miedo de que me haya perdido. | She will be afraid that I have got lost. |

### **12.1.4 Subjunctive after relative pronouns with negative or unknown antecedents**

**Rule:** The subjunctive is used when the relative pronoun refers to something in the main clause that **does not exist** (negative antecedent) or **is not yet known/identified** (unknown antecedent). With a known antecedent, the indicative is used.

#### 12.1.4.1 Negative antecedent

**Rule:** When the antecedent in the main clause is negative (no one, nothing, no X), the relative clause takes the subjunctive.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No tienen ningún vestido que me guste. | They don't have any dresses that I like. |
| No conozco a nadie que pueda hacerlo. | I don't know anyone who can do it. |

#### 12.1.4.2 Unknown antecedent — subjunctive vs indicative

**Rule:** With an **unknown** antecedent (the speaker has not yet identified a specific item), the subjunctive is used. With a **known** antecedent (a specific item in mind), the indicative is used. The English translation is often identical, but the Spanish meaning differs. The relative clause can be introduced by **que**, **cuyo**, **como**, **cuando**, **cuanto**, **donde**, **quien**, **lo que**, **el/la/los/las que**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Lucas quiere subir una montaña que **mida** más de 3000 metros. | Lucas wants to climb a mountain that is more than 3000 metres high. | subjunctive — any mountain, unidentified |
| Lucas quiere subir una montaña que **mide** más de 3000 metros. | Lucas wants to climb a mountain that is more than 3000 metres high. | indicative — a specific mountain |
| ¿Conoces a algún vecino que me ayude? | Do you know a local who can help me? | unknown — subjunctive |
| Los libros que no tengan cubierta han de ser retirados. | Books which have no cover are to be withdrawn. | unknown — subjunctive |
| Necesito a alguien que limpie la casa. | I need someone to clean the house. | unknown — subjunctive |
| Colócalos como te parezca mejor. | Arrange them as you see fit. | unknown — subjunctive |
| Ven cuando quieras. | Come whenever you want. | unknown — subjunctive |
| Debes buscar un país donde el clima sea templado. | You must find a country where the climate is mild. | unknown — subjunctive |
| Escribe lo que te dé la gana. | Write whatever you want. | unknown — subjunctive |
| Compra el que más te guste. | Buy whichever one you like best. | unknown — subjunctive |

#### 12.1.4.3 After cualquiera, dondequiera, quienquiera

**Rule:** Of the **-quiera** forms, only **cualquiera**, **dondequiera** and **quienquiera** have common currency. *comoquiera* and *cuandoquiera* are replaced by *como* and *cuando* (see 12.1.4.2).

- After **cualquiera** (as pronoun) and **quienquiera** → always **subjunctive**.
- After **dondequiera** → **indicative** for past or habitual; **subjunctive** for unknown or future.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| quienquiera que sea responsable | whoever is/may be to blame | subjunctive |
| cualquiera que sea su decisión | whatever his decision is/may be | subjunctive |
| dondequiera que usted se encuentre | wherever you are/may be | subjunctive — unknown |
| dondequiera que ella iba | wherever she went | indicative — habitual past |

#### 12.1.4.4 After *por (muy)* and *por mucho* — 'however (much)'

**Rule:** **Por (muy)** + adjective/adverb and **por** + **mucho/a/os/as** (or **más**) + noun/verb express 'however (much/many)'. Subjunctive for present/future situations; indicative for habitual or past events.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| por (muy) caro que sea | however expensive it is | subjunctive — present |
| por mucho que grites | however much you shout | subjunctive — present/future |
| por mucho que estudió | however hard he studied | indicative — past |
| por muchos trofeos que ganen | however many cups they win | subjunctive — future |

## **12.2 Subjunctive required by certain subordinating conjunctions**

### **12.2.1 Conjunctions always followed by the subjunctive**

#### 12.2.1.1 Purpose conjunctions

**Rule:** **Para que** 'so that', **a fin de que**, **con el objeto de que**, **con el propósito de que** (all meaning 'in order that') always take the subjunctive.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Lo pondré aquí para que nadie lo robe. | I will put it here so that no one will steal it. |

#### 12.2.1.2 Proviso conjunctions

**Rule:** **Con tal (de) que** 'provided that', **a condición de que** 'on condition that' always take the subjunctive. *Note: **siempre que** requires the subjunctive when meaning 'provided that' but takes the indicative when meaning 'whenever' (see 12.2.2.1).*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Lo explicaré de nuevo con tal (de) que todos se callen. | I shall explain it again provided that everyone keeps quiet. |
| siempre que no llueva | provided it doesn't rain |

#### 12.2.1.3 'Unless' conjunctions

**Rule:** **A no ser que** and **a menos que** ('unless') always take the subjunctive. **Excepto que** and **salvo que** also mean 'unless' with the subjunctive; but when they mean 'except for the fact that', they take the indicative.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| No vamos a poder moverlo a no ser que alguien nos ayude. | We aren't going to be able to move it unless someone helps us. | subjunctive |
| salvo que él insista | unless he insists | subjunctive |
| Es un buen muchacho salvo que miente. | He is a good lad except that he tells lies. | indicative |

**Rule:** **Sin que** always takes the subjunctive. In negative sentences it means 'unless'.

**Examples:**

#### 12.2.1.5 Supposition conjunctions

**Rule:** **En caso de que** 'in case' always takes the subjunctive. **Suponiendo que** takes the subjunctive for a hypothesis and the indicative when accepting something as fact.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Sal temprano en caso de que haya un atasco. | Leave early in case there is a traffic jam. | subjunctive |
| suponiendo que lleguen a tiempo | assuming they arrive in time | subjunctive — hypothesis |
| suponiendo que ha sido él | assuming it was him | indicative — accepted as fact |

### **12.2.2 Conjunctions followed by subjunctive or indicative**

#### 12.2.2.1 Temporal conjunctions

**Rule:** Common temporal conjunctions: **a medida que**, **cuando**, **desde que**, **después (de) que**, **en cuanto**, **hasta que**, **siempre que** (whenever), **una vez (que)**. With the exception of **antes (de) que**, the subjunctive is only used when the subordinate verb refers to **future time**. For past or habitual, use the indicative.

**Special case — mientras:** Generally takes the indicative even for future time when it cannot be translated as 'as long as'. Takes the subjunctive when 'while' and 'as long as' are both possible translations.

*Note: After **esperar/aguardar** 'to wait', **a que** commonly replaces **hasta que** when the subjunctive is required.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Siguieron luchando hasta que se rindieron los enemigos. | They continued to fight until their enemies surrendered. | indicative — past |
| Debemos seguir luchando hasta que nuestros enemigos se rindan. | We must continue fighting until our enemies surrender. | subjunctive — future |
| Cuando sale el equipo les dan una calurosa bienvenida. | Whenever the team comes out they give them a warm welcome. | indicative — habitual |
| Cuando salga el equipo les darán una calurosa bienvenida. | When the team comes out they will give them a warm welcome. | subjunctive — future |
| No me ha hablado desde que llegó. | He has not spoken to me since he arrived. | indicative — past |
| Una vez que llegue, vamos a discutirlo. | Once he arrives we will discuss it. | subjunctive — future |
| Voy a estudiar mientras duermes al niño. | I'm going to study while you get the baby off to sleep. | indicative — cannot = 'as long as' |
| Mientras viva no va a abdicar la corona. | As long as she lives she will not give up the crown. | subjunctive — 'as long as' possible |

#### 12.2.2.2 Concessive conjunctions — *aunque*

**Rule:** **Aunque** (most common concessive conjunction) usually takes the **indicative** when the clause is factually true ('although') and the **subjunctive** when the clause is hypothetical ('even if'). However, **aunque** + subjunctive can also be factual when something is expressed as contrary to expectation.

Other concessive conjunctions following the same pattern: **a pesar de que**, **aun cuando**, **si bien**, and literary **así** and **siquiera**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Le pagan poco, aunque él nunca se queja. | They don't pay him much, although he never complains. | indicative — factual |
| Vamos a nadar aunque llueva. | We are going to swim even if it rains. | subjunctive — hypothetical |
| No iría a su fiesta aunque él me invitara personalmente. | I wouldn't go to his party even if he invited me personally. | subjunctive — hypothetical |
| Tiene que pensar en ello aunque sea todavía muy joven. | He has to start thinking about it, even if he is still very young. | subjunctive — contrary to expectation |
| No aceptaría la resolución a pesar de que la mayoría la adoptara. | He would not accept the resolution even if the majority adopted it. | subjunctive — a pesar de que |

#### 12.2.2.3 Purpose and result conjunctions

**Rule:** **De manera/modo/forma que** (and similar) express **result** with the indicative and **purpose** with the subjunctive.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| ¡Escóndelo en el cajón de modo que nadie lo descubra! | Hide it in the drawer so that no one finds it. | subjunctive — purpose |
| Lo escondió en el cajón de modo que nadie lo descubrió. | He hid it in the drawer with the result that no one found it. | indicative — result |

#### 12.2.2.4 Conditional conjunctions

**Rule:** **Si** 'if' is discussed in Chapter 13. **Como** means 'as' with the indicative, but is **conditional** ('if') when followed by the subjunctive.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Como no salgamos en los próximos cinco minutos vamos a perder el avión. | If we don't leave in the next five minutes we're going to miss the flight. | subjunctive — conditional como |

#### 12.2.2.5 Cause conjunctions — *porque*

**Rule:** **Porque** 'because' takes the indicative unless it is **negated** (in which case it triggers the subjunctive). Note: *porque* can follow a negated clause without being negated itself — in that case it still takes the indicative.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Se quejó porque la habitación estaba sucia. | He complained because the room was dirty. | indicative |
| No se quejó porque la habitación estuviera sucia, sino porque no había agua caliente. | He didn't complain because the room was dirty, but because there was no hot water. | subjunctive — negated porque |
| No me cae bien porque es muy cínico. | I don't like him because he is very cynical. | indicative — porque itself not negated |

### **12.3.1 Subjunctive after words meaning 'perhaps'**

**Rule:** After **tal vez** (*talvez* [LA]), **quizá(s)**, and **posiblemente**, the subjunctive (rather than the indicative) expresses a **less likely** possibility. For future time, the present subjunctive is most common, though the future indicative is also found. **A lo mejor** 'perhaps' is very common in speech and takes the **indicative**. **Acaso** is more formal, rare in speech, and usually takes the subjunctive.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tal vez no ha/haya pensado en ir. | Maybe he has not thought of going. | indicative = more likely; subjunctive = less likely |
| Quizás al final se pongan/se pondrán de acuerdo. | Perhaps in the end they will agree. | subj. or future indicative |
| A lo mejor viene. | Perhaps he'll come. | indicative — a lo mejor |

### **12.3.2 Subjunctive to exclaim a wish**

**Rule:** The subjunctive is used after **ojalá (que)** with the meaning of 'I hope', 'if only', 'I wish'. The imperfect subjunctive implies the wish is **less likely** to be fulfilled.

**Quién** + imperfect subjunctive expresses **envy** ('I wish I had...').

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ¡Ojalá que encuentres a tu amiga! | I hope you find your friend. | present subj. — possible wish |
| ¡Ojalá viniera! | If only he would come! | imperfect subj. — less likely |
| ¡Quién tuviera su suerte! | I wish I had their luck! | envy |

## **12.4 The sequence of tenses — which subjunctive tense to use**

**Rule:** The distribution of subjunctive tenses corresponds broadly to their indicative counterparts. These are common patterns, not fixed rules — context may require departures.

| Verb in main clause | Subjunctive in subordinate clause |
|---|---|
| present, future, perfect indicative | present or perfect subjunctive |
| imperfect, preterite, conditional, pluperfect indicative | imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive |
| any form of command | present subjunctive |

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me sorprende que haya cancelado. | I am surprised that he has cancelled. |
| No voy a sugerir que sea así. | I will not suggest that it may be so. |
| Le dije que no me molestara más. | I told her to stop bothering me. |
| ¡Pídele que salga! | Ask him to leave! |
| Es una pena que estuvieras enfermo. | It is a pity that you were ill. |
| ¿Qué hizo para que digas eso? | What did he do that makes you say that? |

## **12.5 Additional uses of the *-ra* form of the imperfect subjunctive**

*For the **-ra** form as an alternative to the conditional with **querer**, **deber**, **poder**, see Chapter 16. For **hubiera** + past participle replacing the conditional perfect, see 13.1.2.2.*

### **12.5.1 Imperfect subjunctive -ra for preterite or pluperfect indicative**

**Rule:** The **-ra** form of the imperfect subjunctive is sometimes used in place of a past indicative tense. Primarily found in the press and literary contexts in Spain, but more widely in Latin America. Most commonly in (i) relative clauses and (ii) after temporal conjunctions **después (de) que**, **luego que** and **desde que**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La iniciativa vino en una carta que dirigiera al presidente. | The initiative came in a letter he had sent to the president. |
| Volvió a la que fuera su casa antes del temblor. | She returned to what had been her house before the earthquake. |
| Realizó cambios fundamentales desde que fuera elegido. [LA] | He carried out fundamental changes after his election. |

## **12.6 The future subjunctive**

**Rule:** The future subjunctive in Spanish is virtually obsolete and unlikely to be used by foreign learners. It may be encountered in outmoded phrases, and in legal, ecclesiastical or official written language, and possibly in journalistic style in parts of Latin America.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| sea lo que fuere | whatever it might be |
| venga lo que viniere | come what may |
