# Chapter 9: Indefinite adjectives, pronouns and adverbs

Indefinites are used to: (i) quantify imprecisely — 'some', 'a few', 'several', 'enough', 'many'; (ii) distinguish one item from another — 'a certain', 'such a', 'other'; (iii) relate items to each other — 'both', 'each', 'the rest'.

**Agreement:** Spanish indefinites must be used in the correct variable (gender and number) or invariable forms.

**Indefinite pronouns referring to persons:** When used as pronouns, many indefinites are assumed to refer to persons unless otherwise specified. Plural forms can combine with 1st or 2nd person plural verbs: *Muchos teníamos miedo* 'Many of us were frightened'. When used as the direct object of a verb, indefinite pronouns referring to persons require the personal **a** (see 21.1.1.2).

## **9.1 Algo**

**Rule:** **Algo** (invariable) is used as a **pronoun** referring only to things, and as an **adverb**.

- As a **pronoun**: usually 'something', or 'anything' in questions and after *único* and *poco*.
- As an **adverb** before adjectives or another adverb: 'rather', 'somewhat'.
- **Algo de** + uncountable noun: 'some', 'a little'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Están tramando algo sospechoso. | They are plotting something suspicious. |
| ¿Quieres algo? | Do you want anything? |
| Era la única que escribió algo. | She was the only one who wrote anything. |
| La sopa está algo fría. | The soup is rather cold. |
| Están corriendo algo despacio. | They are running rather slowly. |
| Necesita algo de sal. | It needs a little salt. |

## **9.2 Alguien**

**Rule:** **Alguien** (invariable) is a **pronoun referring only to persons**. It means 'someone', or 'anyone' in questions.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Alguien ha dejado la ventana abierta. | Someone has left the window open. |
| ¿Estás esperando a alguien? | Are you waiting for someone? |
| ¿Ha llamado alguien? | Has anyone phoned? |

## **9.3 Alguno**

**Rule:** **Alguno** can be used as an **adjective** or **pronoun** referring to persons, places or things. It varies in number and gender. Shortened to **algún** before singular masculine nouns (see 6.3.1). Cannot be used with uncountable nouns.

- Basic meaning: 'some', 'a few'; in questions: 'any'. As a pronoun: 'one/some of them'.
- Placed **after** a noun in a negative sentence: means 'no (at all)' — equivalent to **ninguno**.
- **Alguno/algún que otro/alguna que otra** (singular): 'a few', 'the occasional'.
- In speech **alguna** is sometimes shortened before a feminine noun beginning with stressed **a/ha**: *algún arma*.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| En algunas aldeas no hay tiendas. | In some villages there are no shops. |
| Conozco a algunos (de ellos). | I know some (of them). |
| Vamos a comprar pan. | We are going to buy some bread. (not *algún pan*) |
| ¿Quieres leche? | Do you want any milk? (not *alguna leche*) |
| No hay motivo alguno. | There is no reason at all. |
| en modo alguno | not in the slightest |
| Alguna que otra cigüeña anida allí. | An occasional stork nests there. |

## **9.4 Uno**

**Rule:** In the plural, **uno** means 'some', 'a few', often followed by **cuantos/as** or **pocos/as**. Before a cardinal numeral, **unos/unas** indicates approximation. In the singular, **uno/a** expresses the indefinite 'one/you' (sometimes with implied 1st person plural reference).

**uno** and **alguno** are sometimes interchangeable but **never** before **de**. In the singular, **uno/a + de** is the norm; in the plural, use **algunos/as + de**.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Comimos unas enchiladas riquísimas. | We ate some delicious enchiladas. |
| Había unos cuantos libros en la estantería. | There were a number of books on the shelf. |
| Tiene unos veinte años. | He is about twenty years old. |
| Salí con uno de mis amigos. | I went out with one of my friends. |
| Salí con algunos de mis amigos. | I went out with some of my friends. |
| A veces una se preocupa, dijo Teresa. | 'One sometimes worries,' said Teresa. |
| Uno intenta ayudarles, pero no nos lo agradecen. | One tries to help them, but they do not thank us for it. |

## **9.5 Mucho, poco**

### **9.5.1 Used as adjectives and pronouns**

**Rule:** **Mucho/a/os/as** means 'much', 'many', 'great', 'a lot (of)'. **Poco/a/os/as** means 'few', 'little'. Both vary in number and gender. Both can also be used as **invariable neuter pronouns**. Not infrequently they convey the sense of 'too much' and 'too little'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo pocos libros. | I have few books. |
| Habla con mucha/poca emoción. | He speaks with great/little feeling. |
| Tengo mucho/poco que hacer. | I have a lot/little to do. |
| Esto es mucha/poca comida para él. | This is too much/too little food for him. |

### **9.5.2 Used as adverbs**

**Rule:** As an adverb, **mucho** means 'much', 'a lot'. **Poco** means 'little' and can also **negate an adjective** (sometimes corresponding to the English prefix 'un-'). Preceded by the indefinite article, **poco** means 'a little'.

*Notes:* **una poca de** (with feminine nouns) is best avoided. The forms **poquísimo** and **muchísimo** strengthen the word; **muy poco** also intensifies.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Lee mucho. | She reads a lot. |
| Esta tierra es poco fértil. | This land is not fertile. |
| poco interesante/probable | uninteresting/unlikely |
| un poco raro | a bit strange |
| ¿Quieres un poco de pan? | Do you want a little bread? |
| Se ven muy pocos Ferraris aquí. | You see very few Ferraris here. |

## **9.6 Bastante, suficiente**

**Rule:** As an adjective or pronoun each is **invariable for gender** but has a regular plural. Both can also be used as invariable neuter pronouns. **Bastante** is also an invariable adverb; the corresponding adverb for *suficiente* is **suficientemente**.

In speech, **bastante** commonly indicates moderately large quantity or extent: 'quite a lot', 'quite big'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Crees que hay bastantes sillas? | Do you think there are enough chairs? |
| No hay suficientes. | There aren't enough. |
| Esto es suficiente/bastante para mí. | This is enough for me. |
| Los ganchos no son bastante grandes. | The hooks are not big enough. |
| No pensaron suficientemente en el resultado. | They did not think enough about the result. |
| Tiene bastante dinero. | He has quite a lot of money. |
| Su casa es bastante grande. | Their house is quite big. |

## **9.7 Varios**

**Rule:** **Varios/as** (variable for gender, plural only) is used as an indefinite adjective and pronoun meaning 'several'. When placed **after** a noun, **vario** (usually plural) can mean 'varied', 'different'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Varios invitados no comieron nada. | Several guests did not eat anything. |
| Las desventajas son varias. | There are several disadvantages. |
| carpetas de colores varios | folders of different colours |

## **9.8 Demasiado**

**Rule:** **Demasiado** (variable for gender and number) as an adjective or pronoun means 'too much', 'too many'. As an invariable **adverb** it means 'too (much)'. Also used as an invariable neuter pronoun.

*Note: In Latin America, **demasiado** is sometimes used to mean 'very' rather than 'too much' — see 30.6.3.5.*

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tiene demasiado poder. | She wields too much power. |
| Había demasiada gente en la fiesta. | There were too many people at the party. |
| Antes no tenía trabajo, ahora tengo demasiado. | Before I had no work; now I've got too much. |
| Los colores son demasiado fuertes. | The colours are too bright. |
| Suelen exigir demasiado. | They usually demand too much. |

### **9.9.1 Todo as an adjective**

#### 9.9.1.1 Basic use — 'all (of)', 'the whole'

**Rule:** **Todo** before a noun preceded by the definite article, a possessive or demonstrative: 'all (of)', 'the whole'. Also before a pronoun or place name. **Todo** can be moved to other positions in the sentence, still agreeing with the noun. **Todos/as** + definite article + time noun = 'every'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| todo el edificio | the whole building |
| todos nuestros colegas | all (of) our colleagues |
| todos ellos/ustedes | all of them/you |
| todo París | the whole of Paris |
| Los alumnos son todos muy simpáticos. | The students are all very nice. |
| La habitación estaba toda pintada de azul. | The whole room was painted blue. |
| todos los meses | every month |
| todas las semanas | every week |

#### 9.9.1.3 Todo + relative pronoun

**Rule:** **Todos los que / todas las que** ('all those / everyone who') and neuter **todo lo que** ('all / everything that') are common relative constructions. **Los/las/lo** cannot be omitted. *Todos los que* is occasionally replaced by **todo el que** with a singular verb.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Entrevistamos a todos los que asistieron. | We interviewed all those who attended. |
| Nos mostraron todo lo que habían encontrado. | They showed us everything that they had found. |
| Todo el que pasó la saludó. | Everyone who passed waved to her. |

#### 9.9.1.4 Todo + indefinite article — emphatic, not literal

**Rule:** **Todo + un/una** tends to be emphatic rather than literal. It usually means 'a real' rather than 'a whole'. To express 'a whole/an entire', use **entero** instead.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Habéis tenido toda una aventura. | You've had a real adventure. |
| un día entero | a whole day (use *entero*, not *todo un día*) |

| a todas horas | at all hours |
| por todas partes | everywhere |

### **9.9.2 Todo as a pronoun**

#### 9.9.2.1 Todos/Todas

**Rule:** **Todos/as** means 'all' or, for persons in general, 'everyone'. When used as the direct object of a verb, the object pronouns **los** or **las** are usually added. **Todo el mundo** is equivalent to and more common than **todos** for 'everyone'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| desde el punto de vista de todos | from everyone's point of view |
| En cuanto a sus primos, los conozco a todos. | As for his cousins, I know all of them. |
| Eso se lo dice a todas. | That's what he says to all women. |

| Lo he comprado todo. | I've bought everything / it all. |

## **9.10 Cualquiera**

**Rule:** **Cualquiera** translates English 'any' except in negative sentences (use **ninguno** instead — see 24.2.4) and some questions (where **alguno** may be more appropriate). Invariable for gender. Plural **cualesquiera** is rare.

- As an indefinite **adjective** before a noun: **always** shortened to **cualquier**.
- As a **pronoun**: never shortened; remains **cualquiera**.
- Used **after** a noun: means 'any (at all)', 'any (old)': *un libro cualquiera* 'any old book'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Cualquier libro sirve. | Any book will do. |
| Se puede comprar en cualquier tienda. | It can be bought in any shop. |
| El se lleva bien con cualquiera. | He gets on with anyone. |
| un libro cualquiera | any old book |

## **9.11 Ambos/as**

**Rule:** **Ambos/as** (always plural, variable for gender) is used especially in written language as an adjective or pronoun referring to two persons, places or things — meaning 'both'. In speech, usually replaced by **los/las dos**.

**Misuse warning:** Do not use **ambos** to translate 'both' in sequences like 'both Carlos and María' — this type of 'both' is for emphasis only and cannot be translated literally.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Se discutieron ambas ofertas. | Both offers were discussed. |
| — ¿Con cuál de las hermanas hablaste? — Hablé con las dos. | 'Which sister did you talk to?' 'I talked to both of them.' |
| Hablé con Carlos y con María. | I spoke to both Carlos and María. |

## **9.12 Cada**

**Rule:** **Cada** (invariable) is used only as an adjective. Means 'each' or 'every'. **Cada vez más/menos** = 'more and more / less and less'. **Cada uno/a** (also **cada cual**) = 'each (one)'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Cada número gana un premio. | Each/every number wins a prize. |
| cada cinco años | every five years |
| Es cada vez menos tolerante. | He is less and less tolerant. |
| Cada uno llevaba un banderín. | Each one carried a pennant. |

## **9.13 Solo**

**Rule:** As an **adjective** (variable for number and gender), **solo** means 'alone' and sometimes 'mere'. As an **adverb** (invariable, equivalent to *solamente* 'only'), it means 'only'. The written accent on **sólo** is now only considered necessary when there is danger of confusion with the adjective **solo** 'alone'.

**Solo** cannot translate English 'only' in the sense of 'one and only' — use **único** instead.

*Notes:* **Tan solo** is used for emphasis before the adverbial form. **A solas** = 'alone' (in privacy).

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Andrea y Adela fueron solas a la fiesta. | Andrea and Adela went alone to the party. |
| La sola mención del asunto provocó una pelea. | The mere mention of the subject provoked a quarrel. |
| La única tienda que lo vende es Zara. | The only shop that sells it is Zara. (*único*, not *solo*) |
| Solo me quedaban dos. | I only had two left. |
| Quedaban tan solo cinco ejemplares. | There were only five copies left. |
| ¡Vamos a celebrarlo a solas! | Let's celebrate alone! |

## **9.14 Demás**

**Rule:** Preceded by the definite article and meaning 'the rest', 'the other(s)', **demás** is invariable both as adjective and pronoun. The invariable neuter **lo demás** means 'everything else'. Without the definite article, **y demás...** closes an enumeration.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Las demás (joyas) no valen nada. | The rest (of the jewels) are not worth anything. |
| Los niños están a salvo, lo demás no me importa. | The children are safe, the rest does not matter. |
| franceses, españoles y demás naciones extranjeras | French, Spanish and other foreign nations |

## **9.15 Cierto**

**Rule:** **Cierto** (variable for number and gender) is normally an adjective meaning '(a) certain'. The indefinite article before **cierto** is now quite common. Placed **after** the noun, **cierto** means 'certain', 'reliable', 'definite'.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ciertas plantas son venenosas. | Certain plants are poisonous. |
| Ahora se nota (una) cierta fatiga en su comportamiento. | Now one notes a certain weariness in her behaviour. |
| No hay noticias ciertas de su paradero actual. | There is no reliable news of his present whereabouts. |

## **9.16 Tal, semejante**

**Rule:** Both vary in number only. **Tal** 'such (a/an)' can only come **before** a noun. **Semejante** can come before or after. Neither is normally followed by the indefinite article (unlike English).

- **Semejante** before noun: emphatic 'such', especially in negative phrases.
- **Semejante** after noun: 'similar', 'alike'.
- **Un/a tal** + name: 'a certain (person)'.
- **El/la tal**: refers to who or what is under discussion, sometimes with humorous/mocking tone.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| en tal caso | in such a case |
| Nunca vi semejante chapucería. | I've never seen such poor workmanship. |
| Compraron dos sillones semejantes. | They purchased two similar armchairs. |
| Una tal Magdalena te buscaba. | A certain Magdalena was looking for you. |
| El tal Martínez es un verdadero granuja. | That Martínez is a real rascal. |

## **9.17 Otro**

**Rule:** **Otro** (variable for gender and number) is used as an adjective meaning '(an)other' and as a pronoun meaning 'another one', 'others'.

**Key rules:**
1. Unlike English 'another', **otro** is **never** used with the indefinite article: *otra mesa* (not ~~una otra mesa~~).
2. **Otro** is placed **before** cardinal numbers: *otros cuatro*, *otras cien libras*. But **after** other indefinites: *alguna otra razón*.

**Examples:**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La otra máquina nunca funciona. | The other machine never works. |
| ¿No tienes otra cosa? | Don't you have anything else? |
| ¿Dónde están los otros? | Where are the others? |
| otra mesa | another table (never *una otra mesa*) |
| otros cuatro | four others |
| otras cien libras | another hundred pounds |
| alguna otra razón | some other reason |
